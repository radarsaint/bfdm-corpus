# Phase 2 — Decision Contrast Families v1

**Status:** active derived research.  
**Branch:** `research/phase2-decision-trajectories`  
**Purpose:** begin Stage 2 of the BFDM program by reconstructing expert judgment through contrast, failure, restraint, correction, and changed decisions.

This is **not** a precedent-card dataset and not a Kit runtime artifact. It is a research layer built from source-traceable decision cases. The immediate question is not “what principle does Brendon follow?” but:

> **What condition made one intervention appropriate here and a superficially similar intervention inappropriate there?**

The current substrate is unusually strong: Roanoke S3 has 20 bounded local decision cases plus 10 longitudinal cases; Empire City / S4 has 8 bounded local cases plus 17 longitudinal cases. S3 and S4 share a large persistent multi-DM format, so findings remain bounded until materially different campaign environments test them.

## Selection discipline

A useful Phase 2 contrast should contain at least one of the following:

- same apparent request, opposite DM intervention;
- same intervention, different reason or scope;
- success paired with failure or correction;
- intervention paired with restraint;
- local repair paired with structural rewrite;
- player-created authority paired with authority the DM refuses to grant;
- prepared content preserved in one case and discarded/deferred in another;
- a later decision that narrows, corrects, or complicates an earlier generalization.

Derived case files are navigation. Every family below retains underlying BCS anchors or Discord message IDs so the comparison can be reconstructed against source evidence.

# CF-01 — Handwave the procedure vs preserve the problem

## Surface similarity

A player asks whether mechanics can be skipped because the desired direction is already apparent.

## Case A — handwave

**BDC-S3-004** — A player deliberately chose a dramatic limb loss. Another player asked whether healing mechanics still needed to be played out.

Underlying evidence:
- `BCS-000045:L1573-L1579`
- `BCS-000045:L4420-L4422`
- Discord `736304203265212444`

Brendon was willing to handwave because the central dramatic outcome had already been consensually chosen and achieved.

## Case B — do not handwave

**BDC-S3-005 / BDC-S3-L03** — A Golden Dawn character wanted painful memories removed and the table asked whether the required magic could simply be handwaved.

Underlying evidence:
- `BCS-000045:L1644-L1654`
- `BCS-000045:L1688-L1693`
- Discord `736855826341560371`
- Discord `736856303082668123`
- later persistence: `736859296457031723`, `737368587714428939`, `738136058386055208`, `745842568184463402`

Brendon refused because the question had become a character arc. He granted a narrower memory change while preserving allegiance, future choice, and a recurring consequence.

## Critical distinction

The deciding question is not “can this be resolved without dice?”

It is:

> **Does uncertainty/procedure still carry story-bearing work?**

When all meaningful participants have already chosen the core outcome, extra procedure can become noise. When the proposed shortcut would erase a choice-bearing problem with future consequences, the procedure or fictional constraint remains valuable.

## Tempting bad rule

> “Brendon prefers handwaving when the players agree.”

Too broad. Agreement is insufficient when the thing being skipped is the source of future character definition.

## Phase 2 hypothesis

**Mechanics are expendable after their uncertainty/function is exhausted; they are protected when they still hold unresolved consequence.**

**Confidence:** high within S3.  
**Next test:** find a non-Roanoke example where Brendon either skips or preserves procedure for the same reason.

Underlying evidence includes:

- `865565818481279018`
- `865565932783796255`
- `865566464831258674`
- `865567436970786836`

## Cases where Brendon decreases his own intervention

**BDC-S3-011 — dating-sim ownership.** Brendon catches himself offering so much accommodation that he is beginning to author the players’ emergent activity for them.

Underlying evidence:
- `736688057113378928`

**BDC-S3-013 — meta-solving a player decision.** Brendon recognizes that DM knowledge is beginning to choose a player’s story and hands the choice back.

Underlying evidence:
- `737569808396058654`

**BDC-S3-L01 — Victor after promotion.** Once Victor becomes a genuine player moral conflict, Brendon explicitly says he is “taking my hands off the wheel” and gives the players control over the outcome.

Underlying evidence:
- `735961901648969789`
- `735962244797563080`
- `735963112158658630`

## Critical distinction

Player investment can justify **more world support** while simultaneously requiring **less DM authorship of the outcome**.

The intervention changes phase:

1. notice repeated investment;
2. make the object of investment real enough to play with;
3. supply access, consequences, and adjudication;
4. reduce steering once the resulting problem belongs to the players.

## Tempting bad rule

> “When players like something, add more content.”

That would reproduce the exact overreach Brendon catches in BDC-S3-011.

## Phase 2 hypothesis

**Investment increases the DM’s obligation to support the world around a player-created concern, not the DM’s right to author what the players do with it.**

**Confidence:** high across S3/S4 Roanoke lineage.

# CF-03 — Reward creativity vs reject a cool request

## Surface similarity

A player asks for an unusual exception, interpretation, or personally exciting addition.

## Case A — creativity is seriously considered but not approved

**BDC-S3-014** — A visually spectacular oversized-creature rules interaction is attractive, but its precedent and mechanical implications are unclear.

Underlying evidence:
- `BCS-000045:L1576-L1579`
- `746496793772163183`

Brendon likes the image while weighting the answer toward no pending rules research and co-DM input.

## Case B — deny an exception because it removes designed pressure

**BDC-S4-001** — A private apartment would remove intended housing vulnerability and create a non-scalable exception.

Underlying evidence:
- `863492563784040539`

## Case C — preserve the premise but cut ownership of shared narrative capital

**BDC-S4-002** — Revolutionary assassin is accepted; “like a son to Washington” is trimmed.

Underlying evidence:
- `863889244996632587`
- `863890123526242325`

**BDC-S4-003** — Personal links to famous prior-campaign figures are restricted because legacy references belong to the shared campaign rather than one player.

Underlying evidence:
- `864911042601811980`

## Critical distinction

Creative enthusiasm is not sufficient reason to alter:

- established mechanical precedent;
- intended vulnerability;
- scalable fairness;
- collaborator-owned material;
- scarce shared narrative capital.

Brendon often preserves the viable part of the player’s premise while rejecting the part that would damage those constraints.

## Tempting bad rule

> “Reward creativity by saying yes.”

The cases instead support:

> **Reward creativity by taking it seriously enough to test against the world, rules, fairness, and ownership.**

## Phase 2 hypothesis

**Creative-play support is bounded by the cost the exception imposes on future shared play.**

**Confidence:** high within S3/S4.  
**Open question:** whether the same boundary appears in small synchronous campaigns where population-scale fairness is less important.

**BDC-S3-L09** — The Grey Render event slips a day. Brendon still runs it, acknowledges the schedule failure, and then uses its funeral/aftermath as connective tissue for the Victor invitational.

Underlying evidence includes:

> **Are the assumptions required for the intended experience still true?**

# CF-05 — Patch locally vs rewrite architecture

## Surface similarity

Something in the game is producing an outcome Brendon considers wrong.

## Local repair

**BDC-S3-019** — A resurrection house rule proves too strong.

Underlying evidence:
- `744056939096571924`

Response:
- publicly identify the mistake;
- nerf the rule prospectively;
- preserve resurrections already earned under the old rule.

The failure is bounded to a rule.

## Architecture repair

**BDC-S3-017 / BDC-S3-L07** — Four intended faction lanes collapse into a harmful bipartisan winner-take-all structure after two DMs become less active.

Underlying evidence includes:
- `BCS-000045:L1590-L1617`
- `BCS-000045:L1673-L1705`
- `743967418824523836`
- `745053207327277106`
- `745054916850221076`
- `745737396804518020`
- `745753934861303849`

Response:
- rewrite the ending architecture;
- preserve distinct faction contribution without exclusivity;
- remove dependent mechanics that no longer make sense.

**BDC-S4-L01** — Late-game population no longer supports content prepared for a much larger active population.

Underlying evidence includes:
- `877727568147677214`
- `877727967940345866`
- `877728873230520370`
- `878085474726592542`
- `878397426619260968`
- `879447556667633675`

Response:
- move the finale;
- rework the final encounter;
- preserve RP afterward;
- carry the lesson into S5 planning.

## Critical distinction

The intervention scale follows the failure scale.

A broken rule does not require a campaign rewrite. A campaign architecture whose prerequisites have failed cannot be fixed by polishing one scene or asking players to endure the old incentives.

## Tempting bad rule

> “Prefer the smallest intervention.”

Incomplete. Prefer the smallest intervention that reaches the actual causal layer.

## Phase 2 hypothesis

**Mutation scale should match causal scope. Local failures get local corrections; architectural failures justify architectural rewrites and dependent-system audits.**

**Confidence:** very high within Roanoke lineage.

# CF-08 — Preserve intended pressure vs change delivery when pressure becomes illegible

## Surface similarity

Players experience designed vulnerability or danger.

## Pressure preserved

**BDC-S4-001** — Vulnerable housing is intentional. A safer private apartment is denied because it would remove the pressure and create an unfair exception.

Evidence:
- `863492563784040539`

## Delivery corrected

**BDC-S3-015 / BDC-S3-L04** — A dangerous encounter lands as unfair because the deadliness was not adequately signaled.

Evidence includes:
- `740071292127936623`
- `740071469488537671`
- `740071507027427395`
- `740074415441575937`
- later correction: `743248182640902235`

Brendon does not conclude that lethal danger itself was the mistake. He identifies a communication failure, stops further escalation while table trust is damaged, and later telegraphs lethal stakes directly.

## Critical distinction

Difficulty or vulnerability is not removed merely because it creates discomfort.

The relevant question is whether the player had a fair opportunity to understand the stakes and whether the designed pressure is producing the experience it was intended to produce.

## Tempting bad rule

> “If players are upset, reduce the danger.”

The evidence supports a narrower response:
- preserve legitimate pressure;
- repair missing legibility/trust;
- only change the underlying danger if the danger itself is the broken design.

## Phase 2 hypothesis

**Challenge is protected when its purpose remains valid and legible; communication changes when the risk was not reasonably perceivable.**

**Confidence:** high across the paired S3/S4 cases, though the contexts differ.

## Critical distinction

“Be more player-led late in the campaign” is not the rule.

> **What does the accumulated campaign now have plenty of, and what function is still missing?**

> “Late campaigns should reduce prep.”

## Phase 2 hypothesis

**Late-campaign preparation becomes gap-filling against live history, not continuation of the original content quota.**

**Confidence:** high within Roanoke lineage.

# CF-10 — Central authority vs collaborator sovereignty in multi-DM play

## Surface similarity

Brendon, as campaign owner/lead, can resolve an uncertainty directly.

## Case A — withhold approval to protect another DM’s plan

**BDC-S4-006**

Evidence:
- `865364564056735776`

Brendon refuses to sign off on a request that might interfere with another DM’s planned material until that DM weighs in.

## Case B — let delivered shared canon revise private intent

**BDC-S4-007 / BDC-S4-L03**

Evidence:
- `869309519640010852`
- `869310491187617892`
- `869310601002909736`

Brendon’s internal leyline sequence does not automatically override a collaborator’s delivered play. The staff reconcile the two and clarify public state.

## Complicating evidence

End-of-season S4 player feedback asks for better communication between DMs. These good local examples therefore do not prove that the collaboration system worked consistently.

## Critical distinction

Campaign ownership does not imply that every local decision is centrally owned.

The lead DM’s legitimate action can be restraint:
- wait for the collaborator whose plan owns the dependency;
- accept delivered shared play as a fact that private prep must adapt around.

## Phase 2 hypothesis

**In multi-DM play, central authority is constrained by delegated ownership and delivered canon.**

**Confidence:** high in S4; persistence outside S4 unresolved.

# What these contrasts change in the working model

The early S3/S4 synthesis could be summarized too easily as “adapt to players,” “preserve intent,” or “reward investment.” Phase 2 is already forcing narrower decision boundaries.

## 1. “Player investment matters” requires an inhibition rule

Investment can justify new preparation, but it can also require the DM to **back off** once the resulting activity belongs to the players.

The better question is:
> What support does this player-created activity need from the world, and what part should remain authored by the players?

## 2. “Preserve purpose, change form” requires a validity test for the purpose

A slipped event may still be worth running. A boss fight may no longer be. The existence of prep is neither reason to run it nor reason to discard it.

The question is:
> Are the prerequisites for the intended experience still true?

## 3. “Prefer the smallest fix” is incomplete

A local rule failure can be patched prospectively. A structural failure must be repaired at the architecture level. Minimalism is useful only after causal scope is identified.

## 4. “Give players agency” contains at least two different kinds of authority

Brendon resists private pre-ownership of central/shared campaign capital while granting substantial authority that is earned through live responsibility.

The distinction between **claimed authority** and **performed authority** looks highly valuable for future judgment modeling.

## 5. “Let the world live” needs an eligibility boundary

Persistent world simulation is not prior to every other concern. Table contracts such as PvP consent can determine whether an action exists as a legitimate candidate for adjudication.

## 6. “Challenge matters” is not opposed to “fairness”

The same evidence supports both preserving intended vulnerability and correcting danger delivery when the risk was not legible. The conflict is not challenge vs kindness; it is intended pressure vs unintelligible pressure.

# Initial decision-boundary vocabulary

These are research labels, not final ontology.

- **story-bearing uncertainty** — unresolved procedure or consequence still capable of producing meaningful character/world change;
- **world support vs outcome authorship** — the DM can invest more in making a player-created concern playable while steering its resolution less;
- **exception externality** — the cost an individual exception imposes on shared precedent, pressure, fairness, collaborator work, or narrative capital;
- **purpose viability** — whether the assumptions required for prepared material to produce its intended experience are still true;
- **causal scope** — rule, scene, encounter, schedule, subsystem, or campaign architecture level at which the actual failure originates;
- **claimed authority vs performed authority** — privilege asserted before play versus jurisdiction earned through repeated live responsibility;
- **adjudication eligibility** — whether an attempted action is allowed to enter simulation/resolution at all;
- **legibility repair** — change the information/signaling around a valid pressure rather than erasing the pressure;
- **late-campaign functional gap** — a missing function in accumulated play that may call for either less prep or more connective structure;
- **delegated ownership** — a multi-DM dependency that limits unilateral lead-DM intervention.

These labels should remain disposable. Their value will be tested by whether they help distinguish future cases without forcing evidence to fit them.

# Next Phase 2 research targets

Do not spend the next pass extracting more broad S3/S4 principles.

Prioritize evidence capable of breaking these hypotheses:

1. **Earthfall / current practice** — materially smaller and more synchronous. Search for:
   - a prepared encounter altered or abandoned because the live table made its purpose nonviable;
   - player fixation promoted into real content;
   - a player-created idea deliberately *not* promoted;
   - a failure that changed later telegraphing, pacing, encounter design, or session structure;
   - an example of Brendon preserving difficult pressure rather than smoothing it away.

2. **Bastion / Redoubt** — test authority, consequence, and prep mutation outside Roanoke.

3. **At War’s End** — use revision trajectories for design judgment, but do not treat draft revision as live DM evidence unless delivery evidence is located.

4. **Early Roanoke / S2** — test whether the decision boundaries predate S3 rather than emerging from S3’s production pressures.

5. **Negative-space sampling** — deliberately search for:
   - repeated player interest that Brendon declined to expand;
   - prepared material left unchanged despite live attention;
   - a cool idea rejected because it damaged campaign purpose;
   - a situation where the correct action was to let a scene continue without improvement.

6. **Failure pairs** — for every proposed reusable judgment, find a superficially similar case where applying it would have been wrong.

The goal of the next batch is not to confirm these ten families. It is to attack them.
