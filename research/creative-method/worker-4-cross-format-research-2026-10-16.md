## Synthesis preserved

1. Across several formats, the material tries to make a world premise operational: colonial scarcity produces jobs, production, trade, and dependence; real bowling determines D&D conflict; a city-guard institution provides missions, attendance continuity, and controlled requisition; Earthfall's television premise produces achievement and reward machinery.
2. Selective DM attention is directly visible in Empire City. Brendon declines to read every routine social or dating interaction, but does read important wedding-related material and proposes an officiant role.
3. A live Empire City rest-rule intervention connects the mechanic with explicit concern about unhealthy fear of missing out. That is stronger than merely finding the rule in a manual.
4. Prepared dramatic structure and emergent choice coexist, with documented tensions: early Roanoke's missing-colony destiny has partly open local branches; some script drafts assume outcomes; Earthfall workbench instructions prefer an encounter hook over a scripted series of player actions.
5. Performance evidence is heterogeneous. Short live narration, drafted NPC cast cues, director corrections, and showrunner prompt specifications are not interchangeable evidence of spoken DM skill.
6. Documented friction includes a reported Fryvern fight fizzle, Saturday D&D missed-clue uptake and a mistaken assistant assumption, a Roanoke preparation branch left unfinished, scripted Bastion outcomes without a branch, and sparse Season 5 scheduling templates. These are not a causal failure diagnosis. A full play record is absent.
7. At War's End drafts show distinct narration and scene construction. Passage authorship and full chronology are not resolved. This is a version-family comparison, not demonstrated Brendon prose development.
8. No stage of evidence proves the next: archived, studied, formatted, approved, consumed, and improved human play are different.

## W4-20261016-001 — Early Roanoke offline labor and interdependence

**Type.** Creative technique / historical observation. Bounded supplement, not a second economy history.

**Context.** Early Roanoke settlement design, 2018 family.

Daily food demand and a coming seasonal decline create pressure (lines 88–90). Players are shown choosing professions and workplaces (lines 100–122).

**Interpretation.** The design turns an asynchronous online roleplay environment into an interdependent settlement economy.

**Limitations.** The Season 2 redesign is a different document. It is not restated here.

**Kit applicability.** Candidate for private campaign-economy design when a persistent community has independent jobs. Not for ordinary solo combat. Do not impose unasked-for scarcity.
