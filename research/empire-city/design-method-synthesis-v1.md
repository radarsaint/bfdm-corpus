## 1. Source selection and transformation

Retrospective evidence `BCE-000014` describes a method based on small historically or culturally charged details, with *Neverwhere* named as an influence. Explicit source families include historical mysteries, local mythology, secret societies/institutions, and cryptids.

Working S4 model:

> charged fragment -> implied mythology -> literal transformation -> game function -> thematic pressure -> player consequence

S4 examples include the Quartering Act, Pinkertons, Wall Street, Franklin, Hamilton/political print culture, Benedict Arnold, Coney Island, Masons, and regional cryptids.

A recurring move is to preserve one recognizable hook while changing its game function: Franklin retains the electricity/kite association; Pinkertons retain investigation/intelligence; Arnold retains betrayal; Wall Street retains speculation; cryptids retain their folklore identity.

## 2. Campaign architecture

The contemporaneous `Game design workflow` document defines:
- main story/finale;
- city map;
- borough set/cast design;
- DM assignments;
- rotating borough schedule;
- six-hour daily blocks;
- evenings for main events;
- side play outside main events;
- recurring multi-day borough stories.

S4 therefore operates as:

> citywide spine + semi-independent local play machines

The boroughs support different forms of play:
- The City: finance, civic systems, urban sandbox, famous NPCs.
- Ferrytown: bargains, repeatable games, smuggling, transformation.
- Hampstead: occupation, scarcity, curfew, contraband, horror factions.
- Kingsbridge Noir: investigation, clue chains, memory/identity pressure.
- Staaten Wastes: vehicles, sabotage, action, smuggling, industrial conflict.

## 3. Historical pressure becomes player affordances

Hampstead prep starts from the Quartering Act and connects occupation to housing/resource pressure, curfew, silver restrictions, and abusive idle troops.

S4's “Tax” becomes changing rules affecting magic, healing, equipment, language, resources, and movement.

Message `867572467006570506` explicitly distinguishes player enjoyment from character experience: players may find the taxes fun while the characters are being harassed by occupying forces.

Candidate principle:

> Important historical pressure should alter what players can do, not remain exposition.

## 4. Promotion rule: what earns more authored game

Message `869331287809335367` says DMs may riff on “the things that catch our attention.” It defines Invitationals as improvised threads requested by players or DMs, fulfilled as time and DM interest permit.

Observed promotion signals:
- player-created rebuilding receives explicit support (`865574816647872523`);
- visible stock-market enthusiasm is noticed and reinforced (`869126078491406366`, `869126164055216138`);
- the Arnold clue chain becomes a player-driven mission later scheduled by Brendon (`866861745627987988`, `879860620575518790`);
- player interest later pulls the Kellogg thread back into staff planning.

Working model:

> promising live thread + DM attention/interest + available labor + compatible campaign state -> more authored game

## 5. Priority hierarchy

S4 evidence shows Brendon protecting several things.

**Participation.**
`864049384831844362`: prolonged inability to play is described as boring.
`864130501451055114`: concern that the player will disengage.

**Designed stakes and scalable fairness.**
`863492563784040539`: private housing is denied because homes are intended to remain vulnerable and one exception would not scale fairly.

**Shared-world availability.**
`863890123526242325`: a revolutionary-assassin premise is preserved while an unusually privileged relationship to Washington is trimmed because Washington is a central shared figure.

**Legacy fairness.**
`864911042601811980`: prior-season references are treated as common campaign material rather than private player ownership.

**Collaborator ownership.**
`865364564056735776`: approval is withheld when it could interfere with another DM's active plan.

**Player-specific fun balanced against the event/world.**
`865742343822376970`, `865742559330566154`: every player should have a satisfying arc and voice, while individual fun should not come at the expense of the event/shared world.

## 6. Prep is conditional

Prepared material is repeatedly subordinated to the live state.

- `866777909787623465`: a prepared airship set piece is deferred when player resources and DM readiness no longer support it.
- `869309519640010852`, `869310491187617892`, `869310601002909736`: private sequencing is reconciled with already-delivered collaborator play instead of overriding live canon.
- `878085474726592542`, `878397426619260968`: the finale is reworked and moved when attendance no longer matches the population assumed by late-game content.

Candidate rule:

> Prep is an intention until the live world, player population, collaborator state, and delivery conditions still support it.

## 7. Telegraphing

Empire City avoids relying on a single clue path.

Mechanisms include:
- scheduled routing toward featured boroughs;
- multi-day breadcrumb chains;
- the Empire City Post;
- announcements, radio, notice boards, and town criers;
- changed environmental state;
- recurring information institutions such as Pinkertons, Lamplighters, libraries, and Franklin.

The Arnold thread is a clean case: a betrayal clue points toward a problem; players investigate and then create their own response.

Candidate model:

> telegraph the problem redundantly; leave the solution open.

## 8. Consequence persistence and recovery

Consequences usually remain real until changed through play.

Examples:
- damaged locations become unavailable;
- Ferrytown booths remain damaged until players rebuild them;
- institutional destruction changes where information work lives;
- character transformations require further play to reverse;
- player roles/status can become persistent world institutions;
- political choices are framed as shaping future campaign history.

Candidate distinction:

> reset erases consequence; recovery acknowledges consequence and generates more play.

## 9. Theme through mechanics

Message `867604855710285846` directly identifies identity as S4's theme.

Messages `865186821951324222`, `865187551291506689`, and `865189260877758485` connect body-change/resurrection mechanics to story continuity, social bonds, and the question of what a character will trade away to continue.

Public message `865906613955854346` asks players to consider identity after physical transformation and how their friends respond.

Candidate principle:

> A theme becomes playable when it creates choices between things the player values.

## 10. Humor as machinery

Server rule `850796618051878932` explicitly values laughter while protecting players who do not want to be the target of a joke.

Message `866737179837333534` makes the boundary direct: a fun trope can be abandoned if the player dislikes it.

Humor frequently provides:
- memorable mechanical hooks;
- recognizable historical/folkloric handles;
- NPC characterization;
- reasons to continue emergent riffs.

Candidate rule:

> The joke is useful when it makes play more legible or generative; it is expendable when it stops being fun for the participant carrying it.

## 11. Failure becomes future design

The attendance collapse provides a full learning chain:
- content was built for a larger late-game population;
- the current finale was changed;
- the server remained open for RP;
- next-season planning immediately changed.

Message `879447556667633675` records the future adjustment: advertise and shorten the next season.

Pattern:

> prior experience -> design choice -> live failure -> diagnosis -> current correction -> next iteration

## 12. Provisional S4 model

1. Select material with existing cultural charge.
2. Literalize its implication.
3. Give it multiple game functions.
4. Use geography to separate play modes.
5. Turn theme into costly choices.
6. Telegraph important pressures through redundant channels.
7. Watch what players actually engage with.
8. Promote threads that generate interest and future play.
9. Protect participation, shared-world integrity, stakes, fairness, and collaborator work.
10. Sacrifice prep when the live game no longer supports it.
11. Let consequences persist long enough for recovery to become play.
12. Convert failure into the next design iteration.

## 13. Open cross-season hypotheses

- **Semantic density:** source fragments that support several game functions are more likely to survive and expand.
- **Playable implication:** lore becomes valuable when its implied mythology changes player behavior.
- **Attention promotion:** unexpected player investment is a major generator of new authored content.
- **World-as-participant:** player agency is balanced against the shared world's coherence and other participants.
- **Recovery over reset:** damage is most useful when it creates new actions.
- **Theme-through-cost:** abstract themes are expressed through exchanges between valued things.
- **Recurring source families, changing function:** cryptids and other favored material can recur while serving different campaign architectures.

Test against S1/S2, S3, S5, Bastion/Redoubt, At War's End, Earthfall, and non-Roanoke experiments.
