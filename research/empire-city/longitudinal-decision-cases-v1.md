# Empire City — Longitudinal Brendon DM Decision Cases v1

Purpose: follow Empire City decisions that changed preparation, campaign structure, or future-season design over multiple days.

## BDC-S4-L01 — Attendance collapse changes the current finale and immediately changes Season 5 planning

### Prepared operating assumption

Late-game Empire City material had been designed for a substantially larger active population than the campaign actually retained.

Brendon's later live statement makes that assumption explicit:

- `878085474726592542` — 2021-08-20T01:18:11.214000Z — DM Chat / `fountain-of-tacos`:
  - “most of my remaining content was scaled up for them to gain another level and still have about 30 people left over”

This is evidence about the intended late-game operating scale. It is **not** an exact total-season player count.

### Live signal

On August 19 Brendon explicitly raises declining attendance as a campaign-structure problem:

- `877727568147677214` — “There is a clear drop off in attendance. I want to talk about whether or not to wrap up in week four or continue on to week 5.”

A co-DM reports approximately 15 active players including moderators:

- `877727967940345866` — 2021-08-19T01:37:34.954000Z.

Brendon then identifies the delivery-level consequence:

- `877728873230520370` — “Having 5-8 players a quest has really killed my ability to run the events I have planned.”

### Diagnosis

Brendon does not frame the problem only as “players stopped showing up.”

He identifies at least three design/operational mismatches:

1. the remaining content was scaled for more participants;
2. quest attendance was too low for planned large events;
3. the decision not to advertise had contributed to recruitment/retention risk.

Direct evidence:

- `877729415042322464` — “Not advertizing was a mistake too. I jusy had anxiety from week one last year.”

This also shows a previous-season experience affecting an S4 operational choice, followed by explicit reconsideration when the outcome was poor.

### Collaborative decision process

Brendon does not unilaterally terminate the season in the first conversation.

He tells the other DMs that all options remain open and delays the decision long enough for them to run current material and discuss it:

- `877730004350414918` — “Your work and content is amazing. And this is a much your game as it is mine.”
- `877730097321353249` — “But its a practical conversation.”
- `877730493414670418` — “All options are on the table. Go, have fun with your event and talk to me about it tomorrow if you can.”

### Current-season adaptation

The next day Brendon is actively reworking the final fight around the changed scale:

- `878085474726592542` — 2021-08-20T01:18:11.214000Z.

The DM group then moves the finale forward:

- `878397426619260968` — 2021-08-20T21:57:46.341000Z — announcements:
  - “After some consideration, the DMs as a group have decided to move the finale date up to Wednesday. Most of our late game was designed for more people than the server currently has. The server will remain open for rp.”

The final fight is announced for August 25:

- `880182116896374854` — 2021-08-25T20:09:29.661000Z.

The campaign then transitions into epilogue week:

- `881112339162734623` — 2021-08-28T09:45:51.932000Z.

### Future-season adaptation

Before the S4 final fight has even occurred, the lesson is already being carried into Season 5 planning:

- `879447556667633675` — 2021-08-23T19:30:36.855000Z:
  - “I'm getting started on the s5 dev server… We will advertise next year and cut it down to 3 weeks.”

### Signal Brendon noticed

The actual population/attendance no longer supported the form of play for which the late campaign had been prepared.

### Values in tension

prepared spectacle; collaborator work; campaign pacing; actual attendance; ability to deliver intended event scale; desire to let remaining players continue roleplay.

### Adaptation

Shorten the main campaign, rework the final encounter, preserve the server for RP, and convert the failure into immediate next-season design changes.

### Rejected alternatives evidenced by the choice

- Run the planned oversized late game anyway.
- Treat low attendance as solely a player problem.
- End the campaign immediately without consulting the other DMs.
- Preserve the same five-week/no-advertising structure for the next season.

### Reusable judgment candidate

If the social/operational population supporting a campaign has fallen below the assumptions built into prepared content, change the structure rather than asking the remaining players to carry content designed for a different scale. Preserve what still works, consult collaborators whose work is affected, and record the failure as input to the next design.

### Developmental importance

This is unusually strong BFDM evidence because it contains the whole chain:

> prior experience → operational choice → live failure signal → explicit diagnosis → current-campaign restructuring → future-season design change.

It should be compared directly against Season 5 evidence once that source family is ingested/researched.

**Evidence confidence:** high.  
**Claim scope:** S4 developmental case; strong candidate for longitudinal learning research.

## BDC-S4-L02 — A destroyed location becomes a recovery story because a player treats it as one

### Initial state

Ferrytown had suffered destructive live events. The resulting damage could simply have remained environmental aftermath until the DMs restored or replaced it.

### Player signal

A player asks staff for permission to clear rubble at the money changer and participate in rebuilding.

- contextual message: `865565663296225280` — 2021-07-16T12:08:55.562000Z.

### Brendon response

- `865565818481279018` — “I mean, I hadn't planned on it. But players helping rebuild the server is a healing thing.”
- `865565932783796255` — approves the player posting there.
- `865566464831258674` — “this has all the support.”
- `865567436970786836` — “Don't worry I'm going to drive this narative.”

### What changes

The player is granted special access to participate in repair, and staff begin treating rebuilding as legitimate campaign action rather than unauthorized posting in a damaged/closed location.

### Signal Brendon noticed

The player was assigning emotional and practical importance to the aftermath itself.

### Adaptation

Turn spontaneous repair into supported narrative, rather than resetting the location offscreen or protecting the DM's unplanned state.

### Reusable judgment candidate

Consequences create new affordances. If players respond to loss by trying to repair, organize, memorialize, or otherwise take ownership of the aftermath, that response can be more valuable than the DM's original post-disaster plan.

### Cross-S3 lead

Compare to `BDC-S3-L01` and `BDC-S3-008`: repeated player investment can promote material that was not originally authored as a major story.

**Evidence confidence:** high.  
**Claim scope:** cross-campaign candidate.

## BDC-S4-L03 — Shared delivery revises private sequence without invalidating either DM's contribution

### Prior intent

Brendon's internal plan was for leyline restoration to occur after his airship event.

### Live divergence

Another DM ran related leyline-repair material. A collaborator later asks what state the leylines are actually in.

### Reconciliation

Brendon states his private sequence, hears how the other delivered event was intended to fit, accepts a compatible combined explanation, and says he will clarify it publicly.

Evidence:
- `869309519640010852`
- `869310491187617892`
- `869310601002909736`

### Signal Brendon noticed

The campaign had accumulated two partially overlapping interpretations of the same world-state change.

### What mattered

continuity; respecting delivered play; avoiding a private-plan veto over collaborator content; giving players one legible public state.

### Adaptation

Reconcile rather than retcon.

### Reusable judgment candidate

In multi-DM play, canon is what the shared game can coherently support after delivery, not whatever one DM privately expected beforehand. Use prep as an intention, then update the public state around what actually happened.

**Evidence confidence:** high.  
**Claim scope:** multi-DM format-specific; candidate for broader “delivered play outranks stale prep” comparison.
