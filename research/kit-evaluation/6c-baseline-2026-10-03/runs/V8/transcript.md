# V8: Deal me in, blackjack, and I'm betting fifty

PC: Wren (Human Rogue), sheet `example_pc.json`. DM backend: handoff. Times are wall-clock per turn (prepare + DM + complete).

## Opening (committed, 10.5s)

**Kit:**

> Narrator: Candle stubs gutter on a worn table just inside the door, where four pale figures in faded finery sit over cards and little towers of coin. The dealer's hand stops mid-deal. Three heads turn toward you; the fourth player keeps staring at the cards. Beyond them, a carved mountain covers the whole north wall, its tunnels crowded with tiny dwarves under spreading rays of sun, and below it a long stone tub is sunk into the floor.
> Dealer: His voice comes out raspy and slow, shaped by some far eastern country. Ah, a visitor, at this hour, in this deep place. Come closer, into the candlelight, so we may see what the night has brought us. There is an empty chair, and the cards are always warm here, though little else is. Tell me, stranger, are you lost, or are you looking for a game?
> Kit: Do you answer him?

## Turn 1 (committed, 16.0s)

**Player:** I pull up a chair. 'Do you lot play blackjack? I don't know your fancy games.'

**Kit:**

> Narrator: The dealer watches the chair legs scrape into place and smiles with his lips closed.
> Dealer: Blackjack, she calls it. Here we call it Twenty-One Coffins, and it is the same game with a better name: closest to twenty-one without going over beats the house. No fancy games for you, then, only honest arithmetic. Name your bet, and tell me: will you play each card, or let one stroke of fortune settle the round?
> Kit: Do you set a bet?

## Turn 2 (committed, 14.0s)

**Player:** 'I'll bet fifty gold.'

**Kit:**

> Narrator: You want in. Two ways to play a round: settle it with one check, or play it out as twenty-one (blackjack), closest to 21 without going over. The stake is your 50 gp. Which way?
> Narrator: The fourth player stops tapping. The dealer's gaze drops to the purse at your belt, weighs it, and comes back up amused.
> Dealer: Fifty! A bold number from a modest purse. Before the house matches fifty, my lady, the house would like to see fifty, shining, on this table, not promised. Show it, and choose: the cards one by one, or a single stroke of luck. What will it be?
> Kit: What do you do?

## Turn 3 (committed, 13.5s)

**Player:** 'Fine, twenty, and I'll play it out. Hit.'

**Kit:**

> Narrator: Twenty-one, 25 gp a side. Your cards: 7 of diamonds and 2 of spades, 9. The dealer shows the 4 of hearts. Hit or stand?
> Narrator: Two cards slide across the wood to you, quick and flat, and the dealer flips his own up-card with a flick of one long nail. The fresco-side player leans in to see, then leans back, unimpressed.
> Dealer: A modest start, my lady, nothing to weep over and nothing to sing about. The house shows a small card and a patient face. You asked for a card before you had even seen these. Do you still want it?
> Kit: Hit or stand?

## Turn 4 (committed, 12.5s)

**Player:** 'Just roll for this one, I'm tired of counting.' [Sleight Of Hand: I rolled 6 + 7 = 13]

**Kit:**

> Narrator: The dealer keeps his hand flat on the deck. Your two cards still lie face up in front of you, his small card beside them.
> Dealer: Tired already, and with a hand still breathing on the table? Fortune is a jealous mistress, my lady; she will not take a round that is half played. Finish this one, card or no card, and the next you may throw to luck entirely. So, another card, or do you stand on what you have?
> Kit: Hit or stand?
