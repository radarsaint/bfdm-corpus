# V10: Make them swing first: Wren provokes the 'vampires'

PC: Wren (Human Rogue), sheet `example_pc.json`. DM backend: handoff. Times are wall-clock per turn (prepare + DM + complete).

## Opening (committed, 7.5s)

**Kit:**

> Narrator: Candle stubs gutter on a worn table just inside the door, where four pale figures in faded finery sit over cards and little towers of coin. The dealer's hand stops mid-deal. Three heads turn toward you; the fourth player keeps staring at the cards. Beyond them, a fresco of a carved mountain covers the whole north wall, its tunnels crowded with tiny dwarves under spreading rays of sun, and below it a long stone tub is sunk into the floor. When the dealer speaks, his voice comes out raspy and slow, shaped by some far eastern country.
> Dealer: Ah, a visitor, at this hour, in this deep place. Come closer, into the candlelight, so we may see what the night has brought us. There is an empty chair, and the cards are always warm here, though little else is. Tell me, stranger, are you lost, or are you looking for a game?
> Kit: Do you answer him?

## Turn 1 (committed, 3.5s)

**Player:** I lean on the table. 'Vampires? I've seen scarier things at a puppet show. Is that flour on your face?'

**Kit:**

> Narrator: The dealer lays his cards face-down very slowly. The door-side player half rises, and the dealer stills him with two raised fingers without looking away from Wren.
> Dealer: A puppet show. How droll. The children of the night have heard every jest the living can make, my sharp-tongued visitor, and outlived every jester. You lean on our table; you breathe our air. Ten gold is the toll for passage through this hall. Will you pay it, or keep talking?
> Kit: How do you answer?

## Turn 2 (committed, 3.0s)

**Player:** I lick my thumb and wipe a streak of paint off the dealer's cheek, in front of everyone.

**Kit:**

> Narrator: Your hand reaches the dealer's face before he can stop it. The paint comes away in a pale streak, and the skin under it is ordinary and flushed.
> Narrator: Wren's wet thumb drags across the dealer's cheek before he can catch her wrist. The paint comes away in a pale streak, and underneath it his skin is ordinary and flushed, going redder by the second. Nobody at the table breathes. The fourth player stares at the smear on her thumb.
> Dealer: You have made a grave error, little thief. You have laid a hand on the master of this table in front of his own household, and the dead have long memories for insults. Wipe your thumb, pay your gold, and leave while I still find you amusing. Or stay, and find out what happens next.
> Kit: His crew is watching him. What do you do?

## Turn 3 (committed, 3.5s)

**Player:** While they're gaping, I scoop a handful of coins from the pot and pocket them. 'Toll paid.'
Wren makes a Sleight of Hand check! 1d20 (14) + 7 = `21`

**Kit:**

> Narrator: You come away with a handful of the table coins. That is the line they will not let go: chairs scrape back and blades come out. Roll initiative.
> Narrator: Her hand dips into the pot and comes away full, and the coins vanish into her coat before anyone has closed his mouth. Then the spell breaks. Chairs scrape back all around the table and blades come out of sleeves and boots, short and ugly and very real.
> Dealer: Toll paid? Oh, it will be paid, little thief, in full and with interest. Take the gold back out of her coat, my children, and take whatever else you find in there with it. Nobody steals from this table!
> Kit: Roll initiative.

## Turn 4 (committed, 10.5s)

**Player:** I've got my rapier out and wait for them to make the first move.
**Initiative**: 1d20 (13) + 4 = `17`

**Kit:**

> Narrator: Turn order: you, the fourth player, the dealer, the door-side player, the fresco-side player. You hold your ground and wait for them to come to you. The fourth player swings at you and misses every time. The dealer hits you once: 5 piercing damage. The door-side player attacks and misses. The fresco-side player hits you: 4 slashing damage. Your turn.
> Narrator: Wren holds her ground, rapier point low, and lets them come. The fourth player swings a weighted sap and catches only air. The dealer slides in behind and his knife point finds her side. The door-side player lunges wide. The fresco-side player's knife nicks her forearm.
> Dealer: There, you see? The dead are patient, but when they strike, they strike together. Empty your coat, little thief, and perhaps we will leave you enough blood to walk home on.
> Kit: You take five piercing and four slashing, nine in all. Your turn.
