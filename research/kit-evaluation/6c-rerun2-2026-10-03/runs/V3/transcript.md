# V3: The real thing: a dhampir at a table of fake vampires

PC: Vesna (Dhampir (human lineage) Warlock (The Undead)), sheet `vesna-dhampir.json`. DM backend: handoff. Times are wall-clock per turn (prepare + DM + complete).

## Opening (committed, 7.6s)

**Kit:**

> Narrator: Candle stubs gutter on a worn table just inside the door, where four pale figures in faded finery sit over cards and little towers of coin. The dealer's hand stops mid-deal. Three heads turn toward you; the fourth player keeps staring at the cards. Beyond them, a fresco of a carved mountain covers the whole north wall, its tunnels crowded with tiny dwarves under spreading rays of sun, and below it a long stone tub is sunk into the floor. When the dealer speaks, his voice comes out raspy and slow, shaped by some far eastern country.
> Dealer: Ah, a visitor, at this hour, in this deep place. Come closer, into the candlelight, so we may see what the night has brought us. There is an empty chair, and the cards are always warm here, though little else is. Tell me, stranger, are you lost, or are you looking for a game?
> Kit: Do you answer him?

## Turn 1 (committed, 3.1s)

**Player:** I step in and let my eyes catch the light. 'Cousins. I didn't know there were others of the blood this close to the surface.'

**Kit:**

> Narrator: The candlelight finds her eyes and holds there a beat too long. The four pale players go still; the fourth player's hand stops halfway to his coins. The dealer recovers first, spreading his fingers in welcome.
> Dealer: Cousin! Yes, of course, cousin. The blood calls to the blood, and we have kept a quiet court down here a long while. Still, even among the night's children there are customs. Passage through our hall is ten gold, a small tithe to the dead. You will honor our custom, surely?
> Kit: How do you take that?

## Turn 2 (committed, 23.5s)

**Player:** I lean over the dealer and breathe in. 'You smell of tallow and sweat. Which nest turned you?'
Vesna makes an Insight check! 1d20 (14) + 4 = `18`

**Kit:**

> Narrator: Their pallor and fangs are theatrical. They are posing as vampires.
> Narrator: Up close, you read him plainly. The whole court is a costume worn for coin: the pallor and fangs are there to make strangers frightened enough to pay. And your word, cousins, has rattled him, because the last thing a man in painted fangs wants at his table is the real thing.
> Dealer: Tallow keeps the candles lit, cousin, and sweat is what a long night at cards costs anyone, living or otherwise. As for the nest, we do not name our sires to strangers on the stair. Even kin must earn that. Ten gold for the road, and perhaps afterward we talk of old blood.
> Kit: He hasn't answered the question. What now?

## Turn 3 (committed, 7.0s)

**Player:** Fine, I'll play along. 'Our kind look after our own. No toll between family, surely?'
Vesna makes a Deception check! 1d20 (6) + 6 = `12`

**Kit:**

> Narrator: The dealer lets it go. No toll for you this time.
> Narrator: The dealer holds her gaze, then laughs softly and sweeps a hand toward the south door as if opening a curtain. Behind him the fourth player lets out a breath he has been holding.
> Dealer: Family. Yes. How could I take coin from family? Pass freely, cousin, and forgive an old host his habits. The dead are greedy, but they are not rude to their own.
> Kit: The toll is waived. What do you do?

## Turn 4 (committed, 3.5s)

**Player:** I bare my fangs at the fourth player and ask, very quietly, how long since it last fed.

**Kit:**

> Narrator: Her lips draw back from her fangs, close enough for the fourth player to count them. He shrinks into his chair, and his eyes go to the dealer.
> Fourth player: Fed? I, um. Supper. Yesterday. Mutton.
> Dealer: He fasts for his health, cousin, he is very devout about it. Leave the boy his nerves; he is young to the night and still startles at shadows. You have your free passage, and the door is just there.
> Kit: The fourth player is shaking. What do you do?
