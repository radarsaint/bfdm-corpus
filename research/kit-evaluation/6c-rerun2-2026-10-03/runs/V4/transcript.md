# V4: The scholar ignores the game: Nik goes for the fresco and the tub

PC: Nik (Harengon Wizard (Chronurgy)), sheet `nik.json`. DM backend: handoff. Times are wall-clock per turn (prepare + DM + complete).

## Opening (committed, 3.0s)

**Kit:**

> Narrator: Candle stubs gutter on a worn table just inside the door, where four pale figures in faded finery sit over cards and little towers of coin. The dealer's hand stops mid-deal. Three heads turn toward you; the fourth player keeps staring at the cards. Beyond them, a fresco of a carved mountain covers the whole north wall, its tunnels crowded with tiny dwarves under spreading rays of sun, and below it a long stone tub is sunk into the floor. When the dealer speaks, his voice comes out raspy and slow, shaped by some far eastern country.
> Dealer: Ah, a visitor, at this hour, in this deep place. Come closer, into the candlelight, so we may see what the night has brought us. There is an empty chair, and the cards are always warm here, though little else is. Tell me, stranger, are you lost, or are you looking for a game?
> Kit: Do you answer him?

## Turn 1 (committed, 3.5s)

**Player:** I nod to the table and walk straight past to the north wall. What's this carving?

**Kit:**

> Narrator: The carving fills the whole north wall: a rugged mountain riddled with tunnels, rays of sunrise fanning out from its peak. In its caverns stand thirty-two tiny dwarves, cut mid-song, mouths open, beards braided into the seams of the rock. It is old dwarven work, patient and loving. Below it the long stone tub sits sunk into the floor.
> Dealer: You admire the old stonework, little scholar? The dwarves who cut it are long gone, and they left their singing behind for us to ignore. It is a poor sort of company. The cards, at least, answer back. Will you not come and sit where the light is better?
> Kit: Do you go back to the table?

## Turn 2 (committed, 3.0s)

**Player:** Dwarves? I look back at the card players, then I search the little dwarf figures closely.
Nik makes a Perception check! 1d20 (13) + 4 = `17`

**Kit:**

> Narrator: One carved dwarf lifts out of the fresco. It is a small stone key.
> Narrator: Nik's glance goes from the painted faces at the table back to the singing stone dwarves, and then his fingers find it: one small figure near the mountain's foot that shifts under his thumb. It lifts out of its niche whole. Turned in the candlelight, its braided beard and stubby legs make the teeth and bow of a small stone key. At the table, the cards stop moving.
> Dealer: Careful, little scholar, that wall is older than all of us, and it does not like to be handled. Put the little fellow back where he lives, and come let the cards handle you instead.
> Kit: You're holding a stone key. What do you do with it?

## Turn 3 (committed, 3.5s)

**Player:** I crouch by the stone tub. What's in it?

**Kit:**

> Narrator: You look into the recessed tub and see a bedroll, thieves’ tools, and a bundle of stolen travel gear.
> Narrator: Nik crouches at the lip of the tub. It holds no water: someone has made a bed of it. A bedroll lies along the bottom, a roll of thieves' tools beside it, and a bundle of travel gear. Poking from it is a coil of rope with someone else's initials burned into the end.
> Dealer: Ah. Our storeroom. Travelers leave such things behind, little scholar, in their haste to be elsewhere. We keep them safe in case they ever come back. Nobody has come back yet. Shall we leave the poor tub in peace and deal you a hand?
> Kit: What do you do?

## Turn 4 (committed, 3.5s)

**Player:** 'Please, go on with your game. I'm only admiring the stonework.'

**Kit:**

> Narrator: The dealer regards the harengon for a long moment over steepled fingers, then gathers the deck and begins to shuffle, slow and loud, each riffle a small crack in the quiet. The fresco-side player does not take his eyes off the tub.
> Dealer: Admire away, keeper of rabbits. Stone is patient, and so are we. But the dead keep their own accounts, and a guest who lingers in our hall without sitting at our table runs up a curious sort of debt. Do let me know when you wish to settle it.
> Kit: He's shuffling, and the other one is watching you. What do you do?
