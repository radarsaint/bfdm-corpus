# V7: Not paying that: haggle, then refuse, then play for it

PC: Nik (Harengon Wizard (Chronurgy)), sheet `nik.json`. DM backend: handoff. Times are wall-clock per turn (prepare + DM + complete).

## Opening (committed, 6.5s)

**Kit:**

> Narrator: Candle stubs gutter on a worn table just inside the door, where four pale figures in faded finery sit over cards and little towers of coin. The dealer's hand stops mid-deal. Three heads turn toward you; the fourth player keeps staring at the cards. Beyond them, a carved mountain covers the whole north wall, its tunnels crowded with tiny dwarves under spreading rays of sun, and below it a long stone tub is sunk into the floor. When the dealer speaks, his voice comes out raspy and slow, shaped by some far eastern country.
> Dealer: Ah, a visitor, at this hour, in this deep place. Come closer, into the candlelight, so we may see what the night has brought us. There is an empty chair, and the cards are always warm here, though little else is. Tell me, stranger, are you lost, or are you looking for a game?
> Kit: Do you answer him?

## Turn 1 (committed, 12.0s)

**Player:** I stop at the door. 'Ten gold just to walk through a room? For what, exactly?'

**Kit:**

> Narrator: The dealer smiles without showing much of his teeth and lays one long hand flat on the table, as if laying out terms.
> Dealer: For what? For safe passage, little scholar. This hall belongs to the dead, and the living who cross it pay their respects. Ten gold a head is the toll, and a modest one for the privilege of leaving with all your fingers. Will you pay the dead their due?
> Kit: Do you pay, or argue it?

## Turn 2 (committed, 11.0s)

**Player:** 'I'll give you three. That's more than this room is worth.'
Nik makes a Persuasion check! 1d20 (10) + 1 = `11`

**Kit:**

> Narrator: 3 gp gets no traction. The ask stays at 10 gp.
> Narrator: The dealer laughs, a dry little sound, and the fresco-side player snorts into his cards. Nobody reaches for the three coins.
> Dealer: Three. He offers the dead three, as if they were a ferryman with a bad leg. No, keeper of rabbits. The price was ten when you arrived and it is ten still. The dead do not bargain, and neither do their servants.
> Kit: The ask stays at ten. What do you do?

## Turn 3 (committed, 12.0s)

**Player:** 'Then I'm not paying. I'll walk through anyway.'

**Kit:**

> Narrator: You refuse the toll. The table closes ranks: the dealer's courtesy goes cold, the others shift their chairs between you and the south door, and the demand is made again as a warning.
> Narrator: The warmth drains out of the dealer's face. Without a word the door-side player and the fourth player rise and drag their chairs across the flagstones, setting them down squarely between Nik and the south door, and sit again with their arms folded.
> Dealer: Anyway. What a brave little word. Let me say it once more, slowly, as a kindness: ten gold, and you walk through. No gold, and you discover how the dead treat a guest who forgets his manners. Choose carefully.
> Kit: Two of them are sitting in your way. What do you do?

## Turn 4 (committed, 11.0s)

**Player:** Actually, wait. 'Tell you what: I'll play you for it. One hand. I win, I pass free.'

**Kit:**

> Narrator: The toll rides on the next round: win and it is waived, lose and the 10 gp is paid from the stake. You want in. Two ways to play a round: settle it with one check, or play it out as twenty-one (blackjack), closest to 21 without going over. The stake is whatever you bet; the house plays 10 gp a round. Which way?
> Narrator: The dealer's eyebrows climb. For a moment the cold holds; then the old courtesy floods back into his face like a tide coming in, and he pulls out the empty chair with one foot.
> Dealer: Now that is a language the dead understand. One hand of Twenty-One Coffins, then: win, and you walk free; lose, and the toll comes out of your stake. The house plays ten a round. Do we lay the cards out one by one, or settle it on a single toss of fate?
> Kit: Card by card, or one check?
