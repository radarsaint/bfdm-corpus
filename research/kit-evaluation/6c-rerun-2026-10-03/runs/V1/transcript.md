# V1: The silver tongue: a bard talks her way past the toll

PC: Sela (Tiefling Bard (College of Eloquence)), sheet `sela-bard.json`. DM backend: handoff. Times are wall-clock per turn (prepare + DM + complete).

## Opening (committed, 3.5s)

**Kit:**

> Narrator: Candle stubs gutter on a worn table just inside the door, where four pale figures in faded finery sit over cards and little towers of coin. The dealer's hand stops mid-deal. Three heads turn toward you; the fourth player keeps staring at the cards. Beyond them, a carved mountain covers the whole north wall, its tunnels crowded with tiny dwarves under spreading rays of sun, and below it a long stone tub is sunk into the floor. When the dealer speaks, his voice comes out raspy and slow, shaped by some far eastern country.
> Dealer: Ah, a visitor, at this hour, in this deep place. Come closer, into the candlelight, so we may see what the night has brought us. There is an empty chair, and the cards are always warm here, though little else is. Tell me, stranger, are you lost, or are you looking for a game?
> Kit: Do you answer him?

## Turn 1 (committed, 18.0s)

**Player:** I sweep in with a bow. 'Gentlemen of the night! I'd heard the finest company in Undermountain kept a table down here.'

**Kit:**

> Narrator: The dealer lays his card face down and rises halfway from his chair, returning the bow with a slow, exaggerated flourish. The fresco-side player hunches over his stack of coins.
> Dealer: Finest company, she says, and she says it so sweetly. You flatter old creatures of the dark, my lady, and flattery is a coin we count carefully down here. But the dead keep this road, and the dead are owed their due. Ten gold a head for safe passage through our hall. Will you pay what the road asks, sweet-tongue?
> Kit: Do you reply?

## Turn 2 (committed, 27.3s)

**Player:** 'Oh, the toll? Harria settled it for me at the well. She said you'd know my face.'
Sela makes a Deception check! 1d20 (12) + 10 = `22`

**Kit:**

> Narrator: The dealer lets it go. No toll for you this time.
> Narrator: The card between the dealer's fingers stops turning. The door-side player glances at him, then quickly at the floor.
> Dealer: Harria. Of course it is Harria, settling my accounts at the well as though the well were hers. Very well, I know her taste, and you are exactly it. Your road is paid, my lady, by a woman who does not deal at this table. But a chair costs nothing, and the night is long. Will you sit, or only pass through?
> Door-side player: She did? Harria never... nobody said.
> Kit: Do you reply?

## Turn 3 (committed, 39.3s)

**Player:** 'You don't have to take my word. But I'd hate to tell her you turned away her guest.'

**Kit:**

> Narrator: The dealer's smile stays exactly where it is, but his eyes go flat. The fresco-side player leans forward over his coins.
> Dealer: Tell her, then. Tell Harria whatever pleases you, and see how far her name carries below the well. You have your road, my lady; do not mistake it for my table. Guests who threaten me in my own hall find the candles burn very low. Now, do you sit with us as a guest, or stand there as her messenger?
> Fresco-side player: Sit. Or go.
> Kit: Do you reply?

## Turn 4 (committed, 11.5s)

**Player:** I take the empty chair and ask the dealer what a lady has to do to be dealt in.

**Kit:**

> Narrator: You want in. Two ways to play a round: settle it with one check, or play it out as twenty-one (blackjack), closest to 21 without going over. The stake is whatever you bet; the house plays 10 gp a round. Which way?
> Narrator: The dealer gathers the scattered cards with one sweep and squares the deck against the wood, watching her settle into the empty chair. The fourth player taps the table twice.
> Dealer: Very little, for a lady who sits so prettily. At this table we play Twenty-One Coffins: come close to twenty-one without going over, and the house pays the bold. Name your stake. And tell me how you like your fortune, my lady: card by card, or all at once?
> Kit: Which way do you play?
