## W2-20261016-01 — Offline economy and explicit redesign

**Type.** Developmental design; failures and corrections.

**Context.** Early Roanoke, 2018–2019, persistent multiplayer campaign.

**Sources.** BCS-000136, BCS-000137, BCS-000042 lines 18–52.

**Observation.** Early Roanoke used specialist jobs, offline production proportional to time away, deliberate scarcity, and barter. The Season 2 planning document explicitly says hour tallying felt like a chore, removing gold had been a mistake, and essential vocations went unfilled. It proposes daily crafting rolls, vocation bonuses, retained work progress, gold, and additional NPC labor.

**Interpretation.** The designer distinguished the desired social outcome—cooperation—from a mechanism that imposed bookkeeping without achieving a unique benefit.

**Confidence / scope.** DIRECT document-level critique; DEVELOPMENTAL within early Roanoke. The exact Brendon-authored 2019 revision should be verified independently before upgrading individual attribution.

**Competing explanations.** The replacement is proposed, not proved successful in play. This does not establish a universal preference for simplified mechanics.

**Overlap.** `research/longitudinal/roanoke-economy-2018-to-s2.md`.

**Kit relevance.** Diagnose systems whose administrative effort exceeds their actual experiential value. Do not generalize to all scarcity mechanics.

**Additional evidence needed.** Native revision attribution and later Season 2 implementation/outcomes.

**Evaluation suggestion.** Given evidence that cooperation occurred despite burdensome bookkeeping, distinguish the goal from the mechanism and propose proportionate changes.

**KIT_CONSUMPTION.** NOT_APPROVED.
