# Roanoke S3 resurrection correction: what happened afterward

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-15, America/Los_Angeles.  
**Source snapshot:** `2a650a735d8e39251979b157cf7aace9f8e443ef`.  
**Scope:** one Roanoke Season 3 correction and the remaining captured week, in a persistent, multi-DM Discord game.  
**Status:** primary-source follow-up; event-specific findings. No general-practice certification or runtime promotion.

## Finding

The captured record supports a narrower positive result than “the system changed”: **a resurrection Brendon had authorized before his correction was played out afterward.** He scheduled Clover to return Percival; he then announced that the night's resurrections stood; Percival's player subsequently narrated the return and continued the scene. This is evidence of follow-through on that commitment in the captured fiction.

The replacement for the once-per-day rule remains unidentified. Later Revivify use is visible, but players were already seeking Revivify scrolls before the correction. Later scroll use therefore does not establish that the correction caused a change of practice.

This closes part of the delivery gap in `BDC-S3-019`. It does not establish that a new rule was implemented successfully, that all three scheduled resurrections completed, or that prospective correction is Brendon's standing policy.

- Every fetched shard parsed as LF-delimited JSONL, and its returned Git blob identity matched the pinned directory listing. No file read failed. These are retrieval checks, not a rerun of the corpus test suite.
- The 37,074-row figure describes lexical coverage. It does not mean every conversation in the week received a complete semantic reading. Proposals, jokes, unrelated item powers, undead animation, and restoring the gods were not counted as applications of the corrected rule.

**Stopping rule reached:** the remaining captured week and the earlier lexical routes above were exhausted for this bounded question. No other campaign or retrospective source was opened to manufacture a missing rule.

New evidence must keep the event, cause, generalization, and present-day endorsement questions separate.
