Mirabelle – A Tragedy in Five Acts

A play performed in-world in At War’s End

Structure: Five-act verse drama with chorus, supernatural elements, and bureaucratic horror

Setting: The Cambrian coastal city of Veyne during the Devil Pox quarantine

Tone: Ironic, intimate, damning—beautiful and brutal

Staging: Stylized, mask-heavy, with ghostly chorus and Angel Nuns exaggerated in costuming and manner

---

Core Theme:

The dehumanization of the short-lived under systemic fear.

The tragedy is not the lie—it’s how easily the lie is accepted, and how little the truth would have mattered anyway.

---

Dramatis Personae

Mirabelle – Young Cambrian woman, devout and poetic. Dies early. She appears only through memory, letters, and others’ recollections.

Misrabelle – A woman of similar name and rough upbringing. Becomes the substitute. Earns the life she was never meant to have—at a cost.

Lord Jove EnCaine – Long-lived dwarven noble. Comes across as kindly, but his ease with Misrabelle’s presence reveals a hollow emotional economy.

Sere Malven – Quarantine clerk, mid-rank Cambrian. Facilitates the substitution—calmly, bitterly.

Keelo – Grave digger and messenger. Seen in the margins. Wry, grim, working-class. Provides emotional ballast.

Rethen – Jove’s steward. Suspects the truth. Removed when he becomes inconvenient.

Delsin – Angel Knight. More violent than pious. A foil to both Sere and Misrabelle.

Sister Fralienne – Lead Angel Nun. Embodies the cruelty of quarantine enforcement.

The Chorus – Masked Cambrian dead and dying. Splinters by the end of the play.

---

ACT I – The Seal and the Name

Function: Set up the contract, establish the social structure, reveal Mirabelle's vulnerability.

Mirabelle is shown writing letters and praying. Her father has arranged a marriage with Lord Jove in exchange for protection and political favor.

The Devil Pox is declared epidemic. Cambrians are forced into the Iron Heart quarantine under the Angel Nuns.

Sere processes each new entry. Keelo buries the bodies of the previous day's dead.

Mirabelle is admitted. Her name and number are recorded.

Before Act I ends, she dies offstage—without ceremony.

Final beat: a letter from Lord Jove arrives, unaware of her death. It contains permission and a promise of release.

---

ACT II – The Substitution

Function: Construct the lie. Ground it in bureaucracy, guilt, and opportunity.

Misrabelle, a stranger with a similar name, is introduced: streetwise, frightened, and tired of waiting to die.

Sere receives the letter meant for Mirabelle. She notices the name similarity, pauses.

Misrabelle jokes about passing as her, but Sere doesn’t laugh.

The next scene: Sere hands Misrabelle the folded letter and a stack of forged clearance papers. “The name is close enough.”

Keelo raises objections, but helps. He teaches Misrabelle how to mimic Mirabelle’s phrasing from memory fragments.

Final beat: Misrabelle is disguised and delivered under forged papers. Delsin nearly stops her—but the paperwork is clean.

## ACT III – THE LIE THAT HOLDS

### Function:

The impersonation is no longer a deception—it becomes a social fact. Misrabelle is nearly flawless, and those who suspect the truth either vanish, stay silent, or are outmaneuvered. The system rewards whoever can play their part best.

![](assets/image2.png)

### Scene I – The Family Requests Attendance

Setting: Lord EnCaine’s drawing room. Morning light. A decanter of wine untouched.

RETHEN enters with a letter from House Cren: formal, expectant, emotionally distant but socially appropriate.

MISRABELLE reads it, stiffens—then folds it and speaks quietly:

“They beat me once, over a spilled lamp.
 My brother threw a cat down a well.
 Tell the Lord, if he loves me, he will not let them near me.”

She turns her back. Rethen watches her a moment longer than he should.

![](assets/image2.png)

### Scene II – Rethen Watches

Setting: A corridor. Jove and Rethen walk.

Rethen reports Misrabelle’s reaction. Jove shrugs:

“She is here. That is enough.”

Rethen hesitates, then produces a note from her prior schooling that doesn’t align with something she claimed.

“She said she learned numbers in the mountain school.
 This says she was taught at the river seminary.”

Jove doesn’t care. Says:

“People misremember. Or wish to forget. Let her.”

Rethen nods—but pockets the letter rather than filing it.

![](assets/image2.png)

### Scene III – The Banquet Rehearsal

Setting: Estate hall. MISRABELLE is measuring distances with a gloved hand, trailing attendants like smoke.

She demands colored silks for the pillars, specific flowers, three carved doves.

Jove watches her indulgently. Rethen takes notes.

Misrabelle pauses, locks eyes with Rethen.

MISRABELLE:
 “You take notes like someone planning a speech.”

RETHEN:
 “Only lists. For now.”

![](assets/image2.png)

### Scene IV – Keelo Delivers an Item

Setting: Garden alcove. MISRABELLE seated with a mirror. KELO arrives with a package—perhaps something symbolic, like a scarf or document recovered from the Iron Heart.

She thanks him with formality—far removed from her former self.

KEELO:
 “You don’t talk like the girl I remember.”

MISRABELLE:
 “I remember her too.
 She was always pretending to be me.”

![](assets/image2.png)

### Scene V – Rethen is Removed

Setting: Jove’s study. Quiet.

Rethen enters with papers and asks to delay the ceremony until the family sends direct confirmation of misrabelle's claims.

Jove declines. Gently, even kindly.

“You’ve done well. You deserve quieter work.”

Rethen is dismissed. Not punished—absorbed.
 The final shot: him walking out with the letter still folded in his coat.

![](assets/image2.png)

### Scene VI – Misrabelle in the Chapel

Setting: The small estate chapel, dressed for the wedding.

MISRABELLE stands alone. She touches the robe she’ll wear.

MISRABELLE (to herself):
 “I have been called worse than a name.
 Now they call me bride.”

The bells toll outside. She doesn’t flinch.

![](assets/image2.png)

---

ACT IV – The Ghost at the Wedding

Function: Confront the cost of the lie and expose the society’s rot.

Misrabelle confesses. Not in full. “I am not the girl you wrote to. But I was the one who read your words.”

Jove replies: “And you read them well.”

The wedding is announced.

Angel Nuns return. Sister Fralienne and Delsin demand Misrabelle’s return—she was never properly cleared.

Jove pays them off. He offers deeds, names, and trade permissions. They leave without argument.

Sere is arrested offstage for forging plague documents. The Chorus murmurs her name once, then stops.

Final beat: Misrabelle walks into the chapel alone.

---

ACT V – Names Forgotten, Names Taken

Function: Collapse everything—truth, identity, health, structure.

The plague reaches the estate. The Chorus fragments. Voices become singular, discordant.

Keelo coughs blood mid-line. He delivers one final letter—blank. “They forgot what they wanted to say.”

Misrabelle, now unrecognizable even to herself, wanders through the ruins. No one calls her name.

Jove builds a statue. The plaque reads: To Mirabelle. The one who came. He looks at it, once, then leaves.

Final Chorus line:

> “We do not know which girl lived. Only which one was remembered.”

---

Refinements Made:

Pacing: Each act now moves forward with urgency and purpose. No act is static. The transition from deception to complicity is gradual and credible.

Character Logic: Sere is no longer morally confused—she’s clearly bitter, deliberate. Misrabelle's turn from desperation to disorientation is tracked in beats. Jove is ambiguous but never innocent.

Theme Delivery: The play never says Cambrians are interchangeable. But every action confirms that no one questions the swap. Even Misrabelle can’t prove herself real by the end.

Keelo’s Role: Strengthened. Not a plot catalyst, but a narrative mirror. He doesn’t stop the lie—but he names what it costs.

Mirabelle A Tragedy in Five Acts

Act I, Scene 1 – The Ledger and the Girl

Setting: The intake chamber of the College of Iron Heart. Stone-walled, vaulted, cold. A desk. A tall iron ledger. Shelves of numbered scarves and name-tags. A curtained rear threshold. The Chorus, masked, stands like sentinels in the gloom.

CHORUS (in unison)

We dream the girl before she enters.
 We know the smell—wet silk, candle smoke, the salt of forgotten letters.
 We know the feet: slow, stitched with hope. The heels of girls who were promised.
 She is not the first to come bearing a name that will not survive her.

They will say she was chosen.
 They will say her name twice, then forget it.
 They will say she was loved—once.
 That will be enough for paperwork.

But we have seen the trick before:
 The fruit that’s ripened just past want.
 The face that fits the memory better than the original.
 The lie that wears its shoes without limping.

One girl will vanish. One girl will live.
 We do not yet know which is which.

They part. One soft bell tolls. A tall, narrow door creaks open.

(Enter MIRABELLE, escorted by two ANGEL NUNS in formal robes. Their wings are ceremonial and heavy, stitched to their shoulders like burdens. MIRABELLE wears a weathered blue shawl and clutches a letter. Her bracelet is woven with red and silver thread.)

(SERE MALVEN is seated at the desk, quill in hand, eyes on the ledger.)

SERE
 Number.

ANGEL NUN
 Seventeen-oh-three. Cambrian. Unconfirmed onset. Literate.

SERE
 Name?

MIRABELLE (soft but clear)
 Mirabelle of Veyne. Daughter of Cren. Betrothed by writ to Lord EnCaine.

SERE (not looking up)
 Shoes off. Bracelet in the cloth. Letter on the desk.

(Mirabelle hesitates. One Nun removes her bracelet. It drops with a soft metallic clink. Mirabelle sets the letter down carefully.)

MIRABELLE
 His seal is unbroken. He said once I was processed, I’d be—seen.

SERE (brief pause)
 Many are promised. Not all are fetched.

MIRABELLE
 He said I mattered.

SERE (quietly)
 He said that once.
 That makes it true for now.

(Mirabelle is guided toward the Chorus. She does not yet join them. She stands beside them, uncertain.)

(KEELO crosses rear stage dragging a shrouded form. He stops midstage and turns to the audience.)

KEELO (to audience)
 They always ask to keep the pretty ones.
 The ones who write. The ones who pray.
 But the ground don’t ask your promises.
 The ground just learns your name by weight.

(Lights dim. Sere closes the ledger. Bell sounds once more. The curtain flutters in a breathless draft. Scene ends.)

Act I, Scene 2 – The Old Chapel

Setting: A once-handsome lecture hall, now repurposed as a chapel. The summoning rings once carved in the floor have been gouged out and replaced with winged glyphs in chalk. A single high window has been bricked over. Bookshelves are chained shut. Candles burn from welded sconces. It is late afternoon.

(An ANGEL NUN moves slowly down the center aisle, swinging a censer that hisses white smoke. She stops beside a bundle of blankets on the floor—clearly a patient—and speaks with brittle warmth.)

ANGEL NUN
 When the eyes cloud, you’ll be transferred. Try not to linger. It makes the paperwork untidy.

(She smiles and walks off. Behind her, the patient coughs once. No one moves.)

(Enter MIRABELLE. She’s paler than in Scene 1. Her shawl is the same, but her walk is slower. She kneels at the front row, center. The altar is a repurposed lectern, stained.)

MIRABELLE (quietly)
 Let him be kind. Let him be young—or kind will do. Let him smell like clean wool and not flint. Let his hands not be old. Let his words be his. Let his name hold.

Let the dowry move faster than the tide. Let it buy a corridor, a carriage, a crossing. Let it open a door before the door locks. Let my mother wear shoes again. Let my brother forget this place.

Let me be gone before I belong here. Let me not end on this bench.

(She unfolds a paper from her sleeve. It’s blank. She folds it again.)

(KEELO appears at the side entrance. He carries a bucket and a tool roll. He watches her for a moment before stepping in.)

KEELO
 That’s a lot of letting.

MIRABELLE (startled)
 I thought you were—

KEELO
 We don’t knock in the chapel anymore. No one hears it.

(He lowers himself onto the bench behind her with a grunt.)

KEELO (cont’d)
 You’re praying to the angel? Or to the dwarf?

MIRABELLE
 To Seraphine. She sees the long plan. And maybe him, too. I don’t know. I’m not good at splitting prayers.

KEELO
 Most folks down here pray to the floor. At least it holds them.

MIRABELLE
 He said he’d speak for me. That he’d fetch me himself. That I’d be honored in name and joined by vow. His letter used real ink.

KEELO
 Real ink? Must be love.

MIRABELLE
 He called me resilient.

KEELO
 Resilient’s what they write when they don’t want to say suffering. You were a student, weren’t you?

MIRABELLE
 Before the walls were sealed. Before the angels took the library. I studied conjuring.

KEELO
 Still are. You’re conjuring a man out of a letter and a promise.

MIRABELLE
 And what are you conjuring, gravedigger?

KEELO
 Space. Enough to bury a name in, without crushing the next one.

(A bell tolls once, slow and dull. Keelo stands. He leaves a folded cloth on the bench beside her.)

KEELO (cont’d)
 Don’t let them see you afraid. They like it too much.

(He exits. Mirabelle remains kneeling.)

MIRABELLE (softly)
 Let me be seen before I disappear.

Act I, Scene 3 – The Sign and the Scheme

Setting: A narrow cell within the College of Iron Heart. The room is bare stone and poorly sealed. A cot with a moth-eaten blanket. A brass basin bolted to the wall. One high barred window. A stub of candlelight. The number “1703” is chalked on the wall above the door.

(Lights rise. MIRABELLE lies curled beneath the blanket, pale. She wakes abruptly with a convulsive cough. Another. A third, harsher. She sits up—and her hand comes away red.)

---

MIRABELLE

No.

No, no. It’s dry air. The plaster. I’ve bitten my tongue. That’s all.

(She stares at the blood.)

It’s too early.

---

(She stands. Sways. Crosses to the basin and washes the blood off quickly. More comes. She wets the corner of the blanket and scrubs at her sleeve, but the stain spreads.)

---

MIRABELLE (cont’d)

They’ll move me upstairs. They’ll put me in the wet hall with the wheezers and the wax-eyed.

If they mark me infected, the dowry stops.

If the dowry stops, the papers won’t clear.

If the papers don’t clear, mother’s name won’t reach the border.

My brother will still be here when the snow cuts off the pass.

(Another cough. She presses her knuckles to her mouth. Blood again.)

---

(She yanks off her overshirt and flips it inside out. Ties the arms around her waist like a sash. The inner fabric still shows damp, but less vivid.)

---

MIRABELLE (cont’d)

Don’t bleed where they can see.

Don’t shake. Don’t stammer.

Don’t look like prey.

---

(She moves to the door. Listens. Nothing yet. Her hand grips the iron latch. Her breath comes fast.)

---

MIRABELLE (cont’d)

The Iron Heart still hums. They said it was sealed, but it hums.

Even now, I can feel it—under the stone, behind the walls. The old wheel still turns.

It doesn’t have to make spells anymore. It can still call.

I was trained for this.

We were taught how to guide shape through breath and need.

I could stand at the gate and speak the name with every part of me.

I could conjure him—not a shape, not a fiction—

him, Lord EnCaine, wherever he is. Pull him to me by blood-contract and promise-seal.

Let him arrive whole, startled, sweating from the sudden journey—

but real.

Let him see me before the fever takes my eyes.

---

(A hard KNOCK at the door. She gasps. Freezes.)

---

ANGEL NUN (offstage)

Seventeen-oh-three. We require confirmation of breath and pulse.

---

MIRABELLE (to herself)

I can’t. Not now. Not yet.

---

ANGEL NUN (offstage)

You will be noted as noncompliant.

---

MIRABELLE

Tonight. It has to be tonight.

I’ll need help. Someone who knows the shaft behind the laundry.

Keelo.

He’d know how to lift the hatch.

He’d do it, if I asked.

If I swore not to die before it mattered.

---

(Another knock. She tears her blanket in two, wraps the rags around her arms like bindings. She smears the blood up into her forearms to blur it. The candle gutter-flickers.)

---

MIRABELLE (cont’d)

Midnight. Through the disused crypt. Past the hall of bells.

One stair down. One ward broken.

One name spoken true.

Let me reach it before they reach me.

---

(She falls silent as the footsteps pause just beyond the door. The light narrows to a single bar across her face. Scene end.)

Act I, Scene 4 – The Iron Heart and the Break

Scene opens in darkness. Then:

Three settings emerge, one by one in shifting light:

A door, marked 1703, half-lit by torchlight.

A low gravedigger’s alcove hung with bone tags.

The Iron Heart—a massive sea-cave machine, unmoving yet humming like something that dreams in metal.

SCENE I – The Missing Number

(Lights rise on DELSIN, Angel Knight, standing outside MIRABELLE’s empty cell. His wings are painted lacquer, his boots shine, his voice is slow and calm. He wears cruelty like liturgy.)

DELSINSeventeen-oh-three.You have elected silence.You have abandoned your number.You are no longer eligible for outcome.

(He draws a sealing rod from his belt. It clicks open, glowing faint blue.)

DELSIN (cont’d)This cell now houses vacancy.This door no longer opens.This girl no longer qualifies.

(He marks a glyph beside the door. A shimmer. A finality. He turns.)

DELSIN (cont’d)Waste by absence.Erased by lapse.May her expiration ease the count.

(He exits.)

SCENE II – The Break

(Lights rise on KELO’s alcove. He sits cross-legged among sacks and slats, bone tags clicking softly as he sorts them. His tools are laid out in a grid. DELSIN enters behind him.)

DELSINYou sat beside her in the chapel.

KEELOShe prayed. I listened.Some days that’s my whole job.

DELSINShe’s gone. You know where.

KEELOYou can’t interrogate a man who only shovels.We spend all day burying what people want to forget.

DELSINAnd yet you remember the numbers.

KEELO (looking up)Only the ones they say sweetly.

(A pause. DELSIN steps closer.)

DELSINSince your mouth won’t speak,your marrow can remember.

(He strikes. The sound is wet and final. KELO cries out, folding at the hip, his leg crooked under him. DELSIN straightens his robes and leaves. He does not look back.)

SCENE V – The Gravedigger’s Vow

(KEELO alone. He breathes hard, binding his leg with shaking cloth. The light grows dimmer.)

KEELOAnd that’s how they do it.Not with fire. With forms.Not with blades. With blanks.She was too sharp, too bright.That kind of hope don’t sit well in a ledger.

(Slowly rises, gripping the wall.)

She said the Heart still whispered.That the wheel remembered her shape.That if she called loud enough—with breath and will and everything left—he would come.

Not a symbol. Not a glamour.

The man.Leather boots. Bad handwriting.Real weight on the boards.

(Steps forward.)

I thought it was fever.

I was wrong.

SCENE VI – The Iron Heart

(The cave. Misted light. The glyphs faintly pulse. The Iron Heart turns once, like a breath caught in iron lungs. MIRABELLE lies at the center of a spiral diagram. Her feet are bare. Her arms are stretched outward. Her eyes are shut.)

(KEELO hobbles into view. His gait is broken and raw, but he presses forward. When he sees her, he stops. The stillness in the chamber makes the air feel suspended.)

KEELONo. No, girl. Not like this.

(He sinks to one knee beside her, pain thick in his breath.)

KEELO (cont’d)You reached it. You called. And now you lie where only gods sleep.

(He brushes a thread of hair from her brow.)

You weren’t meant to make it. That’s what they counted on. A girl with too much name and too little time.

(He stands, barely. Limps forward. The Heart’s hum swells beneath him.)

KEELO (to the Heart)Did you hear her? Did you taste her name on the spell? Was it enough to stir the gears that rust in silence?

You were a wound once. A miracle pumped like a vein beneath stone. You gave the world its conjurors and kept no memory for yourself.

But she remembered you. She spoke like the wheel was listening. Like it could still pull the living through.

She called him by breath, by name, by promise inked in blood.

Not a shadow. Not a statue.

A man. Her man. Hers to summon. Hers to need.

And what did you do?

(He turns back to her, kneels again.)

KEELO (cont’d)You answered. You took her in. You laid her down like scripture.

(Softer now.)

She’s not gone. But she’s not here. You hollowed her to hold the spell.

So now I have to carry what’s left.

(His voice breaks slightly.)

I will break myself to lift her. I will split the bone you cracked and bleed from both ends to see her live.

I’ll keep her in the dark if I must. In straw. In salt. In song.

Until she wakes.

(He gathers her into his arms with care and agony. The Heart’s hum grows rhythmic—like a heartbeat slowed.)

KEELO (last breath before exit)She called you once. If he does come—tell him she waited.

(The wheel behind them groans once, and a low red glow pulses through the runes. Lights fade to black.)

ACT I ENDS.**

ACT II, SCENE I – The Letter and the Substitution

Setting: The intake chamber of the College of Iron Heart. Dimmer now. The tall ledger remains, though it leans slightly. Shelves of scarves and tags have thinned. A shaft of dusk light pierces from the clerestory. KELO, leg stiff, moves trays of inked stamps and dry cloth. SERE enters with a sealed envelope, edges bruised from travel.

(SERE slices the seal with an envelope blade. Unfolds the letter. Begins to read aloud in a tone of professional detachment that falters mid-paragraph.)

---

SERE (reading)

“To the betrothed I have not yet touched—

May your breath be longer than your fever.

May your name be clear, and your wrists unbound.

I come before the registry,

that I may see with mine own eyes

what letters have promised me:

your voice in a room of my choosing.”

(He lowers the page. Silence. Then, softly—)

He’s come early.

---

(He moves to the ledger. Scans. Finds the number.)

---

SERE (cont’d)

1703.

Sealed. No clearance. No remains.

Only the silence of a locked door and no bell rung since.

(Looking back to the letter, voice sharpening.)

He names her.

He claims her.

And she’s gone to the walls.

(Sudden motion—he grips the ledger, then releases it.)

---

SERE (cont’d)

If I say she’s lost, there must be a reason.

If I say the reason, I must cite the fault.

If I cite the fault, it becomes mine.

And that I cannot afford.

---

(KEELO coughs. An unswept sound, intentional. He does not look up.)

---

SERE

Speak, if you must. Or swallow it whole.

---

KEELO

Just struck me, that letter of his.

Read more like a supper order than a summons.

---

SERE

You’ve the wit for insolence, I see. Not the time.

---

KEELO (limping closer)

You’ve got a noble downstairs expecting a girl who no longer breathes.

But what he’s asked for isn’t the girl—

It’s the name. The number. The veil.

And that, clerk, is still in your keeping.

---

SERE

There is no other 1703.

---

KEELO

No.

But there is a 1708.

Misrabelle. Cambrian. Literate. Inhale of pride, exhale of complaint.

(beat)

She shares the syllables.

A half-turn of chalk and she’s what he came for.

The name will please the ledger. The number will confuse no one.

And she—

She’s the sort who thinks betrothal to a dwarf lord sounds better than plague rot and soup queues.

---

(SERE studies him now.)

---

SERE

She’s vain.

---

KEELO

Which is not the worst trait in a bride.

And she won’t need coaxing. Just staging.

Tell her she’s been chosen.

Tell her he asked for her by description.

Tell her the old one’s name fell out of ink, and hers rose in its place.

---

(SERE considers. Lays the letter flat. Taps the ink blotter once. Then once again.)

---

SERE

There will be a reckoning.

---

KEELO

There always is.

But it don’t always land where you think.

---

(Silence stretches. The tall ledger remains open to 1703. SERE gently lifts the page and begins to cross-reference.)

(Scene ends)

---

ACT II, SCENE II – The Girl and the Fetching

Setting: Begins in the intake chamber—SERE at the desk, adjusting his collar, smoothing the ledger with nervous precision. The ANGEL NUNS stand before him in a triangular formation, wings stilled, eyes forward.

---

SERE

You’ll fetch her quietly.

Number one-seven-zero-eight. Misrabelle. Cambrian.

Take her to Keelo’s alcove—not the Chapel.

Tell no one else. If questioned, refer them to my ledger. No one above your station will bother reading it.

---

ANGEL NUN 1

Is she marked for release?

---

SERE (curt)

She’s marked for convenience. That is sufficient.

Lord EnCaine has arrived early. I am preparing him for a... seamless transition.

---

(He moves toward the back curtain, smoothing his robe.)

---

SERE (cont’d)

The girl needn’t understand anything yet.

Let Keelo clean the edges before she sees the center.

(He exits.)

---

(The NUNS remain still for a beat. Then turn together. A slow walk down a side corridor. Their torchlight plays against stained stone.)

---

ANGEL NUN 2 (low)

Keelo’s alcove? That’s irregular.

---

ANGEL NUN 3

So is this order. But the clerk signs beneath the seal now.

---

ANGEL NUN 1

If we question it aloud, we’ll find ourselves reassigned to spore wards.

Or not reassigned at all.

---

ANGEL NUN 2

Then let’s walk.

Let the stone hear no dissent.

---

(They turn the final corner. MISRABELLE’s cell comes into view. The light sharpens into focus. She is seated, fussing with her hair, perfectly unaware.)

Setting: Dormitory, College of Iron Heart. Sparse but personalized. MISRABELLE has stacked crates into a makeshift dressing table. A broken comb. A ribbon she clearly bartered for. She sits cross-legged on the cot, braiding her hair with more drama than necessity.

(Three ANGEL NUNS enter in a hush. One rings a chime. MISRABELLE doesn’t move.)

---

ANGEL NUN 1

Misrabelle. Number one-seven-zero-eight. You will stand.

---

MISRABELLE (without turning)

If this is about the extra blanket—I returned it. You can count the fringe.

---

ANGEL NUN 2

You are summoned.

---

MISRABELLE

By whom?

(beat)

If it’s Sister Fralienne, tell her the boils cleared and she needn’t sniff me twice.

If it’s the ration clerk, I’ve already explained the lentil incident.

---

(The Nuns do not respond.)

---

ANGEL NUN 3

Shoes. Robe. Now.

---

MISRABELLE (finally turning)

Is this an inspection?

Am I being promoted?

I did say I had excellent handwriting. And a noble chin.

(She poses briefly. None of the Nuns react.)

---

MISRABELLE (cont’d)

No?

Then someone’s died.

Or I’ve been mistaken for someone quieter.

---

(She rises slowly, brushing invisible dust from her robe. She speaks to fill the silence.)

---

MISRABELLE (cont’d)

It’s terribly rude not to tell a girl why she’s being escorted by all three of you.

Feels rather ceremonial. Are we celebrating something?

---

ANGEL NUN 1

You will walk, or we will carry.
