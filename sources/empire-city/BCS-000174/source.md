Hampstead Module

# **Borough Description**

Hampstead, heart of Empire City.  Sure The City is clean and glorious, Ferrytown is chaotic beauty, Staaten is pure industry, and Kingsbridge is style and class.  But Hampstead, Hampstead is the rotten core of this city.  Hampstead houses the first building in Empire City, the Empire City Gazette.  The cornerstone of the Gazette, underneath layers of grease and grime are the words “Fear of loss drives all strife. / Truth is the only path to liberty. / Let Justice and Mercy be equal.”

That’s the entire burrough though, grime, dust, dirt, with insects everywhere.  The buildings are made of wood and stone, with rarely a clean window to look out of.  The streets are uneven, cobbled with a mix of bricks and stone.  At night most of the lamps are lit, shedding dim yellow pools, just a bit too far apart.

Hampstead’s weather is often hot, muggy, mildly overcast.  Just enough sun comes through the clouds to keep it hot, there is just enough clouds overhead to trap the heat and moisture in the air.  Thick fog rolls in at irregular times, making it difficult to breathe.  People whisper about the fog being insidious.

The people of Hampstead walk with a hunch, like a weight is on the shoulders, like the air itself is heavy.  Joy seems to be universally absent from the faces of the people.  Illness is so commonplace in Hampstead that people have stopped even looking for cures.

Soldiers can be seen marching down every street, filling every public space, listening in on every conversation.  They force themselves into homes and into beds.   They buy up all the goods, leaving overpriced, half rotten food for the people.  The stranglehold the British have on Hampstead is choking the very life from Empire City’s core.

But there are signs of rebellion.  In secret rooms, and private residences groups are found to be plotting against the occupation of their town.  The soldiers come down hard on the rebellion when they find them.  Graffiti pops up from time to time, its common to see soldiers forcing people to clean the following words off the walls:  
*"Like a plague of locusts they came. They came to our streets, our shops, our homes, they took from us, nearly starved us, and expected us to sit idly by silent in our suffering.* But ***no more*** *now We will become the locusts. We will take back what belongs to us, repulse them, overwhelm them.* **Consume them.**"

# **Sets and Actors**

## **Sets**

* ### **Streets of Hampstead**

  * The cobbled streets are grimy, the air thick and moist.  Windows are covered in a film from not being cared for in far too long.  Most of the buildings are wooden, or a mix of wood and stone.  Swarms of insects are seen flying throughout the borough.  Each street has a muck gutter on either side.  A notice is posted:  
    \`\`\`Notice, all silver is property of the crown.  Anyone found to have silver on them will have it confiscated and will be fined 10 gold, or spend a day in the stocks

    Further, Noone without a pass is to be out on the streets past 10PM.  Violation of this order will result in a 100 gold fine, and up to 3 days locked up.\`\`\`

* ### **Extraordinary Ordinary**

  * This multi-story building is a combination general store, tavern, inn, town meeting hall, and much more.  The main floor is a large room filled with table and chairs, sawdust on the floor, and a small, raised stage on one wall.  Semi-private meeting rooms are available to rent, and there are some rooms still available for rent upstairs. 

* ### **Boars Head Butchery**

  * This building smells heavily of blood and salt.  There is a counter that radiates cold, with various cuts of meat sitting on it, even some more exotic meats like snake.  The wall behind the counter has cured meats hanging from it.  There is a darkened door to the back room.

* ### **Outside the Empire City Gazette**

  * This busy building is one of the few primarily stone buildings in the area.  The smell of ink is heavy in the air.  At all times there are two guards that stand flanking the door.  There is a grime covered cornerstone on the building that states:  
    \`\`\` Fear of loss drives all strife.  
    Truth is the only path to liberty.  
    Let Justice and Mercy be equal. \`\`\`

* ### **Inside the Empire City Gazette**

  * (only opens up after players have gotten past the guards, or have been sent inside).   
    This building is large, with huge printing presses running nonstop.  Dozens of cots can be seen where space allows.  A large pile of silver, partially melted sits in a crucible with some sort of dark liquid.  Next to the crucible is a warning “Do Not Approach without permission from an officer.”  
    \-(if players try to steal the silver, an alarm spell will go off and alert 3d6+10 soldiers who will try to capture and interrogate the players)

* ### **Sewers of Hampstead**

  * (Only opens up after players have gotten the quest to search for rats on week 1\)  
    These sewers are where the muck from the ruts in the streets drain to.  They are circular, only about 12 feet wide, with blackened water running down the center.  Every 15-20 feet there is drainage from above.  The walls are filled with cracks.

* ### **Winding Alleys of Hampstead**

  * Narrow, trash strewn alleys run maze like throughout the borough.  At night most of the lanterns are darkened and dimmed in these alleys, like the firelight isn’t as bright as it should be.  
    (To notice the swarms of insects blocking the light, DC 15 perception or Investigation check if looking for cause of darkened lights)

* ### **Ricker-Lent-Smith Homestead and Cemetery**

  * Some claim this is the oldest building in Empire city.  It is currently the home of a kindly old mortician named Aljarad Smith.  The quaint home doubles as an affordable mortuary and cemetery for many people in Hampstead.  Mr. Smith is known for always having a cup or bag of tea ready for anyone wanting to visit. (DM NOTE: If a player drinks the tea, 1d6 hours after leaving the homestead, or when you remember to, have them gain a level of Hampstead Damnation, but do not tell them how they got it)

* ### **George III Airship Port**

  * This is a major port of call for airships to dock and land.  The tallest and largest building in Hampstead is placed atop a large hill, and is full of towers with landing pads and docks for ships to park.  This is also the cleanest and most well kept part of Hampstead, with pristine white stonework and large mosaic stained glass.  Walkways interconnect many towers, and a single long stair leads down to the streets.  Currently the British are heavily restricting air traffic, and occupying the port.

# **Encounters**

####  HAMPSTEAD 1 \- Awww Rats\! {#hampstead-1---awww-rats!}

The players will have hopefully talked to Aljarad earlier in the day/week.  Aljarad will have told the players of people in Hampstead coming up missing late in the evenings, vanishing into the sewers.  Tonight, he has procured enough curfew passes for the players to be out en masse.  He also has a rough location for the players to look underground based on where the disappearances have been happening.  (Give this quick synopsis to the group so anyone who missed the lead in isn't lost)

This is where the encounter starts.  Hopefully there are enough DMs to run about 10 players to a group.  If possible, divide up the players into equal groups, and have them go into the sewers through different locations.

Once the players are in the sewers, let them explore for a bit.  The sewer tunnels are only 10 feet in diameter.  Build a claustrophobic atmosphere, have them use skill checks to track, stay out of the muck, look for signs of activity.  Each player should roll a skill check that is appropriate for the environment and search.  Make sure to add in details as they make these checks.  Rubble, chittering, feeling of being watched.  Anything that will help give them fodder to use skill checks.  Every time a player rolls a check, give them an answer that adds to the fodder they need.  For instance if someone makes a survival check for signs of those taken, you might say “The signs point you to the right, where you see what appears to be a pile of ratty clothes.”  The DC should be relatively easy, 10 is a success.  As long as the players get more than half successes, they will not lose the surprise in the upcoming combat.  
![][image1]  
Once each player has made a skill check, and you know if they are caught unaware, Chimeric Sewer Rats pour out of holes (share the above photo), there are 2 rats per player, and will fight 2 to a player.  If the players failed the skill challenge, the rats initiative is 20\.  Otherwise it is 1\.  Have the players roll initiative.  The rats pair up one each player.  One rat of each pair will use the help action, the other will attack, as long as there are two rats on the player.  The rats are aware of the players and are not surprised.  These rats have a fly speed equal to half their walk speed.

Anytime a rat dies, its body liquifies into a pool of organic goo.  Once observed, the player with the highest Arcana may roll Arcana to identify what they are seeing.  The DC is 15, and if successful, they will know the goo is tied to Aberrations and Oozes.  That is all the info that can be gleaned atthis time.

At the beginning of the rats initiative count, every 2 piles of goo will recombine at a player (generally the person nearest the two piles, or pick a player) and will always attempt to recombine next to another rat so it can use pack tactics ability.  These new rats have 2 heads, 2 tails, 8 rat legs, 8 spider legs, 4 wings and use the “Diseased Giant Rat” stat block (can no longer fly).

Then when these Diseased Giant Rats die, again they turn to a pile of goo.  At this point, allow the player with the highest Medicine to make a check.  If they hit 12 or higher, let them know that this goo appears more unstable than the previous goo.

When there are two Diseased Giant Rat goo piles, at the top of the rats initiative, they once again recombine.  But this time they will try to recombine on a player that has not been diseased by a diseased rat (double preference if there is another rat on said person).  Use the “Diseased Rat Swarm” stat block, but describe it as the following: 4 heads, 4 tails, 16 rat legs, 16 spider legs, 8 wings and as large as a dog, and definitely can not fly anymore.  When it dies, its body does not liquify, but stays as the corpse of this abomination.  If there is an odd number of players, there will be one giant rat that doesn’t get to combine.  This is as intended.  No matter the number of players, this encounter is considered a medium difficulty and is supposed to force players to use some resources, hurt people, and make them have to deal with the ongoing disease that the rats can pass on.  Make sure that if players get low on hp, you should spread the attacks.  They can smell the disease on people, and want to spread it.  Anyone that is bitten by any of these rats will gain one level of Hampstead Damnation at the end of the event (for ease of running), but will not gain more damnation from these rats.

Each group will end at different times, as they finish, move them to a communal room.  This treats it as each group arrives at the next section with differing timings.  Events in this next section may impact any ongoing rat fight.

After that encounter, and the players of the group move to the more massive room, give the players this description:  
\`\`\`You all have all followed the signs, and have found a large antichamber at least 50 feet across, and 25 feet high.  All the muck from your tunnel flows into this vaulted room.  Islands of debris have figures huddled together.  Some are humanoid rat people, some just appear to be disfigured and twisted.\`\`\`

The people in this room are not aggressive, and instead are cautious.  There are about 4 wererats, and 4 vampires here.  If at any point an NPC in this room is taken to or below half hp, they will get a reaction to disengage and move their full speed trying to get away.  If the party attacks them at all they will defend themselves, but lacking attack they will otherwise attempt stay back.  One of the disfigured people here will talk for their group if any are addressed.

As he addresses the party, give them the following description:  
\`\`\`Blighted from beauty, this man’s form is twisted.  His clothing, while nicer than the others near him, is still tattered and patched.  He stands up and uses a cane to approach the party, looking as if to be the wall between the newcomers, and the people that were already here.\`\`\`  
Then have him say (something like, depending on what the party said) “I am Count Kolro of the under people.  We house those who cannot fit in topside.  Why may I ask have you entered my domain?”

If the PC’s talk to him, he will not know anything of the missing people, and honestly say it sounds like the topside is just trying to get his people killed.  As long as the party works out a friendly alliance, He will offer to grant any of them, and their friends, free passage through his sewers at night to avoid the curfew.  As long as they do not attack his people now or in the future.

#### 6 Shots \- Drinking Game {#6-shots---drinking-game}

A drinking game where you get 6 magical shot glasses set in front of you.  Everyone chips in an amount of gold, and whoever successfully drinks the most gets the pool.

Each shot glass corresponds to a stat:  
Shot of Weakness  
Shot of Stumbling  
Shot of Illness  
Shot of the Dunce  
Shot of Foolishness  
Shot of Repulsion

Each shot you take, if you pass the save, gives you a \+1 to that stat for 6 hours, if you fail, gives you a \-1 to that stat for 6 hours.  Each round the NPC will announce how potent the next shot is by rolling a d12+6 and pulling down a bottle associated with that potency.  Then the players will decide which shot glass to drink from.  The NPC will fill the shot glass, and the player rolls a saving throw tied to the stat in question.  If they pass, they glow golden for a moment, if the fail they glow a dull silver.  The shot glass vanishes back to the bar, and the next round starts.  Once all six shots are done, whoever succeeded the most gets the gold.  If there is a tie, the winners can split the pool, or agree to double down and do 6 more shots, but this time the save DC is 2d12+6.  Each time there is a tie, if they want to continue, the DC will go up by 1d12.

If someone wants to quit before they have drunk the 6 shots, they can walk away from the table, but will take a \-1 to each stat they have not drank the shot of yet.

# **Hampstead Damnations** 

“*The Alchemists were mad.  Those things should have never been experimented on.  Now the whole town is cursed to hell because of their perverse experiments against the natural order.  Don’t come to Hampstead, don’t breathe it’s air, Don’t go into the fog, and don’t let the bedbugs bite.*”

Hampstead’s Damnation is the slow mutation of your mind, body, and some believe your very soul.  As you get exposed to the woes of Hampstead, you will receive layers of corruption that bind to your body, and force it to go through changes. The only way to remove levels of this damnation unless specified by the source of the damnation are: 

* A long rest outside of Hampstead will remove one level of long term damnation.  
* A long rest in Ferrytown will remove one additional level of long term damnation.  
* Being subjected to Remove Curse or Lesser Restoration will remove one level of long term damnation per casting (will not affect conditional damnation)  
* Greater Restoration or Wish can fully cleanse all levels of damnation from your body and soul.

These effects are not cumulative, so when you move up on this list, you lose the previous effect, and gain the new one.  Make sure to note each conditional level you are afflicted by, so that you know when you can remove it.

**Special Note**: Immunity to disease does not protect you from Hampstead Damnations.

###  **Ways of getting Hampstead Damnation levels \-**

* While in Hampstead, you suffer one level of conditional damnation, that is removed when you are outside the burrough.  
* If you long rest outdoors in Hampstead, you gain one level of long term damnation instead of losing a level.  
* If you breathe the fog you gain one level of long term damnation, but won’t gain additional levels from the fog for 12 hours.  
* A natural weapon attack (such as a bite or claw, but this does not include things like punches) from a Hampstead vampire causes one level of long term damnation, but you will not gain more levels from vampires in this fashion for the next 12 hours.  
* Drinking Hampstead vampire blood causes two levels of long term damnation, as long as it has not been cooked.  Once you gain through drinking blood, you will not gain more from drinking blood for the next 12 hours  
* A natural weapon attack (such as a bite or claw, but this does not include things like punches) from a Hampstead non wolf lycanthrope causes one level of long term damnation, but you will not gain more levels from non wolf lycanthropes in this fashion for the next 12 hours.  
* A natural weapon attack (such as a bite or claw, but this does not include things like punches) from a Hampstead Werewolf causes one level of long term damnation (you are protected from getting this long term damnation from werewolves for 12 hours), and one level of conditional damnation that lasts until the Werewolf is killed (you cannot have more than one conditional level from any one werewolf).  
* Being brought back to life with spells or items (instead of with Science) gives a number of Levels of Damnation equal to spell level.  
* Desecrating a humanoids dead body (for instance carving it up) anywhere in Empire City, participating in this desecration, or profiting from this desecration, will result in one level of Hampstead Damnation for each instance of doing so.  
* Other creatures or effects may specifically cause levels as listed in their description.  Also DM discretion is to be used on any supernatural attack done in Hampstead.  They can decide to make conditional or long term levels happen due to those, but should make sure that if a player gains through a source, they are protected from that source for some time.  This list is supposed to be a slow build, that can take days or even weeks to go up, or solve.

#  **Elective Surgery Risk in Hampstead**

Heore will do elective surgery for players.  Have her inform the player that any form of surgery comes at a risk of infection and death.  Also she charges for the surgery in crafting materials.  If its general surgery, any crafting material will suffice, but if the surgery is to fix a magical or mystical alment, or to implant a device in the player, the cost should be more.  If they die, make sure they know they should be able to be brought back, with all the risks and consequences of being brought back.

Have the player roll a d20 when you perform surgery.  This roll is done with advantage if the player is getting simple elective surgery, or if the player wishes to use inspiration if they have it.  In addition, a DM may deem surgery at a significant risk and impose disadvantage.  Otherwise this roll cannot be modified by any player created source.  The d20 roll must roll above 21-(the character’s Con score).  If the roll is equal to or below this DC, the player expires, and must be bright back as per normal Hampstead Rules.

# **Being Brought Back via Hampstead Science**

*“A new species would bless me as its creator and source; many happy and excellent natures would owe their being to me. No father could claim the gratitude of his child so completely as I should deserve theirs.”*

Reborn cannot be brought back this way.

Through the careful application of the alchemical sciences, Heore has discovered how to bring those that were once alive, back to the land of the living.  Cutting necrotic tissue, exchanging damaged parts to be repaired later, sometimes transferring the brain, or even mind into a new body that is awaiting a host, people can be brought back.  Heore will make sure the person bringing in the body that there is no assurance the person will be brought back with all of their old parts.

Until Heore can stabilize the player with the essence from and crafting material, they must wear a eel battery pack that weighs 14\*Str

DM note, have the player that is being brought back roll on the following table.  Then roleplay clearing the old dead tissue, scrapping much, and replacing it with new.  Then use bottled lightning to reanimate the person.  Heore is known to replace parts that don’t even necessarily need replaced, thus even if they kept their old race, they wouldn't have kept all their old parts.

Help the player adjust their character sheet, and make sure they know that their new body won’t quite feel like themselves.
