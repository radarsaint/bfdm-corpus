Creating a pigeon lord

Wreathed in their feathered friends, these hardy and insightful wanderers are connected to Empire City’s hivemind network of Cranium Pigeons. Robed in patchwork leather coats and earth tone scarves, their mystical connection to the pigeons is as innate as breathing. High upon their roof top roosts, they are the eyes of the city. Quick Build

You can make a Pigeon Lord quickly by following these suggestions. First, Constitution should be your highest ability score, followed by Intelligence.

Class Features

As a Pigeon Lord, you gain the following class features.

Hit Dice: 1d6 per pigeon lord level

Hit Points at 1st Level: 6 +your Constitution modifier

Hit points at Higher Levels: 1d6 (or 4) +your Constitution modifier per pigeon lord level after 1st

Proficiencies

Armor: Light, Medium

Weapons: Simple weapons, guns, longswords, shortswords

Tools: Cobblers tools

Saving Throws: Constitution, Intelligence

Skills:  Take Animal Handling, Then Choose Two from Insight, Perception, Sleight of hand, Stealth, and Survival

Equipment

You start with the following equipment,

any two simple weapons

your choice of studded leather armor or scale mail

thieves’ tools and a dungeoneer’s pack

Spellcasting

A Pigeon Lord should never without bread. These highly mystical creatures require bread to use their spell slots for you in combat. The amount of bread that it takes to cast spells is equivalent to spells that you are requesting them to cast by gently cooing. Feeding pigeons bread brings them to your position and will cause them to cast with the same requirements as a normal spell caster. When you run out of bread (which magically regenerates after a long rest) you can no longer request that they cast for you. Your bread is contained in a pouch and counts as their spell casting focus. It is assumed that you are also tossing them the appropriate material components.

Ability Score Increase

When you reach 4th level, and again at 8th, 12th, 16th and 19th level, you can increase one ability score of your choice by 2, or you can increase two ability scores of your choice by 1. As normal, you can't increase an ability score above 20 using this feature.

Coo Tounge

Through years of isolation and living with your pigeons you have learned their hidden language. An outlandish unimaginable combination of coo’s and chirps that allow you to ask them for the spells and abilities they grant you. Only other pigeon lords or pigeons can understand you while in conversation.

Bread Crumbs

At 2nd level you are able to bribe the pigeons with bread crumbs. These bread crumbs are the key to unlocking the pigeon's hidden potential and their secret power.

Levels 1, 2, and 10 are the next most likely to gain subclass features. Levels 1 and 2 typically only appear in spellcaster subclasses, as they typically choose their subclasses earlier.

Level 7 is unlikely to receive a subclass feature. Only 4 subclass groups grant a feature at this level.

Level 15 is very unlikely to receive subclass features.

Levels 9, 11, 17, and 18, are even less likely to gain features from a subclass.

Levels 5, 8, 13, and 20 pretty much never gain subclass features. Only one class grants a level 20 subclass feature, and this is probably due to most classes giving the capstone feature, rather than deriving it from an older feature. Level 8 is ability score increase for most classes, so it makes sense to shy away from adding features at this level.

Levels 4, 12, 16, and 19 have never received subclass features from any class. These are where most classes receive their ability increases, so it makes sense that most features would be considered overpowered when paired with this

Pigeon Flocks

The Flock of Names

Me and a Pigeon named Steve.

Starting at 3rd level, you become owned by a pigeon named Steve. The pigeon assumes a bond with your left shoulder. Steve has the game statistics of a canary, and it always thinks of your commands as it’s own idea which you can give directly through your innate connection with it while it is within 100 feet of you. Steve has a fly speed of 30.

While Steve is perched on your shoulder, you gain +1 to ac and a bonus to your passive Charisma (Perception) score and to Wisdom (Animal handling) checks. The bonus equals your Charisma modifier. While perched on your shoulder, the Steve can’t be targeted by any attack or other harmful effect; only you can cast spells on it; it can’t take damage; and it is incapacitated.

Steve’s can see through your eyes and hear what you hear while it is within 100 feet of you. As a courtesy, he will allow you to see and hear what he sees and hears too. In combat, Steve always goes after your turn. Again, it assumes that your thoughts are it’s own idea and will act accordingly. If it is slain by a creature, you gain advantage on all attack rolls against the killer for the next 24 hours. Long live Steve.

Steve doesn’t require sleep. Steve does require cuddles while in private. While it is within 100 feet of you, it can awaken you from sleep. If it senses danger, roll a d20. On an 8 or higher to alert you to danger as a bonus action. On a 7 or lower it will awaken you to alert you to Steve’s need for more bread.

Steve explodes when it dies, if you die, or if the two of you are separated for more than 15 minutes. If Steve dies, Steve will assume Steve’s old position as your owner and land on your shoulder after a long rest.

Me and a Pigeon named Marv.

Starting at 6th level you may expend X use of your bread as a bonus action to summon Steve’s scrawnier cousin Marv. Marv coo’s are far more soothing than his mangled appearance would leave a spectator to assume. Marv’s coos grant itself and each creature of it’s choice within 10 feet of it a number of temporary hit points equal to 1d8 + your Constitution modifier (minimum of +1). The number you rolled is the number of pigeons who land on the target receiving hit points. They will take the amount of damage represented. Marv will continue to sing the song of his people for 10 minutes. Marv has a fly speed of 40.

Your time spent cooing has caused you to develop a masterful command of the song of Marv’s people, enhancing the healing and damage you create through him. Whenever you spend bread to cast a spell you may spend an additional usage of bread to gain two bonus dice to your roll of the spell’s damage. That roll must restore hit points or be a damage roll. The damage type dealt by the spell becomes your choice of thunder(flap flap flap), psychic (pigeons be crazy), necrotic (pigeons be gross), or poison (pigeons be dirty) damage.

Me and Bad Bertha

Starting at fourth level, in pigeon society, you have become as iconic as one of the many statues of Empire City. By expending X uses of your bread as a bonus action, Bad Bertha can be tempted to claim your head as her roost. When Bertha claims your head as her roost, she will summon a mighty flock to her aid. Her flock can target any creature you can see with a range of 80 feet. As an action you can request in coo tongue that her flock hinder your mutual foe. To request that she hinder more foes, more uses of bread may be given on subsequent turns. Each target must succeed on a Wisdom (your spell save DC plus con mod) saving throw or be charmed by you until the spell ends, or until you or your companions do anything harmful to it which will scatter the flock. While charmed in this way, a creature can do nothing but use its movement to approach you in a safe manner. While an affected creature is within 5 feet of you, it cannot move, but simply stares at Bad Bertha in wonder. At the end of each of its turns, an affected target can make a Wisdom saving throw. If it succeeds, this effect ends for that target. While under this effect, the creature’s move speed is reduced by 10.

Wall of pigeons.

A nonmagical wall of blood thirsty pigeons coaches at a point you and Bad Bertha choose within range. The wall is 5 feet and ten feet high, and extends up to 80 feet in length.

If the wall cuts through a creature's space when it appears, the creature is pushed to one side of the wall (your choice). If a creature would be surrounded on all sides by the wall (or the wall and another solid surface), that creature can make a Dexterity saving throw. On a success, it can use its reaction to move up to its speed so that it is no longer enclosed as they rapidly descend.

The wall can have any shape you desire, though it can't occupy the same space as a creature or object. The wall appears in any orientation you choose: horizontally, vertically, or diagonally. It can be free floating, or it can rest on a solid surface.

The wall is an object made of pigeons that can be damaged and thus breached. Each 5 foot section has AC 15 and 100 hit points. Reducing a 5 foot section of the pigeons to 0 hit points destroys it.

The wall blocks line of sight, but creatures and objects can attempt to pass through it. A creature entering the wall treats it as difficult terrain, and must pass a con save or take 3d10 damage and be blinded.
