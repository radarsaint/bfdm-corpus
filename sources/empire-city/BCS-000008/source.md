Slothful Redcoats Assault Innocents 
~An Eyewitness' Account Through Her Familiar~


In the early hours of the morning on Hampstead's streets, two Redcoat guards were witnessed in disarray - carelessly asleep, an easy target for thieves. And there was one - an opportunist who, despite the ease of stealing from innocent bystanders, targeted instead the indolent Redcoats, taking weapons and armour from them. The writer believes the 'thief' is truly a protector of the city, taking from the indolent Redcoats to give to those who truly intend to help the people.


A war veteran on a morning walk noticed the commotion, and - naturally, if inadvisedly - awoke the Redcoats, intending to talk to their commander about their lack of care. However - how did they respond? They try arresting the poor veteran on unreasonable charges, and would have succeeded if not for the intervention of a good citizen - a citizen who, like the writer, had a bounty on his head, maligned by the Redcoats for defending himself. This brave citizen, despite the risk, made his presence known to allow the veteran time to escape.
The Redcoats took the bait, as the citizen led them on a chase throughout Hampstead and the veteran got away, to where the writer was letting others know of the situation. The writer's familiar was in the area in order to promote the free flow of information, and so was asked to follow. The citizen was chased into a dead-end by the Redcoats, who started to brutally assault him. Of course, he fought back in self-defense, but his main goal was to escape - and so he would have, had he not been grabbed by one of the Redcoats.


In the pursuit of freedom, the writer's familiar lunged at the Redcoat holding the citizen, and was brutally torn apart by them. The last thing the writer saw through her familiar's dying eyes was the manic grin on the Redcoat's face. With the last of her strength as the loss washed over her, she told others the location of the fight; the veteran, wanting to rescue the citizen who had helped him, rushed off to assist.


While the writer does not know the exact details from this point onwards, two things can be said: fortunately, the veteran and citizen returned safely, and they - not the Redcoats - should be treated as heroes of the people.
