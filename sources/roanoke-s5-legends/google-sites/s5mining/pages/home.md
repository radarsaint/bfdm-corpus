Life Path: Miner

Life Path is in addition to your background. It does not replace your background. This choice has major economic impacts for your character. There is no starting cash or equipment outside of this choice.

As the sun rises over the rugged terrain of the Wild West, you find yourself on the precipice of an exciting new adventure. With a sturdy pickaxe slung over your shoulder and a glimmer of determination in your eyes, you stand among a group of aspiring miners ready to stake their claims and delve into the depths of the earth. The City of Legend, a bustling hub of opportunities and untold riches, beckons you from afar.

Word has spread among the mining community of a promising location deep within the wilderness, accessible only by navigating the treacherous river that winds its way through the rugged landscape. The legends speak of a hidden valley rumored to be teeming with precious ores and gemstones waiting to be discovered. To reach this untapped treasure trove, you and your fellow miners must embark on a thrilling river journey, utilizing rafts to transport yourselves and your gear downstream.

As you step aboard your sturdy raft, the river's currents carry you through a mesmerizing landscape. Towering cliffs rise on either side, their imposing presence serving as a reminder of the untamed nature that awaits. The rushing waters propel your raft forward, each twist and turn filling you with a mix of anticipation and exhilaration. Your grip tightens on the paddle as you navigate the occasional rapids, feeling the spray of water against your face, a thrilling reminder of the risks involved in your chosen path.

Along the riverbanks, the wilderness unfolds in all its majestic glory. Lush forests, rocky outcrops, and hidden caves pass by in a blur, each holding secrets and potential riches within. The call of wildlife echoes through the air, while the occasional glint of sunlight off the water hints at the hidden treasures beneath its surface.

After a day of navigating the river's twists and turns, you finally reach your destination: a tranquil stretch of river near the City of Legend, where rumors suggest a rich vein of precious minerals lies waiting to be discovered. The sight of fellow prospectors, their own rafts lined up along the riverbank, fills you with a sense of camaraderie and competition.

With your raft safely moored, you step onto the riverbank, ready to stake your claim and begin your journey as a miner in this vast and promising land. The City of Legend lies just beyond, promising a bustling marketplace where you can sell your finds, forge alliances, and delve deeper into the mysteries that this untamed frontier has to offer.

As you set foot on solid ground, your heart brimming with excitement and determination, you know that your path as a miner is just beginning. The risks are great, but the rewards can be immeasurable. With each swing of your pickaxe, you hope to unearth not only precious metals and gemstones but also the legends and stories that have shaped this wild and wondrous land.

The journey ahead may be challenging, but the lure of adventure and the promise of wealth drive you forward. The City of Legend awaits, and the path to becoming a legendary miner is laid out before you. It's time to stake your claim and make your mark on this untamed frontier.

All miners automatically gain the ability to Pick Mine.

Pick Mining:

Pick mining is a manual and labor-intensive method of mining that involves using a sturdy pickaxe to extract minerals from rock formations. This technique requires strength, endurance, and careful precision. Here are the rules for pick mining:

Mechanics:

Location:

The player chooses a suitable rock formation or mine face where they suspect valuable minerals are present.

Roll:

The player makes a skill check using their Mining Tools proficiency. The DC for the check depends on the hardness of the rock and the richness of the mineral deposit. The Dungeon Master determines the DC based on the specific circumstances.

Outcome:

Failed Check:

The miner spends the day pick mining but fails to extract any significant amount of minerals.

Success:

The miner successfully extracts a moderate amount of valuable minerals, earning 2d6 gold pieces or 2d8 silver pieces.

Critical Success: The miner strikes a particularly rich vein or uncovers a hidden gemstone, greatly increasing their yield. They earn an additional 3d6 gold Nuggets.

Fatigue: Pick mining is physically demanding work. After a full day of pick mining, the player must make a Constitution saving throw (DC begins at 12 and increase by 2 for each day you pick mine) to avoid exhaustion. Failure results in gaining one level of exhaustion, which can negatively impact future mining attempts and other activities.

Skill Proficiencies: Athletics, Investigation

Mining Proficiency:

As a Miner, you have honed your skills in extracting valuable resources from the earth. You have advantage on ability checks related to mining, such as identifying mineral deposits, operating mining equipment, and navigating underground tunnels.

Gold Income:

Your expertise in mining allows you to generate a steady income from your endeavors. Each day, you generate 2 gp worth of valuable minerals or precious metals, reflecting your successful mining operations. This income is automatically converted into gold pieces.

Comfortable Furnishings:

With your reliable income, you can afford to live a comfortable lifestyle. You automatically spend 2 gp per day on comfortable furnishings, enhancing your living conditions and providing a sense of stability wherever you go.

Mining Techniques:

As a skilled Miner, you are proficient in various mining techniques, allowing you to adapt to different mining environments and maximize your yield. You gain the ability's "Pick mining" and can choose one of the following mining techniques (the rest can be learned

during down time)

Panning:

Description: Panning is a basic and traditional mining technique that involves sifting through gravel and sediment in search of valuable minerals, particularly gold nuggets.

Mechanics: To pan for gold, the miner selects a suitable location near a water source, fills a shallow pan with gravel and sediment, and gently swirls it in the water. The player makes a luck check check with no skills or proficiency and with out the benefits of any other means of manipulating the results of a roll. Roll 1d20. An 11 or greater is a passed check.

Results:

Failed Check:

The miner spends the day panning but finds no significant amount of gold or silver.

Success:

The miner finds a modest amount of gold or silver, earning 1d4 gold nuggets

Critical Success:

The miner strikes it lucky, discovering a rare find. They earn an additional 1d10 gold pieces

Risk:

There is a low chance (5% chance, roll 1d20) of encountering hazards like an aggressive animal, minor injuries, or a rival prospector attempting to steal the findings.

Sluicing:

Description:

You are adept at using water to separate valuable materials from sediment or gravel. When using the sluicing technique, your mining income has a chance to be increased by 50%. However, this technique requires access to a water source, requires a large amount of digging (one day's worth of set up) and may attract unwanted attention from thieves or competitors. It is best to use this as a passive form of income while in a town.

Mechanics:

The miner sets up a sluice box along a riverbank or stream, creating a channel for water to flow through. They shovel dirt and gravel into the sluice box and use water to wash away the lighter materials, leaving behind the heavier minerals. The player makes a luck check check with no skills or proficiency and with out the benefits of any other means of manipulating the results of a roll.

Failed Check:

(10 or Less): The miner spends the day sluicing but finds no significant amount of gold or silver.

Success:

(11 or greater): The miner collects a decent amount of gold or silver, earning 2d12 gold nuggets.

Critical Success:

(Nat 20): The miner discovers a rich deposit, significantly increasing their yield. They earn an additional 4d12 gold nuggets and 3d6 silver nuggets.

Critical Failure (Nat 1):

There is a low chance (5% chance, roll 1d20) of encountering hazards like collapsing riverbanks, water-related accidents, or encountering territorial creatures.

Rockerbox:

You excel at using a rockerbox, a portable mining tool, to process soil and extract valuable minerals. When using the rockerbox technique, you have a higher chance of finding rare minerals or gems. However, this technique requires time and patience, and it generates a slightly lower income compared to other techniques. Rockerboxes can be crafted for free (you may only own and/or use one at a time.)

Mechanics:

The miner sets up a rockerbox, which resembles a cradle, and loads it with gravel and sand. They rock the device back and forth, allowing the gold to settle and be collected in riffles. The player makes a luck check check with no skills or proficiency and with out the benefits of any other means of manipulating the results of a roll. Roll 1d20. An 11 or greater is a passed check.

Results:

Failed Check (10 or Less):

The miner spends the day using the rockerbox but finds no significant amount of gold or silver.

Success (11 or greater):

The miner successfully extracts gold, earning 2d6 gold nuggets.

Critical Success (Nat 20):

The miner strikes a particularly rich vein, greatly increasing their haul. They also find one minor gem worth 10 gold.

Critical Failure (Nat 1):

There is a low chance (5% chance, roll 1d20) of encountering hazards like equipment malfunctions, exhaustion, or collapsing mine shafts.

Blast Mining:

You are skilled in the art of controlled explosions to extract minerals from the earth. When using the blast mining technique, you have the potential to uncover rich mineral veins quickly. However, this technique is more volatile and carries the risk of causing cave-ins or damaging the surrounding environment. The income generated from blast mining can be higher than other techniques, but it may also attract attention from authorities or rival mining operations. Critical failure examples for river mining:

Poisonous Critter:

While sifting through the sediment, the miner disturbs a venomous snake, scorpion, or other poisonous creature. The miner is bitten or stung, suffering from poison damage and experiencing debilitating effects until cured.

Rockslide:

The miner's vigorous panning causes a small rockslide from the nearby cliff or riverbank, burying them partially or fully under debris. They take bludgeoning damage and must make a Strength check to free themselves.

Quicksand:

Unbeknownst to the miner, they step into an area of quicksand while panning near a marsh or bog. They start sinking, requiring assistance from their companions or successful skill checks to escape its grasp.

Unstable Ground:

The ground beneath the miner's feet gives way unexpectedly, causing them to fall into a concealed sinkhole or underground cavity. They take falling damage and must navigate their way back to safety.

Lost Supplies:

During the panning process, the miner accidentally drops or loses a valuable item or piece of equipment, such as a valuable gem, a crucial tool, or a map. The item becomes irretrievable or is washed away by the current.

False Hope:

The miner mistakenly believes they have struck a significant gold or silver deposit, investing valuable time and resources in digging and excavating. However, it turns out to be a fool's gold or another mineral of little value, resulting in wasted efforts and disappointment.

Here is the table for negative effects for failing at blast mining.

Accidental Detonation:

The explosive charge goes off prematurely, causing damage to the miner and their surroundings. The miner takes 2d6 points of fire damage and may suffer from temporary deafness due to the blast.

Cave-In:

The force of the blast causes a section of the mine or tunnel to collapse, trapping the miner inside. They must make Strength or Dexterity saving throws (DC determined by the Dungeon Master) to escape, taking 2d8 bludgeoning damage if they fail.

Toxic Fumes:

The blast uncovers a hidden pocket of toxic gases, which quickly fill the mining area. The miner must make a Constitution saving throw (DC determined by the Dungeon Master) or be poisoned, suffering from ongoing poison damage until cured.

Unstable Shaft:

The explosive force destabilizes the mine shaft, causing it to become structurally unsound. The miner must make Dexterity saving throws (DC determined by the Dungeon Master) to avoid falling debris or collapsing platforms, taking 2d8 bludgeoning damage if they fail.

Rival Prospectors:

The loud blast attracts the attention of rival prospectors or a group of bandits who see an opportunity to seize the miner's valuable findings. The miner must deal with an unexpected encounter, possibly leading to a confrontation or negotiation.

Creature Encounter:

The noise and vibrations from the blast disturb the den of a dangerous underground creature. The miner must face off against a hostile creature, such as a territorial basilisk, an enraged earth elemental, or a group of aggressive dire rats.

Contaminated Minerals:

The force of the explosion mixes the valuable minerals with worthless impurities, significantly reducing their overall value. The miner's earnings from the blast mining are reduced by 50% for this particular excavation.

Cave Network Collapse:

The blast triggers a cascading collapse throughout the interconnected cave network. The miner becomes disoriented and lost, having to navigate through treacherous and unfamiliar tunnels to find their way back, possibly encountering additional hazards or wandering monsters.
