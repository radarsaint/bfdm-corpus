Marker Chips are more than just money. These poker chip icons are a social currency and effect game mechanics. Only a gambler may leave a gambling hall with poker chips to use as Marker Chips. Once a Marker has been spent, its magic is depleted and is worthless.

Overview:

Reputation points:

These represent the standing and recognition earned by gamblers in the River's Edge Poker League. They serve as a measure of a gambler's skill, success, and notoriety within the gambling community. These points reflect the gambler's ability to navigate the high-stakes games, win against formidable opponents, and uphold their honor and integrity at the poker table. Accumulating reputation points signifies the gambler's progression through the ranks, unlocking access to higher-tier games, exclusive opportunities, and the respect and admiration of their peers. Reputation points serve as a tangible representation of a gambler's journey, marking their ascent from novice players to legendary high rollers in the world of gambling. Rep must be earned, not gifted. Gaining chips from IOUs, pickpocketing, gifts, drunken declarations of love etc do not count toward your progression to the next tier of gaming.

Tiered System:

The markers are organized into five tiers, each representing a different level of gambling prestige and risk. As players progress through the tiers, the value and rarity of the markers increase.

Betting Structure:

Each tier has its own betting structure, determining the level of the game and the type of opponents and events players will encounter. Higher tiers feature grand tournaments and exclusive competitions.

Marker Generation:

Markers are generated when you give poker chips in lieu of other forms of payment. Shops don't accept these. But humanoids that are friendsly to you will.

Trade-offs and Benefits:

Each tier has its own unique set of benefits and trade-offs associated with the IOU Markers. Higher-tier markers provide more powerful advantages but also impose more significant drawbacks or limitations on the gambler. While benefits cannot stack, the negative trade off effects do.

Gambler

:

The Gambler is the player character who possesses and wields the Poker Chips. They are the one engaging in games of chance, placing bets, and using the Poker Chips to gain advantages and benefits. The Gambler relies on their skill, luck, and strategic choices to win games and accumulate wealth or valuable markers.

Bearer

:

The Bearer is the individual or entity to whom the Gambler owes a favor or debt. When the Gambler gives their Poker Chip to the Bearer, the Bearer becomes the recipient of the marker's abilities. The Bearer can call upon the powers or benefits associated with the specific Poker Chip they hold, utilizing them to their advantage.

Welcome to the prestigious River's Edge Poker League, where the thrill of high-stakes gambling awaits! As a gambler, you will navigate through various tiers of games, each offering unique challenges and opportunities to amass reputation points. This guide will walk you through the mechanics of the Poker Chip system, progression between tiers, and the exciting rewards that await you.

The Tiers:

1. Common Tier:

- Players start their journey here, participating in common-level games, and with the ability to turn their poker chips into an IOU that can be used by other players (called bearers in this document) in combat or other non-gambling related adventures.

- Games in this tier feature Tier 1: Silver Dime Chips.

- Each Marker Chip obtained represents 1 reputation point.

- Participating in a Common game earns reputation points equivalent to the number of Marker Chips earned.

- Winning a Common game grants 2 Marker Chips plus 1 additional Marker Chip for each player with the Gambler life path.

2. Uncommon Tier:

- To enter the Uncommon Tier, players must accumulate 10 reputation points in the form of 10 Silver Dime Chips from the Common Tier.

- Games in this tier feature Tier 2: Double Dime Marker Chips.

- Each Marker Chip obtained represents 2 reputation points.

- Participating in an Uncommon game earns reputation points equivalent to twice the number of Marker Chips earned.

- Winning an Uncommon game grants 3 Marker Chips plus 1 additional Marker Chip for each player with the Gambler life path.

3. Rare Tier:

- Players can ascend to the Rare Tier by accumulating 25 reputation points from the Common and Uncommon Tiers.

- Games in this tier feature Tier 3: High Roller Marker Chips.

- Each Marker Chip obtained represents 5 reputation points.

- Participating in a Rare game earns reputation points equivalent to five times the number of Marker Chips earned.

- Winning a Rare game grants 4 Marker Chips plus 1 additional Marker Chip for each player with the Gambler life path.

4. Very Rare Tier:

- Players can enter the Very Rare Tier by accumulating 60 reputation points in the Common, Uncommon, and Rare Tiers.

- Games in this tier feature Tier 4: Prestige Marker Chips.

- Each Marker Chip obtained represents 10 reputation points.

- Participating in a Very Rare game earns reputation points equivalent to ten times the number of Marker Chips earned.

- Winning a Very Rare game grants 5 Marker Chips plus 1 additional Marker Chip for each player with the Gambler life path.

5. Legendary Tier:

- The pinnacle of gambling prowess, the Legendary Tier awaits those who amass 100 reputation points from all previous tiers.

- Games in this tier feature Tier 5: Fortune Chips.

- Each Fortune Chip obtained represents 20 reputation points.

- Participating in a Legendary game earns reputation points equivalent to twenty times the number of Fortune Chips earned.

- Winning a Legendary game grants 6 Marker Chips plus 1 additional Marker Chip for each player with the Gambler life path.

**Progression and Reputation Points:**

- Players accumulate reputation points by obtaining Marker Chips and winning games.

- Each tier has specific reputation point requirements to progress to the next tier.

- The number of reputation points earned is determined by the tier of the game and the value of the Marker Chips obtained.

- Marker Chips given away or transferred to other players do not count towards progression to the next tier.

- Once a tier has been unlocked, it remains unlocked unless a Dungeon Master decides otherwise.

Set up for "River's Edge Showdown" begins by gathering a group of players ready to test their wits and cunning in a high-stakes game of deception. The dealer for the first round is determined, through rolling for initiative. Each player starts with an equal amount of money determined by the players. This is an intense game of skill and deception. As the players gather around the table, the atmosphere is thick with tension and anticipation. The deck consists of four suits, each representing a unique aspect of this dark fantasy old west-themed game.

To determine the cards each player holds, the game begins with a roll for initiative. In the order rolled, players attempt to gain two cards of their choice by manipulating the dealer through contested skill check combos. Players can assume an attacking stance or a defending stance, utilizing various skills such as Sleight of Hand, Perception, Deception, Insight, Stealth, Performance, Persuasion, and Investigation. Successful contested rolls allow players to select their desired cards, while failed attempts result in two random cards dealt face down.

Once the card acquisition phase is complete, the first player in initiative becomes the big blind and pays the ante. The game then proceeds through several phases:

Phase 1: Cheating

Players who successfully acquired their desired cards during the initiative round are aware of the cards they selected, while those who failed are unaware. The players can choose to attack or defend by indicating their target player.

Attackers make contested rolls against their targets using the chosen skill check combos. Successful attacks grant the attackers various options such as stealing a card from the defender, forcing the defender to replace a known card with a randomly generated one, casting doubt, intimidation, or confusion upon the defender, imposing a -2 penalty to their future cheating or defending checks.

Phase 2: Communal Cards

Three communal cards are revealed. Roll a 1d4 to determine the suit of the communal cards: Shadows (♠), Vipers (♥), Ghosts (♦), or Ravens (♣). Roll a 3d13 to determine the value of each card.

Phase 3: Betting

Players, in initiative order, can choose to bet, fold, or check based on their hand strength and strategic considerations.

Phase 4: Individual Card Reveal

Players reveal one card from their hand. If a player is unaware of their card, generate one using the same suit and value generation rules as the communal cards.

Phase 5: Betting Continues

Players, in initiative order, can bet, fold, or check based on their updated hand strength and the revealed communal and individual cards.

Phase 6: Final Showdown

In this final phase, attackers won't have to give up their hidden cards. Instead, they'll concentrate their attack on either the communal cards

or

their opponents' undisclosed cards.

As an aside:

Remember, the communal cards are those that everyone can see, while undisclosed cards are those that players have not revealed yet.

For attackers, if you choose to attack the communal cards, you're tryin' to manipulate 'em to benefit your hand or disrupt your opponents. A successful attack on the communal cards might swap one of 'em with a new one from the deck or remove a specific communal card from the table.

If you opt to attack your opponents' undisclosed cards, you'll try to affect their hands in some way, maybe force 'em to swap one of their hidden cards with a new one or even steal a specific undisclosed card if your skill check succeeds.

As for defenders, you'll be guardin' against the attackers' attempts to change the communal cards or affect your hand. If you defend successfully, you'll prevent the attacker from making any changes to the communal cards or your hidden cards.
