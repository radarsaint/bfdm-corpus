Spellshot Wizard.

School of the Spellshot

Spellshots are wizards who have mastered the art of blending spellcasting with firearm combat. They specialize in customizing their firearms, imbuing them with arcane runes and glyphs to unleash potent spell effects through their ammunition. Each gun used by a Spellshot is unique, reflecting their mastery over the fusion of magic and firepower.

Runed Tinkering

Starting at 2nd level, you gain proficiency with Tinker's Tools. Additionally, you can modify your firearms to harness the power of spellcasting. To do so, you must spend an hour working on a firearm and make a DC 10 Tinker's Tools check (DC 15 for magic firearms) to add the necessary runes and glyphs. If you roll a natural 1 on the check, the firearm suffers disadvantage until you succeed on a DC 20 Tinker's Tools check to repair it. Once you modify or repair a firearm, you cannot do so again until you finish a long rest. The modified firearm becomes a Runed Firearm, usable only by you.

Spellshot

At 2nd level, you gain proficiency with firearms, and your runed firearm serves as a spellcasting focus for your wizard spells. You can use your Dexterity modifier for any spell you cast using your runed firearm.

As a bonus action on your turn, you can ensorcell a bullet, infusing it with a wizard spell you have prepared. The spell must have a material component cost of less than 10 gp and a casting time of less than 1 minute. These enchanted bullets lose their magic at the beginning of your next turn.

On your turn, you can use your action to load one of the ensorcelled bullets into your runed firearm and fire it at a target you can see. Firing the bullet does not count as casting a spell, and it does not require verbal or material components. However, it does require somatic components involved in loading and firing the shot. Each shot is thunderous and loud due to the modifications you made to the gun and bullets.

When you fire an ensorcelled bullet, make a ranged spell attack (using Dexterity) with the firearm up to its short range. This attack ignores disadvantage when the target is within 5 feet of you. If the attack hits, you deal damage based on the firearm's damage, and the spell contained in the bullet activates with the target as its primary target. If the spell requires an attack roll, use the attack roll from the initial attack instead.

The point of origin for the spell must be the primary target fired at, even if it is a creature, object, or terrain feature. If the spell does not have a primary target, the creature or object fired at becomes the primary target, and if possible, it must be used as one of the spell's targets.

You can only fire one ensorcelled bullet per turn, and each bullet can only be cast with one of your runed firearms.

Arcane Wake

Beginning at 6th level, when you fire a damaging spell of 1st level or higher from your Runed Firearm, the spell creates an arcane wake that follows up and hits the target. The spell deals an additional +2 damage per level of the spell slot used to the primary target of the spell.

You can use this feature a number of times equal to your proficiency modifier. All uses are regained after a long rest.

Extended Range

At 10th level, your proficiency with Runed Firearms allows you to fire ensorcelled bullets up to the firearm's long range. However, any bullet fired beyond the short range granted by the Spellshot feature suffers disadvantage on attack rolls, and the first saving throw made by each target to resist this effect is made with advantage.

Arcane Cavitation

Upon reaching 14th level, you gain the ability to collapse the arcane wake created by an ensorcelled bullet fired from your runed firearm. After resolving the spell's effect, you can have the primary target of your spellshot take 1d6 force damage per level of the spell slot used.

You can use this feature once, and you regain its use after a short or long rest.
