Longevity is an important factor when it comes to improvised weapons. These weapons are not built to last, as they are quickly scrounged together during combat or hobbled together over a short period of time. The following rules determine the durability and lifespan of improvised weapons:

Scrounged Weapons:

If you find or quickly assemble an improvised weapon during combat as a bonus action, it remains viable for 1d4 actions. These weapons are hastily put together and have a limited lifespan.

Hobbled Together Weapons:

If you take at least 10 minutes to carefully construct or modify an improvised weapon, it becomes more durable. A weapon hobbled together in this manner remains viable for 2d4+4 actions. The extra time spent ensures a more functional and sturdy weapon.

Breakage or Unviability:

An improvised weapon breaks or becomes unviable once its uses are expended or after one hour, whichever comes first. These weapons are not built to withstand prolonged use, and their makeshift nature eventually takes its toll.

Remember, improvised weapons are a temporary solution born out of necessity. They offer a resourceful means of fighting, but their inherent fragility means you must be prepared to scrounge or assemble new weapons as needed.

Scavenging Rule:

As a bonus action on their turn, a player can attempt to scrounge for improvised ammunition for their weapon.

Ammunition Quantity:

When the player scrounges for ammunition, they roll a d6 to determine the quantity of ammunition found. The result of the roll indicates how many units of ammunition the player obtains, ranging from 1 to 6.

Reloading:

The player can reload their improvised weapon with the amount of ammunition obtained from the scrounging roll. This allows them to continue using their weapon.

Limited Ammunition:

The scrounged ammunition is limited and will eventually be used up. Once the player has depleted all the scrounged ammunition, they must find a way to improvise another weapon.
