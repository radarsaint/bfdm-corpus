Crafting with Class
(work in progress)
(highlighted portions are subject to alteration)


Abstract:
This document outlines a crafting system for Roanoke Season 3. The intent is to create a crafting system that allows players to craft items for use in the game, that is engaging, easy to understand, encourages interaction, requires minimum bookkeeping, is balanced, not susceptible to exploiting, and supports the main story. Division of labor will be broken into three classes, Serfs, Merchants, and Aristocrats. All three have advantages and disadvantages and must work together to create gear.

Crafting Example:
(Anastasia the aristocrat would like to craft a +1 shortsword). 
1. She has 10,000 gold worth of resources to trade that she has brought with her to the new world. 
2. Looking in the Sane Magic Items List, she sees that a +1 weapon costs 1000 gold. 
3. She approaches the village bladesmith, Maurice the merchant. 
4. Maurice informs her that she must pay 1000 gold and 2 days of labor (every 500 gold of costs takes a day of labor).
*  Maurice knows that he is providing this service at cost, but that doesn’t bother him, as he thinks it will be handy to get on good terms with the Aristocrat. Anastasia makes her way to the tavern and asks if there are any serfs looking for work. One crusty looking serf named Sedrick says he will help, for the right price. 


1. He asks for a payment of 500 gold, and Anastasia, not wanting to delay, agrees to the price. 
2. Anastasia pays Maurice up front for materials, and agrees to pay Sedrick once the work is complete. 
3. Using the Crafting Tracker, Sedrick schedules to work for the next 2 days at Maurice’s forge under close supervision. 
4.  After the 2 days have passed, the work is complete and the sword is made. 


There are three parts to crafting any object.
1. Money - Everything takes money to craft. Money is an abstract simplification of all the sundry items and consumable resources used to craft any object. Nobility comes to the new world flush with cash, but no practical skills to make their own things. As time goes on, Merchants, and even Serfs may be able to acquire money and shut out the Aristocrats, or even become an Aristocrat.


2. Workshops - Merchants bring their knowledge and expertise to bear. Only Merchants have the specialized knowledge and equipment needed to craft amazing items. They need to rely on others to do all the labor of crafting.


3. Labor - Labor makes the world go round. While not glamorous, it is absolutely necessary for any crafting. Any Serf can perform labor. Serfs were plentiful in the old world, but here, labor is always in short supply, and people may find they can command a high price for their labor. Be aware of idle hands however. Unassigned labor is wasted, and only working with a skilled merchant can produce quality results. 


Nobility:
* Aristocrats of the Old World bring what wealth they can with them, and start with a sizable amount of gold. In addition, they bring civilization, rule of law, and a certain sophistication not found among the serfs or merchants. Only Aristocrats may own land, being granted a charter from the king. They are able to levy rents and taxes against those who wish to use this land. A wise Aristocrat can enjoy growing wealth, while a foolish one may soon be forced to beg for his daily bread, or even worse, work for it.[f]
   * * Aristocrats are granted an estate registered with the king and approved by the town council. There are strict zoning regulations because protected land is scarce. Only 1 merchant may operate effectively on each parcel of land. As the town grows, more parcels may become available. Aristocrats may come to any agreement they like with merchants. After merchants have been established, it is very difficult to tear down their structure and build a new facility, so it is better to acquire more land. An established merchant is effectively permanent.[g]  Will this work since we will be moving around a lot more this season? Will there be a feel of establishment in an area? Merchants:
        Merchants are the heart of crafting. They can make amazing items, but can’t do it on their own. They need someone to bankroll each recipe, and they need laborers to do the work. In addition, a permit is required. Permits can be granted by the Aristocracy,[h] and help ensure that the business does not interfere with the king’s interests. It takes 1000 gold worth of resources to get set up. This cost can be paid by anyone, but is often paid by the patron aristocrat. Each merchant can come to their own agreement with their Aristocrat for securing a permit, but they can not craft on their own. The penalty for such an act is to have their goods and equipment seized and be thrown into jail.
        Merchants without a patron can still participate, however. Each day they can produce 2 mundane items valued at 50 gold or less each. These items must be appropriate to their profession, and can be put up for sale at the town market. 
        There are five main types of merchants:[i][j]
Weapons - If it does damage, this merchant can make it. Professions include: Bladesmith, Bowyer
Armor - Cobbler, Dress Maker, Forger, Furrier, Friseur, Girdler, Haberdasher, Mercer, Nobthatcher
Magic Items - Carpenter, Cooper, Goldsmith, Mason, Milliner, Schrimpschonger
Potions - Alchemist, Allspice, Apothecary, Aquavitalist, Bee Master, Cheesemonger, Sawbones, Keeper, Vintner
Service - These merchants specialize in valuable services. Each is unique. Professions include: Arbiter, Barber, Beadle, ClodHopper, Berner, Pettifogger, Waymaker

Resources:
In Roanoke, there is a barter system. When you gain resources, it’s assumed that you will trade among the villagers and town stores to get exactly the materials you need for your craft. To simplify tracking resources, we are officially tracking them by their value. For example, if you find a gem worth 100 gold and a rare herb worth 100 gold, you can use either to craft a sword. It’s assumed you bartered with others until you acquired the proper materials for your crafting.

Aristocracy Class:
This island is now owned by King George, and all profits are subject to tax. As representatives of the crown, it is your solemn responsibility to keep an ordered society. You are now the lawful rulers of all inhabitants, both settlers and native. It is your responsibility to see that they contribute to the efforts of the crown.


Running the town is costly, as there are always expenses to be paid. If certain costs cannot be paid, then mass debuffs are put on the whole population (excluding the aristocracy). After enough debuffs, the people may take matters into their own hands.


A cost of 5000 gold per day is required to pay for all the little needs of the colony. These included clean latrines, sleeping quarters, providing food for everyone, patrolling the perimeter, etc. For each day that the amount is not paid, the common people gain one level of exhaustion, up to the fourth level.


The governor is responsible to pay this cost. They must pay this cost by posting in the “Town Upkeep Room”. If there is no response to the previous day’s comment by the governor, or their deputy, then one level of exhaustion is placed on all non-aristocracy. Each day the fee is paid, one level of exhaustion is removed from the population.


In addition to this baseline cost, additional cost of money or labor may be required as events unfold.


(The point of this is to give the Aristocracy a problem they have to solve. Even if they pool their money, they will be broke very quickly. Assuming 12 aristocrats, they would all be completely broke in 24 days. If they want to do any crafting, they will run out for sure. This is assuming they all work together. Only the governor is responsible for paying this cost. So they could levy taxes against the people, or organize expeditions to impose taxes on the natives and take their resources. It’s up to them.)
