BFDM’s Note:
Welcome to our newly reworked Crafting system, Crafting with Class.
Our crafting system, like most of the work that has been put in the game, is designed around cooperative play. It can also serve to introduce some key components of how our play-by-post system runs in-between Dungeon Master hosted events. Crafting begins on day 15 out of 35 in our journey. Despite its late introduction, it is a critical part of the experience we are creating.


This year, our game incorporates a flexible and voluntary crafting system with a caste based class system. There are three castes: Serfs, Merchants, and Aristocrats. All three have advantages and disadvantages and must work together to create gear.


By breaking it up into three castes we are able to allow players to signal to one another how much they intend to be a part of the daily village life of Roanoke. Daily down time is an important component of the game. To some, it is absolutely the most important part. The Caste system can also be seen as a story prompt for helping to fill out your backstory and character arc for your personal narrative. 


Down time is spent in player created rooms on the discord server. These rooms act as the shops and the homes of you and your fellow colonists.They can be made individually, or players can choose to live together or even run businesses together. Descriptions and art for shops and homes are provided by the players with DM approval for anything that might break immersion.


DM Rob’s Note:
Crafting Goals:
To create a crafting system that is engaging, easy to understand, encourages interaction, requires minimum bookkeeping, is balanced, not susceptible to exploiting, and supports the main story. 
Crafting Overview:
Serfs, Merchants, and Aristocrats:
* Serfs get one “Labor” to spend each day. They must work directly under a merchant to turn that labor into useful crafted items. 
* Merchants manage laborers to make items. Every Merchant must have a patron in the aristocracy. Each magic item has a cost in labor and a cost in gold. Gold costs are listed in the Sane Magical Items document (a free resource by Saidaro and the folks over at the Giant in the Playground forums). Serfs always provide the labor, and generally aristocrats provide the gold.
* Aristocrats provide leadership and investment capital to the merchants.






Resources:
In Roanoke, there is a barter system. When you gain resources, it’s assumed that you will trade among the villagers and town stores to get exactly the materials you need for your craft. To simplify tracking resources, we are officially tracking them by their value. For example, if you find a gem worth 100 gold and a rare herb worth 100 gold, you can use either to craft a sword. It’s assumed you bartered with others until you acquired the proper materials for your crafting. Equipable items do not follow these rules, and may be sold to other players, or you may use the Fulker as an item sink to claim back 50% of the base value of the item.


Town inventory and buildings:

Section 1: The Aristocracy.


The term “Commonwealth” comes from the idea that for an orderly society to function, some of the wealth must be spent on issues that pertain to the common good. Aristocrats of the old world bring what wealth they can with them, and upon arriving at the island, start with 10,000 gold (described as 10,000 gold worth of resource goods and supplies in game). In addition, they bring civilization, rule of law, and a certain sophistication not found among the serfs or merchants. Only Aristocrats may own land, being granted a charter from the king. They are able to levy rents and taxes against those who wish to use this land. A wise Aristocrat can enjoy growing wealth, while a foolish one may soon be forced to beg for his daily bread, or even worse, work for it. 
        Aristocrats can vote on the direction of the settlement and influence key areas of politics.

        Aristocrats mini FAQ:


Q: Aristocrats are wealthy right? 
A: Yes! Both money, and group buffs will be available to the Aristocrats. Neither of which will be available to the players until the Tir Na Nog gets to Roanoke at the end of week 2. Your money will come in one lump sum and will be the bulk of the money available to commission items from merchants. The other 2/3rds of the players are going to be looking to you for your participation, even if it is the least involved of Caste of the three.


Q: I’m an Aristocrat and I spent all my gold! What do I do now?
A: I guess you should have managed your money more wisely. You can adventure for more gold, or you can take more drastic measures. You can renounce your title, give up any remaining wealth to the governor, and become a merchant or serf, bringing shame upon your family name in exchange for the ability to either help manage a shop or enter the ranks of the workforce.


Q: Is there a limit to how expensive an item we can craft?
A: Yes. Only three laborers can work for a merchant at any time. Practically, you will probably not be crafting anything costing above about 10,000 gold. If you had all the gold and ran your shop non-stop for 5 weeks, the theoretical maximum item you could craft would be anything up to 51,000 gold. It would be completed on the very last day of the event.




Section 2: The Serfs


Labor:
Every day, a serf has one unit of labor to spend. They do this by scheduling their labor in the crafting tracker. Labor is passive. They can adventure and role play all day, and still receive the benefits of their labor. They also don’t have to be online every day. As long as their labor is scheduled in the crafting tracker, it will count for that day.

Section 3: Merchants.


Merchant’s have the most control over their own lore, abilities, and storylines in Roanoke. This is for people who enjoy the crafting, bureaucracy or political portions of other games the most. Merchants are the heart of crafting. They can make amazing items, but can’t do it on their own. They are in charge of organizing their own shops, the order in which the items will be fulfilled  and finding the needed laborers to do the work.  


In addition, a permit is required. Permits can be granted by the Aristocracy, and help ensure that the business does not interfere with the king’s interests. It takes 1000 gold worth of resources to get set up. This cost can be paid by anyone, but is often paid by the patron aristocrat. Each merchant can come to their own agreement with their Aristocrat for securing a permit, but they can not craft on their own. The penalty for such an act is to have their goods and equipment seized and be thrown into jail. The cost is paid to an npc aboard the TirNa Nog.


Merchants without a patron can still participate, however, the merchant will not be able to take a cut of the profit, and the results may be considered an illicit act or service unless the mayor gives their permission. Damn the man! Power to the people!
