The Rowing Oak.

The Good King Edwin, long has been his reign, invites you, nobles, delegates, champions and the faire folk to attend him. Be you mighty of hand, voice or spirit, come to celebrate a life well lived. Near on a decade since his beloved but barren wife has passed on to the celestial mountain, Edwin is known to be searching far and wide for a future for his once mighty Empire. His failing health has begun to influence his ability to rule, and so he gathers the best and brightest together in hopes that a solution will present itself. Perhaps a suitable match can still be found in his late age? Or perhaps his nobles can be found wise and kind enough to be trusted to dissolve the monarchy peacefully? Or will his failing faculties cause him to place his trust in some sweet talking sycophant?

Let's talk play style and themes. Given the nature of a persistent game several things are going to be different than a typical game.

1. Time doesn't jump ahead out of convenience.

2. Your jobs and inventory are more important than most games.

3. Skill usage and combat isn't permitted without a DM present.

4. Retroactive continuity, while convenient in some circumstances, can give unfair advantages. It will only be done sparingly.

5. No combat against player characters outside of DM'd voice events.

6. This is a largely barter based economy. Learn to depend on others and be dependable. Griefing should only be at a shenanigan level.

7. Everyone deserves to be heard and have a satisfying story arc. Don't keep your concerns to yourself. Talk to your DMs it's our job to help you have fun.

Please ask questions.

This is a player synopsis for the events leading up to The Rowing Oak. Followed by instructions for the first week.

The Story of Roanoke begins with the story of a Dwarven Pirate who called himself Admiral Timmond. Admiral Timmond was once a clever and cunning smuggler and held a Letter of Marquess from the the city state Elloden (for the purposes of our game, Elloden is our Spain Analog). Elloden was ruled by a shrewd but elderly genius. Now know as King Edwin Kimvor the feeble. Edwin had a wife once, but she was stricken with werewolfism and so, employed the use of our clever Timmond to supply her with rare herbs to prevent her disease from being spread by way of progoney. This put him in contact with a local hero with a… flexible moral character. A goliath named Torax held a tight grip on smuggling in and out of the harbor.

The Queen died, of natural causes some years beyond where she would have been of a child bearing age. And sometime after, the king fell in love again. This time with his caretaker Nindis Pearl. Nindas was his closest confidant as the king succumbed to the debilitating effects of ALS.  The two had a child out of wedlock who would go on to become the famous bard Cora Pearl, and later one half of the explorer team Rust Dust and Pearl.

The foundation of the nation of Elloden was interwoven with a story about the birth of the Goddess Mystra and the Ioun stone mines which were the source of the nation’s considerable wealth.

The story however was a lie. The Kimvors and the Wizards of the Palace of Mysteries wove a compelling tale that hid a darker truth. It was not the birth of Mystra, but rather the death of her other half, the Goddess of Chaotic Magic Mystryl which had spawned the stones.

And so it came about that at the King’s 75th birthday, he organized a great tournament to decide the fate of the nation, ensuring that those he had cherry picked to be candidates for his legacy would be in attendance.

By arranging to have his own bank raided, for his own 35 tons of gold, moved the contestants into position to go through several trials to display their cunning, wisdom, mercy and strength.

Despite his own death on the second day, his will continued and the contestants moved along the path he had made until they were in the depths of the mine, making deals with nations and powers from beyond the material plane.

At the end of the third day, Torax the mighty and his companions slew a great leviathan who lived deep in an underground lake, reversed the ley lines which bled magic away from Mystryl’s final resting place and set into motions the events that would restore her to power.

When they did, however, they also tapped into an ancient truth about Elloden. It was built upon the Lode Stone and was in fact a city built to fly. It lifted, and the king’s estranged brother A’ren Kimvor took the throne with Torax’s soul forever melded with his family’s blade.

And now much of their story has been lost to time. Some of the most important names left unnamed, many of the secrets still yet to be revealed. It has been 50 years since Elloden took flight.

The city above is empty. It's streets once alive with people (now, banished to the bustling port with its slums and ghettos of the shore line below the floating city.)

The cities of Elloden. The Ruins of Kimvor and Loch Levithan (the port, metropolis and truest definition of a city) are your choices for starting places.

Beyond this, the rival nations of Cormyr, Netheril, Impultur, and Pondugaul have begun sending out ships to the land Rustdust and Pearl discovered and dubbed Arcania, based on the fact that despite its lack of Gods it was bursting with arcane power.

Your ship was the Peerless, and your capitan, Capitan Greyskies II, awaits your town charter and for you to vote a player to take on the mantle of Town Mayor. Your town charter is due by third day of play. If he leaves without one, your colony will be considered in rebellion to Cormyr.

Your charter must take into account a 5% crown tax, any laws you wish to enact, any justice system you wish to have, and a credo.
