Crafting Goals:
To create a crafting system that is engaging, easy to understand, encourages interaction, requires minimum bookkeeping, is balanced, not susceptible to exploiting, and supports the main story. 


Crafting Overview:
The people are broken into three classes, Serfs, Merchants, and Aristocrats. All three have advantages and disadvantages and must work together to create gear.


* Serfs get one “Labor” to spend each day. They must work directly under a merchant to turn that labor into useful crafted items. 
* Merchants manage laborers to make items. Every Merchant must have a patron in the aristocracy. Each magic item has a cost in labor and a cost in gold. Gold costs are listed in the Sane Magical Items document. Serfs always provide the labor, and generally aristocrats provide the gold.
* Aristocrats provide leadership and investment capital to the merchants.


Crafting Example:
(Anastasia the aristocrat would like to craft a +1 shortsword.) 
1. Anastasia has 10,000 gold worth of resources to trade that she has brought with her to the new world. 
2. Looking in the Sane Magic Items List, she sees that a +1 weapon costs 1000 gold. 
3. She talks to Maurice the merchant, who can make “Combat” Magic items. He informs her that he will make the sword for 1000 gold in resources and 2 days of labor, since every 500 gold in price requires one day of labor. 
(Maurice knows that he is providing this service at cost, but that doesn’t bother him, as he thinks it will be handy to get on good terms with the Aristocrat. Anastasia makes her way to the tavern and asks if there are any serfs looking for work. One crusty looking serf named Sedrick says he will help, for the right price.)
4. Sedrick the Serf asks for a payment of 100 gold for his two days of labor. Anastasia does not want to delay, and agrees to the price.
5. Anastasia pays Maurice up front for materials, and promises to pay Sedrick once the work is complete. 
6. Using the Crafting Tracker, Sedrick assigns his labor for the next 2 days at Maurice’s forge. 
7. After the 2 days have passed, the work is complete and the sword is made.


Important Points:
* Only Merchants can craft
* Only Serfs can provide labor
* Aristocracy starts with 10,000 gold
* Each merchant can only have 3 laborers working for them each day
* Use the Crafting Tracker
* Refer to Sane Magical Prices for costs and categories of items.
* You can only craft items in your category (consumable, combat, noncombat)
* Unassigned labor is wasted


Aristocracy:
Aristocrats of the old world bring what wealth they can with them, and start with 10,000 gold. In addition, they bring civilization, rule of law, and a certain sophistication not found among the serfs or merchants. Only Aristocrats may own land, being granted a charter from the king. They are able to levy rents and taxes against those who wish to use this land. A wise Aristocrat can enjoy growing wealth, while a foolish one may soon be forced to beg for his daily bread, or even worse, work for it.
        Aristocrats are granted an estate registered with the king and approved by the town council. There are strict zoning regulations because protected land is scarce. Only 1 merchant may operate effectively on each parcel of land. As the town grows, more parcels may become available. Aristocrats may come to any agreement they like with merchants. After merchants have been established, it is very difficult to tear down their structure and build a new facility, so it is better to acquire more land. An established merchant is effectively permanent.
        After a rudimentary wall is erected, land is partitioned out according to writs of ownership from the king. The settlement has 8 parcels of land to start with. Each can be purchased from the town, and the funds can be used by the Mayor for communal projects such as housing, latrines, and defences. 
        Aristocrats can vote on the direction of the settlement and influence key areas[a]

Merchants:
        Merchants are the heart of crafting. They can make amazing items, but can’t do it on their own. They need someone to bankroll each recipe, and they need laborers to do the work. In addition, a permit is required. Permits can be granted by the Aristocracy, and help ensure that the business does not interfere with the king’s interests. It takes 1000 gold worth of resources to get set up. This cost can be paid by anyone, but is often paid by the patron aristocrat. Each merchant can come to their own agreement with their Aristocrat for securing a permit, but they can not craft on their own. The penalty for such an act is to have their goods and equipment seized and be thrown into jail. Merchants without a patron can still participate, however. Each day they can produce 2 mundane items valued at 50 gold or less each. These items must be appropriate to their profession, and can be put up for sale at the town market. There are three main categories of merchants, and one special service category. Each category contains a list of items that can be crafted along with their price in gold. Some items can not be crafted.


Consumables - Alchemist, Allspice, Apothecary, Aquavitalist, Bee Master, Cheesemonger, Sawbones, Keeper, Vintner, Fisherman
Combat Items - Bladesmith, Bowyer, Haberdasher, Goldsmith, Cobbler,Girdler, Nobthatcher, Forger, Stonemason, Furrier.
Non Combat Items - Carpenter, Cooper, Dress Maker,  Friseur,  Mercer, Milliner, , Schrimpschonger, Beadle, ClodHopper, Arbiter.

Resources:
In Roanoke, there is a barter system. When you gain resources, it’s assumed that you will trade among the villagers and town stores to get exactly the materials you need for your craft. To simplify tracking resources, we are officially tracking them by their value. For example, if you find a gem worth 100 gold and a rare herb worth 100 gold, you can use either to craft a sword. It’s assumed you bartered with others until you acquired the proper materials for your crafting.


Serf Class:
Labor:
Every day, a serf has one unit of labor to spend. They do this by scheduling their labor in the crafting tracker. Labor is passive. They can adventure and role play all day, and still receive the benefits of their labor. They also don’t have to be online every day. As long as their labor is scheduled in the crafting tracker, it will count for that day.

Aristocracy Class:
This island is now owned by King George, and all profits are subject to tax. As representatives of the crown, it is your solemn responsibility to keep an ordered society. You are now the lawful rulers of all inhabitants, both settlers and native. It is your responsibility to see that they contribute to the efforts of the crown.


Running the town is costly, as there are always expenses to be paid. If certain costs cannot be paid, then mass debuffs are put on the whole population (excluding the aristocracy). After enough debuffs, the people may take matters into their own hands.


A cost of 5000 gold per day in resources is required to be paid by the governor for all the little needs of the colony. These included clean latrines, sleeping quarters, providing food for everyone, patrolling the perimeter, etc. For each day that the amount is not paid, the common people gain one level of exhaustion, up to the fourth level.


The governor must pay this cost by posting in the “Town Upkeep Room”. Daily posts will outline that day’s issue that requires payment. If there is no response to the previous day’s comment by the governor, or their deputy, then one level of exhaustion is placed on all non-aristocracy. Each day the fee is paid, one level of exhaustion is removed from the population.


In addition to this baseline cost, additional cost of money or labor may be required as events unfold.


(The point of this upkeep is to give the Aristocracy a problem they have to solve. Even if they pool their money, they will be broke very quickly. Assuming 12 aristocrats, they would all be completely broke in 24 days just from the upkeep. If they want to do any crafting, they will run out for sure. This is assuming they all work together. Only the governor is responsible for paying this cost. So they could levy taxes against the people, or organize expeditions to impose taxes on the natives and take their resources. It’s up to them, and the imagination of the GM’s.)

Questions and Answers:
Q: I’m a merchant. What can I craft?
A: Crafting of magic items is limited to items in the Sane Magical Prices document, under the appropriate category based on your merchant profession (Consumable, Combat, Noncombat). Each merchant can flavor their items to fit their particular profession, as long as the effects don’t change. For example, a cooper might craft a magic bucket that when poured out over you, grants the same ability as boots of levitation.


Q: I’m a merchant, but don’t have any gold, what can I do?
A: Beg money from your patron, make friends with someone who is rich, or adventure for resources and treasure. 


Q: I don’t have a patron. Can I still craft?
A: Merchants can craft 2 mundane items costing 50 gold or less per day for free without needing any labor or a patron.


Q: I’m an Aristocrat and I spent all my gold! What do I do now?
A: I guess you should have managed your money more wisely. You can adventure for more gold, or you can take more drastic measures. You can renounce your title, give up any remaining wealth to the governor, and become a merchant or serf, bringing shame upon your family name.


Q: I’m a serf. What can I craft?
A: Nothing. Serfs are only good for one thing. Cheap Labor. You must work directly under a merchant to use your labor. Serfs do have an ability to generate a small amount of gold in resources from their specific boon, so they are not completely useless.


Q: What if I can’t find the right merchant to craft what I want?
A: There are only three categories of crafted goods, there should be someone who can make what you need. You could always train to become a merchant yourself. But you must find a willing patron in the aristocracy to sponsor you, and pay the setup cost of 1000 gold. 


Q: Is there a limit to how expensive an item I can craft?
A: Yes. Only three laborers can work for a merchant at any time. Practically, you will probably not be crafting anything costing above about 10,000 gold. If you had all the gold and ran your shop non-stop for 5 weeks, the theoretical maximum item you could craft would be anything up to 51,000 gold. It would be completed on the very last day of the event.


Q: Can merchants join forces?
A: Yes, but with restrictions. Opening a joint shop does not reduce setup costs, but it does allow multiple items categories to be crafted under one company, and spreading out the responsibilities can ensure that you have someone available to help customers, and manage labor. 


Q: Can two merchants in the same category work on the same item together?
A: No. Merchants can pool their resources and open a joint shop under one patron, but the setup costs are the same as if they opened two shops, and they can’t collaborate on the same item. Each merchant is still effectively running their own operation.


Q: I’m a serf, and I forgot to assign my labor yesterday. Can I retroactively work for someone?
A: No. Any labor not assigned in the Crafting Tracker is wasted. You can and should schedule your labor as far into the future as possible. You shouldn’t have been so lazy… serfs don’t get days off.
