

The story of worlds, both Old and New.
T
This document represents the story telling of four seperate
DMs. Not only is it meant to help equip you and orient you to
our world. It is also meant to help inform you about the very
different playstyles your DM's will be presenting.
he Old world (Everything in the Eastern
Hemisphere of real world geography) is the
domain of the Gods. All Gods great and small
have dominion over their land, their people and
their religions. There is no escape from the
impact this has on daily life in the Old World.
The God’s feed on the faith and offerings of
their devoted. Each of them embodying a different aspect of
life, or championing their many conflicting causes.
Finding your place in the worlds:
As to the specific gods, our game concerns many pantheons
of gods, including our Gods of the Round Table and their
dominion of England. More information can be found here.
Other countries may be referenced, but for the time being this
is an unwritten blank space for you to apply your imagination
and research. The writing that exists in the game pulls from a
wide variety of folklore (from the huldufolk of Icelandic Myth,
to the Myths and legends of the Hermetic faiths that point to
Egypt as their fictional backdrop. In other cases, our DM’s
have free reign to use any assets (including pantheons) that
are published by WOTC in their game crafting and lore that
pertains to the stories they tell. Because we are writing
collectively, the lore of Arcania can be amorphous and fluid at
times. Speak to your DM about any ideas you might have
about where your Character might be from, and what sort of
folklore you may wish to adapt. Hopefully future iterations of
the game will have more choices than just England.
There is a single narrative that weaves the fates of the Old
World and the New World. This narrative is the story of the
two brothers who founded the Occult traditions, The Golden
Dawn and The Freemasons. They represent two ways to
change the world. Hiram champions forethought and
planning, and the magic of his Freemasons equate to having
empathy for the struggles of those around you. The Golden
Dawn represents a reactionary world. A world obsessed with
outrage and entitlement. Solomon is selfish and lazy. He is a
petty immortal charlatan who still pines for his brother
Hiram’s throne, despite the kingdom of the Brunelocks falling
beneath the sands of the Sahara well over 1000 years ago.
Coming to Arcania
The setting is in the first half of the 17th century. Nearly 100
years since Christopher Columbus stole the secrets to
accessing the New World from a group of Masonic Vikings
known as the Roseline Temple. Along with the other
members of Roseline Temple, the First Arcanian Cryptid to
set foot on the shores of The Old World was a Sasquatch
named Bigfoot. He, along with the Roseline Temple leaders
Hiram Abiff, the half Orc Pym “of the Rosecutter”, and many
more of their ranks worked with the Wardens of the New
World (of the Continent Arcania) Columbia, Cascadia,
Fontaine And Oro to build a barrier between the two
locations to prevent the Gods of the Old World from
dominating the New World.
Despite the fact that roughly one third of the Gods are
aligned to the ideas of good and benevolence, two thirds are
either indifferent, or have dominating designs for the lesser
beings beneath them. The dangers outweighed the possible
benefits of opening their shores to the Cleric, Paladins and
Warlocks of the Old World.
The New world (Everything in the Western Hemisphere of
real world geography) is the home of Arcane magic. While
Arcane magic is used around the world, it is pristine and
unfettered by the will of the Gods of the Old World. Leylines
carry Arcane magic from its source in Arcania to all other
locations in the world. And this causes a subtle pull on all
spell casters to journey to its source. The search for the point
of origin for the nexus of all arcane magical power is called
the search for the NorthWest Passage, and is the obsession
of many Wizards, Sorcerers and other types of Arcane
Casters.
Colonialism in the Worlds both
Old and New.
Your reasons for traveling to the New World are yours and
yours alone. Understanding your “coming to America” story
starts with an understanding of the people who actually came
here. It represents change. Change that can be either
carefully thought out with good intentions and considerations
for those around you, or change that can be brutal, violent
and scar your heart. In this fantasy remimaging of early
American History, you are what you bring with you from the
Old World. Or in the case of a Native Race, you are left with
the choice of how to deal with those who have come. This is
not intended to follow history. It is intended to give you the
chance to imagine being at the beginning of a new
civilization. The conflicts you have in the game, and the
resolutions you make are likely to inform the shape of the
games to come.

The Knights du Roi Soleil:
The Knights du Roi Soleil represent everything Arcania fears
(whether or not some of their fears maybe misguided if for
the players to decide). The will of the gods is vast and varied,
but the knights believe that they (the gods) existed to help and
guide mortals through life. Giving them strength when they
needed it, and hope when they were without it. Somewhere
in the cycle of Croatoan years ago, the Day of the Black Sun
commenced and all warmth that emanated from Machidiel's
power was destroyed. One man's divine oath before all the
living gods of good faith ignited a movement and passion
inside the founding order: the Knights du Roi Soleil, the sun
king.
The Eclipse Knight who began the order has been
lost to time, and the oral tradition of his myths and
fables. However, his deeds were not forgotten as
the Knights spread their ideals through their pure
hearts and noble minds into the world. All those
who gave their faith to any God of justice or nature
could find sanctuary within their walls and a greater
purpose within.
The dark days did not cease however, as when tales of a
monstrosity so evil had been delivered unto the highest
heights of the Celestial mountain palaces of the gods, a great
battle took place. The battle of epic proportions could not be
contained to that single plane, as the great evil hunger called
upon its creations and followers, dividing the gods forces and
luring them to the material plane. Catastrophic damage
would have been done to all mortals had the gods not brought
it to the abyssal Atlantic Ocean. Many gods here suffered
casualties of grand proportion. An outcome too costly to be
called a victory, for they gave their lives to only delay the
inevitable in an unearthly prison, to let its hunger swell to
insatiable size.
This pyrrhic success dubbed the Battle of the Atlantic
Abyss led for many newer and younger gods to take up the
mantle in attempts to guide others and answer upon divine
call, however the last battle of the gods solidified the Knights
du Roi Soleil's path. It is their belief and practice that through
the combined conviction of man and the lost relics of the
gods, they can face this ancient evil being and untwist the
corrupted souls that follow it.

Roanoke Island Lore
A
rcania, the New World is a vast and magical
place, long sealed off from the Old World by a
magical barrier that sheltered it from the
influence of divine magic. That Barrier fell
during the course of the events of season 2.
Arcania is ruled by four powerful entities known as
“Wardens”. The wardens are Columbia, Cascadia, Oro and
Fontaine and are divided by physical location. Columbia is
the Warden of the North East, and her District will one day
become the capital of Arcania. Fontaine is the Warden of the
South East and makes her home deep in the Floridian
jungles.
Roanoke, being set in North Carolina in the 1630’s is at a
midpoint between Fontaine, the warden of life and Columbia
the warden of Justice. They are protectors and patrons of the
Arcanian people.
The island itself is populated with 5 playable races:
The Urukocra
The End of Season 2 left the Uru as a largely untouched
feature of the Island. This year they have been far more
fleshed out, with their own city and strange politics. The
Vulture men of the canopy are by far the most dominant
force on the island, seeing their home in roostloft as the
highest authority on the island.
The Jackalope
The End of Season 2 left the whereabouts of the Druid
Lich Oleander unaccounted for. He and his Lychwood
Treants are nowhere to be seen. The sly and obnoxiously
cute vagabonds of the island, Jackalopes live in
inaccessible warrens deep below the island.
The Chinnokin
The End of Season 2 left the Chinnokin in The
Salmonfolk are two races that are in conflict this year.
They may not be as passive as they have been in past
seasons.
The Tatankan
The end of Season 2 left the fate of the Tatankan people in
grave peril. This passive culture was turned to stone with
but a single Tatankan left as guardian of the pen.
Physically, these are the most imposing people on the
island. As herd animals and plainsmen, they are rarely
seen alone.
The Halfwudgie
If the Uru were our Skeksis, then the halfwudgies would
be our podlings. Our final race, and the only new race to
this year’s campaign, the halfwudgies are medium sized
humanoids covered in thick brown fur and long quills that
can be used as natural weapons.

Combat Field Scholar
Academics, Servants, and Flunkies
Support your Slayer
You have spent your days in service of St. George the Dragon
Slayer. And his guild of Combat Field Scholars. The Order's
Combat Field Scholars are an organization whose founders
were a group of adventurers who famously assisted the
slaying of an ancient Green Dragon named Bwytawr ffwl
(Fool Eater). Fool Eater was a brutal tyrant prone to cruelty
who ruled over several Welsh hamlets with a careless
gluttony for their livestock. Desperate to rid his parish of the
terrible beast, Saint George turned to the scholars of the land
and brought them together to find a way to overcome their
oppressor. Famously, they were the first to try to use the
Identify spell (a spell with the range “touch”) on a Dragon, in
all the known world . Together they stealthily infiltrated his
lair and ritually cast for hours while the dragon slept atop his
horde pile. Once the knowledge was gained, Saint George
himself Slew the wretched monster.
Since then the group has grown. And grown more
organized along the way. There was little in their way to
hamper their efficacy and self organization. Over time, their
ranks began to swell with the clever middlemen content with
their comfortable lives but capable of amazing acts of will.
The order is a true meritocracy with only the most skilled
making the decisions for the group, and the leader passing on
their responsibilities when they are surpassed.
Choose one of the Gods of the Round table and work with
your DM to detail the nature of your service to the order. Are
you a field researcher, or a cloistered scholar? Were you
raised amongst the order? Or are you seeking sanctuary
within it? Do you have rank within the organization, or are
you but a humble monk? Perhaps you came from a village
where you were the highest authority, only to find that upon
coming to London your reputation isn’t nearly as
distinguished as you had thought.
Why be powerful or famous when you can
be close to those who are?
Who do you thank or blame for your station in life? Did your
mother send you off to the school despite your father's
concerns about your scrawny size? Did you get assigned a
squireship to keep a king's third son (WULF the half ghast)
from any more property damage? Do you sometimes envy the
table scraps being given to pampered pets? Did you join
because you wanted to be as near to the fame and glory of the
most famous knights and nobility as you could? Or did you
join in hopes of raising your station in life by catching
someone's eye?
Everyone trusts a wellspoken person
with a clipboard.
Combat Field Scholars control the infrastructure of London.
Despite being a private entity, they are the postal service, the
town criers, they are your school masters, they handle the
affairs of busy work and steadily entwine the country with
silent help. As the country's news source, education, lines of
communication, and bureaucracy, the Combat Field Scholars
wield a sort of slow, silent pressure that influences so
profoundly that no one notices.

To Lord Chamberlain:
June 29th, 1613 was the day the greatest bardic college in all
of England was revealed to be an private spy and smuggler
organization run by the nefarious Christopher Marlow.
Marlow’s theater was under protection from the Crown, as it
was one of the Queen’s favorite forms of entertainment.
Marlow, who is now well known to be a master spy and

former custodians of the crater in the ground which was once

The Groundlings of the Globe
You are one of the few, one of the dedicated suffering artists
who is as likely to be paid in stew and ale for his art as he is
to play for precious gems and sacks of gold from royalty. The
Globe may be burnt to the ground, but the Groundlings of the
Globe still live on in every audience member, in every
rhyming couplet of iambic pentameter, is every brush stroke
and every line penned. To be a Groundling is to accept that
you are the master of the Craft that must constantly justify its
own existence.
No one says to a carpenter “Show me a new way to build a
house.” But a musician must constantly learn or write new
songs. And a playwright cannot ask his actors to act out the
same play everyday for all time. And so, they must continue to
invent new worlds and characters to explore. To be a
Groundling, you can be anyone one. But to be a true
groundling, you cannot be anyone else. Forsake your trade,
forsake your inheritance, there is only the spotlight and the
knowledge that to chase it is more likely to lead you to ruin
than fame. But for the groundlings who stay true through the
lean times, there is a bond that is unshakable. The is a
consistency of good character and a shared brotherhood
between players and artists that cannot be compared. Artists
who call themselves groundlings take care of one another,
they share when they have nothing to give. And so long as it
goes back into raising up the community to be better, they
give even more when they have plenty.

a marriage or a smear campaign to help influence politics.

The Lazaurian Oath
The Oath
“I will use my mind to restore the health of all creatures with
sympathy, compassion, and heedfulness
I will use my hands to be able to prepare medicines well
I will use my eyes to know what medicine is suitable and what
is not. I will not give the unsuitable, only the suitable.
I will use my heart to minister to the sick with an attitude of
love, not out of desire for gain.
I will use my legs to remain with the sick wherever they are. 
I will use my lungs to instruct the novice and bring cheer to
the ill.
And when all fails, and death arrives, it is I who will thank him
for the peace given unto the wretched.”
Your pledge to the Oath of Lazarus is to the Oath and no
other. And though many speak for the Oath takers, there is no
one who governs you and no code but your own ethics. Those
who have taken the Oath refer to themselves as the Afflicted

The Vigil of Siege Perilous
Before there was England there was the throne. Before the
first of the angler tribe departed the main land of the Old
World, there was the throne. At the dawning of the dragons,
the green and the red, the white and the black laid claim to all
of the land. A single gold dragon looked on the isles and wept
for their future. And so Merlin the Golden Dragon, and 33rd
degree mason, came to Wales. He set the capstone in Cardiff,
of what would one day become Camelot. And Upon that place
Merlin returned to make a throne for his King. Not the throne
he would sit on, but a masonic machine of perfecting justice.
The measuring tool upon which good is divided from bad.
The Siege Perilous. Those who prefer no uncertain terms in
life, rigidity, habit and discipline are likely to belong to a local
chapter of the the Siege Perilous.
A Cult of Vigilantism
You believe that the world is clearly and absolutely divided by
Good and Evil. And the dividing line between them exacts a
toll upon those who live their lives as anything less than
good.The vigil of Siege Perilous seeks to safeguard Arthur’s
kingdom in his absence by deed and song. The deeds done
are recorded in a heraldic tome called the Y brenin unwaith
ac yn y dyfodol (The Once and Future King). Any deed one
wishes to record may be inscribed in the tome for a fee of 5
silver pieces. This has made the leadership of the Vigil both
rich, and sure of the rightness of their deeds (however correct
or misguided they may be). Talk to your DM. Work out where
you fit in this rigid religious structure. Are you a shrewd and
clever leader ready to lay claim to a mayoral race? or perhaps
you are a hedge knight, roaming the king's roads in search of
evil to punish. In most small towns a chapter of the Vigil
chooses to self police their home and help protect their
region against invasion. Were you an upstanding citizen of
some hamlet assigned to town defense only to find that after
a day's trip to the market, the home you returned to had been
sacked by monsters leaving you to seek a new start in the
world?
The fate of the New World relies on you
Decide the moral cause you will champion. Chastity,
Temperance, Charity, Diligence, Patience, Kindness, or
Humility. And a vice you wish to eliminate from the world:
Lust, Gluttony, Greed, Sloth, Wrath, Envy, or Pride. Design a

your DM what your chosen options do for your lifestyle and
RP interactions.
