It is useful to pretend.

Let's pretend that the world's problems could in-fact be solved with magical powers, brave adventurers, clever planning, shenanigans and maybe even a heartfelt moment or two.

In this world there is a fundamental kind of magic. One can impart their intentions on an object by writing them in a specific kind of way. And while many echoes of this idea percuss through the eons and cosmos, there is one object that is more important than any other upon which is written the intentions of one man. 

His name is Hiram. He is (or perhaps was) a desert-born king. A brown-haired Dwarven mute master Mason. He sets his intentions by way of engraving them upon the world's cornerstone.  

After he sets his intentions, the epoch begins. As the cornerstone is set outside of time, all of time and space sprawl out from the cornerstone. Each time he does this is called an “iteration. This certainly isn't the first time Hiram has set his intentions on this stone. In each iteration, he makes changes. Trying to solve the problem of the Will in the Wind. 

The wind does not blow in the place where the cornerstone is until Hiram departs. But as soon as he does, the dark wind blows, and slowly begins to erode his will from the stone until one day it will be smooth once again. 

While it can vary from iteration to iteration depending on Hiram's goals (the game runner's story) a typical iteration spans a specific set of years (an epoch) in a specific location (Arcania, Empyrean etc.). This handbook covers The New World: Arcania in the 1600s. And The Revolution: Arcania in the 1700s.

Hiram's own story is sometimes useful. And sometimes it is not. But as the Game runner, it can be useful to pretend that your homebrew rules are Hiram's rules changes for this particular iteration.

What your players need to know is themselves, and the world you are bringing them into. 

Here is how to introduce them to character building. 

Your character has several traits that are useful for describing how they interact with the world

1. Your character will have several facts that are in common with every other character such as: Your level, your luck,  your speed and your endurance.   
2. The fact that your powers come from a specific source called a wellspring. These wellsprings are named Arcane, Divine, Valor and Eldritch Shadow. The first two, Arcane and Divine are external sources of power. Either Arcane magic drawn from the leylines in the world around you. Or a measure of Divinity, granted through petition to a higher order of beings. The wellsprings of Valor and the Eldritch Shadow are both internal, coming from your body or mind. Your Wellspring provides Odd leveled abilities as you progress in level.  Additionally, where your power manifests in your person, either in your body (called Corporea) or your mind (called Essentia) is called a focus. There are six of each type of focus to choose from. The confluence (combination of) your focus and your wellspring then becomes your character class for battle with your focus providing the even level class features that your character attains as you increase in level.  
3. Every Character has both a social and a battle disposition. The three types of disposition are: Engage, Remain and Disengage. Its typifies both your attitude in any situation you find yourself in as well as your starting range during combat respectively.  
   Socially, a character with the Engaged disposition could be the face of the group. They tend to be proactive and engage with NPCs more readily. A character that   
   Remains has a more passive stance, ready to react or counter the social abilities of an NPC. A character with the Disengaged disposition is more like to be an expert at bypassing confrontations through stealth or guile. Similarly, a character that has the Engaged disposition in battle (and you can have a different disposition in battle to your social disposition) begins in the Frontlines and takes a more direct approach. In the Midfield you will find the Remain disposition ready to be more opportunistic in their combat style. And finally you have the disengaged disposition. At the range called The Backlines, those who have a playstyle more akin to a support, a healer, a controller or perhaps a glass cannon will find options that they might find advantageous. Your battle disposition, in conjunction with your speed stat, helps determine your turn order in combat.   
4. Each character has two sets of skills. Your social skills are built around Adventure Momentum. Adventure Momentum is a pool of points used to help make non combat things happen and help move your story forward. Your particular skills, besides the ones available to all characters, are derived from a combination of choices made during character creation. Many such choices are contained in your Saga Your character's ongoing narrative, revealing who they are beyond their combat abilities, and how they interact with the world around them/Your saga sets the stage for your non-combat abilities.  Including your  Masks and Core-principles. Your Core Principles are the central aspects of your character's self-image that dictate their natural inclinations and abilities in social interactions. Whereas Masks are facets of your character's personality that they present to the outside world, often used to navigate or manipulate social situations. These can include traits that gain superficial or temporary bonuses but often come with drawbacks that may hinder your long term character growth. You gain one Adventure Momentum per level. This resets at the beginning of a new game session, after a long rest, through the use of abilities or items, or at your Game runner’s discretion.   
5. Like your Adventure momentum, Battle momentum is how you get things done in combat. Combat in this play system is set up to be highly tactical but also far quicker than most ttrpgs. While in combat, you may either act or move (you may change to one adjacent range which determines your new disposition) without spending battle momentum. It can be useful to think of it as “the first thing you do on your turn is free.” So long as it is one of the 9 basic actions that are common to all characters.   
6. Your character has a choice of three actions during combat that are related to their combat disposition. In addition to your single action or movement,  Battle momentum can then be spent to either change your disposition (and therefore your range) or activate the abilities granted by your confluence of wellspring and focus. Your starting range/disposition is determined by another part of your character’s backstory called your Awakening Story. Your Awakening Story describes the circumstances under which you discovered your abilities. This also provides your starting equipment and money. You gain one Battle momentum per level. This resets at the beginning of a new game session, after a long rest, through the use of abilities or items, or at your Game runner’s discretion.   
7. As you level, you will gain a single point per level that can be put into either luck, speed or endurance. Your luck stat is your modifier for contested rolls. Contested rolls come both inside and outside of combat in specific key circumstances. Your speed is a value that gets added to your turn order roll. While this might not seem important at first, with the speed of combat in the game, getting the jump on an enemy can often determine the outcome of a battle. Endurance is like hit points. You can take up to two attacks at level one. If you’d like to be able to soak more damage than that, you’d better level your endurance. Your endurance stat can also be called upon in certain circumstances to help determine your resilience to physical challenges like traps, poison and disease. Losing endurance points can make you more susceptible both in and out of combat. Losses are not typically permanent and can often be restored through various methods such as resting, healing abilities or items. Speed, on the other hand is often incredibly important. The low number of endurance points and the ability to pile on game changing actions mean that getting the jump on your opponents (either socially or in battle) can greatly impact the battle. 

**Actions**

Each character has a disposition that influences their approach to situations, particularly in combat. These dispositions – Engage, Disengage, and Remain – dictate the types of actions a character is predisposed to take.

**Engage Disposition**:   
   \- *Attack*: Directly confront enemies in combat.  
   \- Cast Spell: Use magical abilities against foes or to aid allies.  
   \- Talk: Engage in dialogue to use social abilities in combat.

**Disengage Disposition**:   
   \- *Defend*: Focus on personal defense or shielding allies.  
   \- *Protect*: Actively work to prevent harm to comrades.  
   \- *Revive*: Bring incapacitated allies back into the fray.

**Remain Disposition**:   
   \- *Analyze*: Assess the situation for strategic insights.  
   \- *Evasive Maneuver*: Dodge attacks or reposition to avoid danger.  
   \- *Counter:* Prepare to respond to enemy actions, striking back opportunistically.

**Battle Momentum**

Battle Momentum is a key resource in this system. It represents a character's ability to perform extra actions or enhance their effectiveness in combat.

\- Momentum Use: At the start of each turn, characters get one free action roll. They can spend Battle Momentum to take additional actions or to boost the effectiveness of their rolls.  
\- Momentum for Bonus: Spending one Momentum can grant a \+1 bonus to any roll, making the action more likely to succeed.

**Roll Types**

There are two main types of rolls in this system, each serving a different purpose:

1\. **Uncontested Rolls**:   
   \- These are standard rolls where a character tries to achieve something without direct opposition, like casting a spell or jumping over a chasm.  
   \- The size of the die used for these rolls decreases as the character hits odd numbered levels (level 1: d4, Level 3: d6, Level 5: d8, Level 7: d10, Level 9: d12), reflecting their increasing proficiency.  
   \- A \+1 bonus can significantly improve the chances of success in these rolls.  
Your luck stat also increases the results of these rolls.

2\. **Contested Rolls**:   
   \- These occur when characters directly oppose each other, like in a duel or arm-wrestling match.  
   \- Characters roll a number of d6s equal to their attempts (including the free roll and any additional ones from spent Momentum).  
   \- In defense scenarios, there's usually a limit of 2 defense dice unless the character has abilities or gear that increase this cap.  
Your luck stat increases the number of die you can add to your attempt. 

**Tips: Strategic Implications**

**Disposition Choice**: Players must carefully choose their disposition based on their character’s strengths and the tactical needs of the situation.  
Momentum Management: Wisely managing Battle Momentum is crucial. Players must decide whether to spend it on additional actions, boost a critical roll, or save it for future turns.  
**Roll Strategy**: The choice between uncontested and contested rolls depends on the situation. In uncontested scenarios, players aim for high rolls, while in contested ones, they need to out-roll their opponents.

Movement and combat turn order.  
Movement and Positioning on the Battle Grid

Combat in this game is a dance of strategy, taking place on a grid that represents the battlefield. Understanding your range and position is crucial to effective gameplay.

**Frontline**

When you're within one square of an enemy combatant—what could be termed "melee range"—you're considered to be on the Frontline. At level 1, if you're in an Engage disposition, you must be on the Frontline to act accordingly.

**Midfield**

If you're within 2-3 squares of an enemy, you're in the Midfield. This is the range where your Remain disposition comes into play. Here, you can observe the battle, prepare for counter-actions, or use abilities that have moderate reach.

**Backline**

Beyond 3 squares away from the enemy lies the Backline, where your Disengage disposition becomes relevant. This is where you're safest from immediate physical threats and are free to use long-range abilities or spells.

**Movement**

It costs momentum to move from one range to another, transitioning from Frontline to Midfield or Midfield to Backline and vice versa. Special focuses, like Icarus or Stride, may allow you to bypass some of these standard movement restrictions or even take to the air.

Keep in mind, actions such as engaging in combat, defending, or any of the various options your character has available will depend on their position on the grid as well as their current disposition. Some abilities and spells may also have specific range requirements, so always consider your position carefully.

**Enemy Movement**: In this system, range is a relative concept, closely tied to your position relative to your enemy. This means that if an enemy moves to a different range, it affects your own positioning as well. It's a dynamic dance where every step counts, and your choices must adapt to the shifting battlefield.

For instance, if you've specialized in mid-range combat and an enemy moves to close range, you now find yourself at a disadvantage. You have two primary options:

**Spend Battle Momentum to Change Disposition**: If you're unwilling or unable to change range, you can spend momentum to adjust your combat disposition.

**Difficult Terrain**: Should the battlefield be a snarl of roots or a field of mud, moving between adjacent positions consumes a Battle Momentum point. The environment, then, becomes more than a backdrop; it's a strategic consideration that impacts your available options.

**Tips: Special Tactics and Battle Momentum.**

Flanking, Grappling, magical walls or conditions  and other special maneuvers typically require Battle Momentum. Their efficacy can hinge on factors like your current disposition, positional strategy, and environmental challenges.

Wellsprings

**Arcane:**

The Arcane Wellspring is the distillation of wisdom and ordered power, a legacy safeguarded by the Arcanian Wardens. It isn't a gift from gods or a mysterious internal force; it's the language of the magical world spelled out in incantations and ancient principles. Your weapon seems to hum, almost of its own accord, vibrating to an inaudible melody when near ley lines or faded inscriptions. The swing of your blade becomes a dance of geometry and precision, a deadly expression of arcane rationale.  
Alternatively, your manipulation of spells becomes a kind of arcane artistry. You find yourself not just casting, but unweaving and reweaving the tapestry of magic that constitutes a spell. Your "Hero Moment" reveals to you the unseen scaffolding of the arcane world, allowing you to anticipate an opponent's magical movements, or even to risk distorting the magical aether for a momentary advantage, albeit making you vulnerable to counter-spells.  
You are a custodian of the Arcanian legacy, each spell a continuance of the Warden's vows to guide and protect. Your power comes with the obligation to apply great wisdom, ethical nuance, and mental acuity—a compact with a long line of unseen mentors. In wielding this Wellspring, you're not just gaining mastery; you're contributing to Arcania's ongoing story.

**Arcane Wellspring**  
Level 1: Arcane Bolt  
You harness the arcane energy within you to project a powerful bolt of energy at a single target within your current range. Spend 1 battle momentum to unleash an Arcane Bolt, dealing damage equal to 1 point to the selected enemy's endurance. This ranged attack allows you to strike down foes within your current range, reducing their endurance by 1 point upon a successful hit.

Level 3: Arcane Nova  
Your control over arcane energy grows, allowing you to create an explosion of energy. Spend 2 battle momentum to cast Arcane Nova, causing a burst of arcane power to radiate from you in a radius matching your current range. All enemies within this area take damage equal to 1 point to their endurance, and their endurance is reduced by 1 point upon a successful hit.

Level 5: Arcane Venom  
You infuse your attacks with lingering arcane venom. Spend 3 battle momentum to poison a target, causing them to lose 1 endurance point at the start of each of their turns for a duration of 3 rounds. This damage-over-time effect is especially effective against enemies with high endurance.

Level 7: Arcane Blink  
Your mastery of arcane forces grants you the ability to teleport short distances. Spend 4 battle momentum to cast Arcane Blink, allowing you to teleport to any location within your current range on the battle grid and within your line of sight. This sudden movement is swift and unpredictable, giving you a tactical advantage.

Level 9: Arcane Cataclysm  
Your command over arcane energy reaches its zenith. Spend 5 battle momentum to initiate an Arcane Cataclysm, unleashing a devastating wave of arcane power. This explosion affects a radius matching your current range, dealing damage equal to 2 points to all enemies' endurance caught within it. Additionally, their maximum endurance is reduced by 2 points upon a successful hit.

The Divine Wellspring is not just an external force; it's the ethereal core of existence, a ceaseless flow of wisdom and energy connecting the fabric of all things. Engaging with it feels like stepping into an ancient cathedral, where each stone whispers tales of bygone epochs. Light streams through stained-glass windows, bathing you in a celestial glow that's as much a blessing as it is illumination. When you speak, the timbre of your voice carries an inexplicable resonance, like a hymn that transcends words. Your speech doesn't merely inform; it shapes the very arcs of destinies. Those who hear you sense it—something in your words leaves an indelible mark on their souls, a sense of awe they can't articulate but feel deeply. This wellspring is not about flashiness or theatrical shows of power. It's subtler, more profound, revealing its depth through layers of unembellished purity. It's as if the universe has leaned in to murmur its oldest secrets into the very marrow of your being. This communion transforms you, not just in the actions you perform, but in the very essence of who you are. In times of conflict or ethical quandaries, your decisions aren't just simple selections among options. They're reflections of deeply ingrained convictions, imbued with a cosmic wisdom that feels as ancient as the stars. You're not just leaving a footprint on the world; you're serving a purpose grander and more celestial, a calling that resonates in chambers occupied by deities whose names might be forgotten but whose legacies are eternal.  
Your actions, then, are more than feats or accomplishments; they're manifestations of divine character. It's not the miracles you can perform that define you, but the quiet, transformative miracle that you become. In that humble yet sublime transformation, you're not merely a hero or a messenger; you're a shard of the universe becoming aware of its own sacred nature.

**Divine Wellspring Wellspring**

Level 1: Blessed Ward  
You channel the divine energy within you to form a protective barrier. Spend 1 battle momentum to grant you or an ally a shield that absorbs damage equal to your character level until your next turn. This shield can be used to reduce incoming damage.

Level 3: Sacred Grace  
Your divine power imbues your actions with celestial grace. Spend 2 battle momentum to automatically succeed on a defensive roll or a contested roll that aids an ally, reinforcing the strength of your bonds. This success applies to actions like protecting, healing, or aiding an ally.

Level 5: Divine Intervention  
At the brink of despair, the gods hear your call. Spend 3 battle momentum to negate a potentially fatal blow against you or an ally, as a sudden divine intervention deflects the attack. This ability can be used as a reaction to prevent an attack from reducing your or your ally's Endurance to 0 or lower, effectively saving you from being knocked out.

Level 7: Sanctified Ground  
Your connection to the divine allows you to sanctify the battlefield. Spend 4 battle momentum to create a zone of sacred energy within your line of sight. Enemies entering or starting their turn in this area suffer a penalty to their defensive rolls, making it harder for them to defend against attacks, and allies within the area regain Endurance at the start of their turn.

Level 9: Ascended Being  
You become an avatar of your divine source, a beacon of light in the darkness. Spend 5 battle momentum to gain resistance to all damage types, reducing damage taken by half, and your focus abilities (from the even levels on the Divine side of the confluence) become exceptionally potent for 3 rounds. This includes abilities like Blessed Ward, Sacred Grace, Divine Intervention, and Sanctified Ground, which now have increased effects during this time. After 3 rounds, the effects of Ascended Being end, and you return to your normal state.

**The Wellspring of Eldritch Shadow**  
The Eldritch Shadow Wellspring is a murky pool of uncertainty, like reflections seen in a shadowed mirror. You can't tell if the voice you hear is from some far-flung entity or a fragmented part of your own psyche. In moments of combat, your limbs move with an eerie fluidity, guided by an intelligence that may or may not be your own. Or perhaps you find profane or forbidden knowledge flooding your mind—insights, strategies, awareness that you can't recall ever learning.  
Channeling this Wellspring feels like teetering on the edge of a precipice between enlightenment and madness. It's unsettling, charged with a persistent undertone of danger, like the feeling of a blade pressed lightly against the skin. Your Focus Point becomes less a source of power than a conduit, an open channel to a realm—or a consciousness—you can never fully comprehend. The thrill and the trepidation become two sides of the same coin, making this Wellspring a potent but perilous path to tread.

Eldritch Shadow Wellspring

Level 1: Shadow Step (Passive)  
You harness the power of the Eldritch Shadow to swiftly move across the battlefield. Without spending battle momentum, you teleport to any location within your line of sight, allowing for strategic positioning and quick escapes.

Level 3: Telekinetic Defense  
The shadows protect you from ranged attacks, and you can extend this protection through telekinesis. You gain an additional defense die against all ranged attacks, making it harder for enemies to hit you. This bonus does not require battle momentum and is always active.

Level 5: Evasive Shadows  
Your mastery over the Eldritch Shadow extends to your allies. Spend 3 battle momentum to shroud your team in elusive shadows. This grants all allies within your line of sight the ability to take the Hide action as a free action on their turn, regardless of their current position on the battlefield. This tactical advantage allows your team to hide and reposition effectively.

Level 7: Phantasmal Grasp  
You extend your control over the Eldritch Shadow, allowing you to ensnare and manipulate the minds of your enemies. Spend 4 battle momentum to target a single enemy within your line of sight. Make a contested roll against the target's defense die. If successful, you can force the target to move a number of squares equal to your character level in a direction of your choice, potentially repositioning them or disrupting their plans.

Level 9: Nightmare Veil  
At the peak of your mastery over the Eldritch Shadow, you unleash a nightmarish creature from your own nightmares that engulfs your enemies. Spend 5 battle momentum to create a terrifying illusion manifested from your deepest fears. This illusion affects all enemies within your line of sight. Each affected enemy must make a contested roll against your Eldritch Shadow ability. If they fail, they are affected by the Nightmare Veil for two rounds. While affected, they are plagued by visions of their worst fears, causing them to have impaired vision and making it difficult for them to target and perceive their surroundings effectively.

Your power comes from a place of inner turmoil, a chaotic essence that you've learned to harness. These abilities reflect your journey to master that internal chaos and bend it to your will.

**Valor:** Your Practice, Art, or Training  
The Valor Wellspring is an internal reservoir of raw human spirit—of courage, resilience, and practiced skill. It's as straightforward and resonant as the clang of steel against steel. Your fists, when clenched, become extensions of your sheer will, landing blows that are felt far beyond the physical point of impact. Or perhaps your voice takes on an undeniable gravity, articulating unspoken truths that resonate with those who hear you, making them heed your words as if a primal chord had been struck in their souls.  
When you tap into this Wellspring, you draw from an untainted pool of pure resolve. You feel it in your bones, in the steady beat of your heart; you're not just a force to be reckoned with—you are force itself. You don't perform feats of courage; you become the embodiment of it, a towering pillar in your community, a beacon to those who fight beside you. It's not your actions that define you; it's your unyielding core.

Level 1: Battle Rush  
Your valor fuels your need to engage in combat. Activate Battle Rush to move from your current position to the Frontline immediately, without spending battle momentum. This swift maneuver showcases your eagerness to enter the heart of the battle.

Level 3: Counterstrike  
Your combat prowess enables you to counterattack without expending battle momentum when you lose an endurance point. This ability reflects your ability to retaliate against your foes when they manage to land a blow on you.

Level 5: Mighty Grasp  
Your strength allows you to manipulate the battlefield. Spend 3 battle momentum to use Mighty Grasp, allowing you to either drag a grappled enemy to a different range (Frontline, Midfield, or Backline) or shove an ally into a different range. This versatility lets you control the positioning of both your enemies and allies.

Level 7: Unyielding Presence  
Your unyielding presence on the battlefield grants you an advantage in contested situations. You win ties when rolling contested attack dice, showing that your resolve and determination make you a formidable opponent.

Level 9: Second Wind  
Your indomitable spirit enables you to rise again even when knocked out. Spend a minimum of 4 battle momentum to activate Second Wind. For every 2 additional battle momentum points you spend beyond the initial 4, you regain 1 point of endurance. This determination and refusal to stay down in the face of adversity demonstrate your heroic spirit.

Anyone can touch the powers that are abundant in the world. Not all choose to. Some choose greatness. For others, circumstances conspire to bring greatness to them. With or without awareness of what they are doing, the powers that manifest in the people of the world are transformative.

SOCIAL GAME PLAY:

\#\#\# Adventure Momentum System

The Adventure Momentum system:  
Each character’s social approach is defined by their disposition – Engage, Disengage, or Remain – which influences their available actions in social situations.

\#\#\#\# Dispositions and Actions

1\. \*\*Engage Disposition:\*\*  
   \- \*Persuade:\* Attempt to convince others through argument or appeal.  
   \- \*Inspire:\* Motivate or uplift others to take action or improve morale.  
   \- \*Intimidate:\* Use assertiveness or threats to influence outcomes.

2\. \*\*Disengage Disposition:\*\*  
   \- \*Negotiate:\* Seek a compromise or mutual agreement in conflicts.  
   \- \*Deceive:\* Employ subterfuge or lies to manipulate situations.  
   \- \*Withdraw:\* Disengage from a situation to reassess or avoid conflict.

3\. \*\*Remain Disposition:\*\*  
   \- \*Observe:\* Analyze the dynamics and undercurrents of a social situation.  
   \- \*Mediate:\* Act as an impartial party to resolve disputes.  
   \- \*Plan:\* Strategize and prepare for future social interactions or maneuvers.

\#\#\#\# Adventure Momentum

Like Battle Momentum, Adventure Momentum is a key resource in social scenarios, representing a character's capacity to influence, adapt, and succeed in non-combat situations.

\- \*\*Gaining Momentum:\*\* Characters accrue Adventure Momentum through successful social interactions, clever role-playing, and achieving narrative goals.  
\- \*\*Using Momentum:\*\* Players can spend Adventure Momentum in various ways:  
    \- \*\*Enhance Actions:\*\* Spend Momentum to gain bonuses on social rolls, increasing the likelihood of success.  
    \- \*\*Additional Attempts:\*\* Use Momentum for extra attempts in a social encounter, like trying to persuade different individuals or re-negotiating.  
    \- \*\*Counter Opponents:\*\* Spend Momentum to counteract another character’s social maneuver, like countering a deception with an insight check.

Roll Types

The system employs two types of rolls for social interactions:

1\. \*\*Uncontested Rolls:\*\*  
   \- Used when a character’s action is not directly opposed by another character.  
   \- The difficulty is set by the Game Runner based on the situation's complexity.  
   \- Spending Momentum for a bonus is particularly effective in these rolls.

2\. \*\*Contested Rolls:\*\*  
   \- Occur when characters have opposing goals in a social scenario.  
   \- Both parties roll, with the number of dice based on their actions and any spent Momentum.  
   \- Characters can spend Momentum to add dice to their pool or to counteract their opponent’s actions.

\#\#\# Strategic Implications

\- \*\*Disposition Choice:\*\* Choosing the right disposition and corresponding actions can drastically affect the outcome of social encounters.  
\- \*\*Momentum Management:\*\* Deciding when to spend Adventure Momentum is crucial. Players must balance immediate gains against potential future needs.  
\- \*\*Roll Strategy:\*\* Players need to consider the context and their opponents' possible actions when deciding how to use their rolls and Momentum.

This system enhances the role-playing aspect of the game, providing a detailed and strategic framework for social interactions that parallels the combat system, encouraging thoughtful and immersive engagement with the game's narrative.

Saga.  
What Are Sagas?  
A Saga is a story-driven framework that guides your character's social and emotional development. Unlike traditional RPG elements that focus on combat skills and physical attributes, Sagas delve into the deeper aspects of character building – their beliefs, aspirations, fears, and the masks they wear in social interactions.

How Do Sagas Work?  
Creating Your Saga: As you create your character, you also begin to weave their Saga. This includes defining key moments, challenges, and changes that your character will encounter, shaping their social interactions and personal growth.

Chapters of Growth: Each Saga is divided into chapters, with each chapter representing a significant phase or turning point in the character’s life. These chapters are not just narrative milestones but also gateways to new abilities, traits, and deeper understanding of the character's role in the world. Completing a chapter adds one point of Adventure Momentum to you pool of Adventure Momentum points.

Social Dispositions and Masks: Your character’s Saga includes a social disposition (Engage, Disengage, Remain) that influences how they interact in social settings. Alongside this, characters adopt 'Masks' – social personas that provide certain advantages and disadvantages in interactions. These Masks reflect how the character chooses to be seen by the world.

Heart-Coded Actions: These are pivotal actions or decisions that resonate deeply with your character's personal story and beliefs. Performing a Heart-Coded Action propels the Saga forward, bringing your character closer to their true self and often leading to the shedding of a Mask.

Shedding Masks and Completing Sagas: As your character grows and evolves, they shed their Masks, symbolizing personal development and the revelation of their true identity. Shedding a mask also adds a point of Adventure Momentum to your pool of Adventure Momentum points.

Completing a Saga chapter marks a significant transformation in the character, often granting new insights, or relationships. While a mask might have a desired trait, shedding it gains you additional adventure momentum.

Adventure Momentum: This is the currency of social interactions and Saga progression. Earned through role-playing, successful encounters, and narrative achievements, Adventure Momentum is used to activate social abilities, influence encounters, and perform Heart-Coded Actions.

Implementing Sagas in Gameplay:  
Game Runner's Role: Game Runners integrate characters' Sagas into the overarching narrative, creating opportunities for Heart-Coded Actions and challenges that test and develop the character.  
Player Engagement: Players actively engage with their Sagas, making choices that reflect their character's growth and responding to the narrative cues provided by the Game Runner.  
Dynamic Storytelling: Sagas ensure that character development is an integral part of the gameplay, providing a rich, evolving story where characters are not just adventurers but also evolving personalities with their own tales to tell.  
Sagas transform characters from mere adventurers into protagonists of their own epic tales, intertwining their personal journeys with the grand narrative of the game. Through Sagas, the game becomes not just a series of quests and battles but a canvas for storytelling, character growth, and emotional depth.

Saga homebrew guide:  
Writing a Saga  
Define Your Character’s Core: Start by understanding the essence of your character. What are their deepest desires, fears, and motivations? What is their background, and how does it shape their worldview? This core will be the foundation of your Saga.

Outline Key Milestones: Identify the significant events or turning points that will mark your character's growth. These can be external conflicts, internal struggles, revelations, or decisions that change their path.

Structure Your Saga into Chapters: Divide your Saga into a series of chapters, each representing a distinct phase in your character's journey. Each chapter should have its own mini-arc that contributes to the overall narrative.

Incorporate Social Dispositions and Masks: Decide how your character interacts with others. What social disposition (Engage, Disengage, Remain) do they have, and what Masks (social personas) do they wear in different situations? How do these elements evolve throughout the Saga?

Craft a Climax and Resolution: Your Saga should build towards a climactic event or decision that embodies the essence of your character's journey. Following this climax, provide a resolution that shows how your character has changed or what they have learned.

Ensure Flexibility and Open-Endedness: While it's important to have a structure, your Saga should also be flexible enough to accommodate the unpredictability of gameplay and collaborative storytelling.

Choosing a Heart-Coded Action  
If your character's Saga doesn't align with provided examples, creating a Heart-Coded Action involves a few thoughtful steps:

Align with Character’s Core: Your Heart-Coded Action should be deeply tied to your character’s core values, beliefs, or backstory. It should feel like a natural and significant expression of who they are.

Reflect a Key Milestone: Choose an action that represents a pivotal moment in your character’s journey. It could be an act of bravery, sacrifice, revelation, or transformation that propels the story forward.

Ensure Consequence and Impact: The action should have tangible consequences and impact both on your character and the broader narrative. It should be a moment that changes things, either internally for the character or externally in the game world.

Incorporate Emotional Depth: A good Heart-Coded Action is emotionally resonant. It should feel meaningful and evoke a strong response from your character and potentially from other characters or the game world.

Consider Long-Term Effects: Think about how this action will affect your character’s future decisions and interactions. It should have a lasting impact, influencing the course of the Saga.

\*Level 2 (Engage): Passionflare\* – The first time you deal spell damage each round, add \+1 to the damage if you are below half your Endurance.

\*Level 4 (Remain): Smoldering Words\* – When your Talk action succeeds in combat, the target must choose: suffer 1 Endurance damage or lose 1 momentum.

\*Level 6 (Disengage): Emberstep\* – When you move away from an enemy, leave behind a trail of arcane fire. If they pursue and enter your square, they take your current die size in damage.

\*Level 8 (Remain): Cindershield\* – Spend 2 battle momentum to wreath an ally in burning sparks. The first enemy to attack them takes 1 Endurance damage and suffers disorientation (roll one die size lower).

\*Level 10 (Engage): Bonfire Soul\* – Once per combat, unleash an aura of burning emotion. Allies within 2 squares gain \+1 Speed, and enemies lose 1 Endurance if they are within the aura at the start of their turn.

\*\*Arcane Philosophy – The Philosophers\*\*    
\*Level 2 (Disengage): Axial Insight\* – You may substitute your die roll with the average result of your current die size (rounded up). Can only be used once per round.

\*Level 4 (Engage): Spiral Recursion\* – If you repeat the same action as your previous turn, it gains \+1 to hit and \+1 damage.

\*Level 6 (Remain): Pattern Seeker\* – After analyzing a target, choose a keyword (e.g., "fire," "movement," "illusion"). Until the end of combat, you gain \+1 to defend against any effect with that keyword.

\*Level 8 (Disengage): Reversal Thesis\* – When you fail a contested roll, spend 2 momentum to force a reroll. You must take the new result.

\*Level 10 (Remain): Convergence of Paradox\* – Once per combat, when both sides roll the same value on a contested roll, you win the roll and deal 2 Endurance damage to the opponent, regardless of modifiers.

\*\*Eldritch Guts – Oracles of Discord\*\*    
\*Level 2 (Remain): Whisper Reflex\* – Once per round, when targeted by an attack, roll a d4. On a 3 or 4, you may switch places with a nearby ally or summon a shadow double that takes the hit instead (vanishes after being struck).

\*Level 4 (Disengage): Panic Sense\* – If you start your turn at 1 Endurance or below half, your Speed increases by 2 and you gain \+1 Defense until end of turn.

\*Level 6 (Engage): Aberrant Impulse\* – Spend 2 battle momentum to reroll your next attack. If the new result is lower than the first, it instead triggers a minor reality glitch: an enemy within 2 squares takes 1 psychic damage.

\*Level 8 (Remain): Suspended Mind\* – Once per rest, delay a decision made this round (such as a movement, spell target, or reaction) until the start of your next turn. Choose it retroactively.

\*Level 10 (Disengage): Precipice Edge\* – If you're at 0 Endurance, you may still spend 1 momentum to act as if you weren’t. If you deal damage that round, regain 1 Endurance.

\*\*Eldritch Stride – Stalkers of the Void\*\*    
\*Level 2 (Engage): Skipstep\* – After making a successful attack, you may teleport one square in any direction. This movement does not provoke reactions.

\*Level 4 (Remain): Trail of Dread\* – While moving, you may leave behind a flickering illusion of yourself. The first enemy to enter that square rolls one die size lower for their next action.

\*Level 6 (Disengage): Rattlepulse\* – Spend 2 battle momentum to emit a sudden burst of haunting motion. All enemies within 1 square must reroll their initiative next round.

\*Level 8 (Engage): Out-of-Sync\* – Once per encounter, take two movement actions back-to-back before or after your main action. For the rest of the round, your location flickers, and attacks against you have disadvantage.

\*Level 10 (Remain): Step Into Echo\* – You can move through enemy spaces without penalty. If you pass through more than one, you may make a free contested roll against one to knock them back.

\*\*Eldritch Atlas – Devotees of the Grin\*\*    
\*Level 2 (Disengage): Shoulders of Madness\* – Gain 1 Endurance whenever an ally within 2 squares is hit and you do not intervene. You may smirk knowingly.

\*Level 4 (Remain): Grin of Patience\* – When you defend and roll max, you may choose to absorb the full effect instead of reducing it. If you do, your next action against that attacker has \+2 to the roll.

\*Level 6 (Engage): Void Shoulders\* – Spend 3 momentum to take the damage meant for an adjacent ally, and immediately return the same amount as psychic damage to the attacker.

\*Level 8 (Disengage): Broken Backbone Boon\* – If knocked back, prone, or repositioned against your will, you may instead use the movement to switch places with an ally or disappear (teleport within 3 squares).

\*Level 10 (Remain): Fanged Bastion\* – As long as you haven’t moved this round, enemies who begin their turn adjacent to you must roll a defense die or lose 1 Endurance from oppressive aura.

\*\*Eldritch Maul – Psychofist\*\*    
\*Level 2 (Engage): Thirsting Knuckles\* – The first time you reduce a foe's Endurance each round, regain 1 Battle Momentum.

\*Level 4 (Remain): Echoed Strike\* – If you miss with a melee attack, the sound of your blow echoes. At the start of your next turn, the target takes 1 psychic damage if still in range.

\*Level 6 (Disengage): Hurl Into Mind's Eye\* – Spend 2 momentum to lash out with mental force. Target makes a defense roll; on failure, they lose 1 Endurance and you may teleport them 1 square.

\*Level 8 (Engage): Many-Armed Grasp\* – Once per round when grappling, make a second contested roll against another nearby enemy. On success, they are pulled adjacent and dazed.

\*Level 10 (Remain): Hand of the Undreamt\* – When you hit with a melee attack, roll your damage die twice. One result is damage, the other determines a random effect from a short table (GM-created or player-built).

\*\*Eldritch Icarus – The Kites\*\*    
\*Level 2 (Engage): Plunge into Absurdity\* – If you begin your turn airborne, your first attack gains a bonus die and the target must reroll their defense.

\*Level 4 (Disengage): Wings of Disbelief\* – Once per encounter, negate any fall damage and instead hover just above the ground, untargetable by melee until struck.

\*Level 6 (Remain): Drifting Menace\* – Enemies you fly over this turn must make a contested roll or lose their next reaction.

\*Level 8 (Engage): Dagger Dive\* – Spend 3 momentum to fly in a straight line and attack all enemies in the path. You must end your turn on the ground.

\*Level 10 (Remain): Kite’s Judgement\* – For 3 rounds, your elevation increases your spell or ranged attack damage by 1\. Additionally, enemies below you roll one die size lower on contested rolls.

\*\*Eldritch Virtuoso – Order of the Brown Recluse\*\*    
\*Level 2 (Remain): Web of Gesture\* – You may use gestures or minor tricks to communicate telepathically with one ally per round, even without line of sight.

\*Level 4 (Engage): Fingerlace Curse\* – Spend 2 momentum to bind a foe with invisible threads. They cannot move away from you without spending an action.

\*Level 6 (Disengage): Twitch-Snare\* – When struck by a ranged attack, may use 1 momentum to reflect the pain back at the attacker, dealing 1 psychic damage.

\*Level 8 (Remain): Cacophonic Stringwork\* – Use a musical instrument or gesture to emit chaotic tones. All enemies within 2 squares must make a defense roll or suffer \-1 Speed until end of turn.

\*Level 10 (Engage): Pattern of the Recluse\* – Spend 4 momentum to designate a "locus" square. For 3 rounds, you may teleport to that square as a free action once per turn and roll an extra die on all spells cast from it.
