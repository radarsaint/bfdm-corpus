**Class Goals**  
I’m gonna try to stay loyal to the concept of the Harbinger (at least as I understand it) while I make suggestions ‘cause it’s a cool idea. So far as I know, we’re looking for:

- A class with competitive armor & weapon proficiencies  
- Spellcasting that focuses on utility & single-target damage; limited access to AoE & non-self buff/debuff  
- Connection between magic & vital forces for thematics – more spiritual than sorcerer

**Design Inspirations**  
If you wanna know where I pulled concepts & ideas for my suggestions, check out the Magus & Oracle classes from Pathfinder 2E. Remember: good artists borrow, great artists steal\!

**Class Chassis**  
	When I say ‘chassis,’ I mean your starting proficiencies & spell slot progression. I think most of this should stay the same for the idea we’re goin’ for, but I’d probably **switch to a half-casting progression with the addition of cantrips, like Artificer.** A Harbinger has a strong martial backbone to rely on alongside their magic – we don’t want them to be able to utilize spells the same way full-casters do, even at very high levels.

**Spellcasting**  
	So, Harbinger should have freer access to the spells they need at any moment. Having access to *every* spell on the list is a lot, though, and balancing that would make it hard to make a good list. I want Harbinger’s spontaneous and unknown magic to be on full display during each pillar of play, though, so here’s the middle ground I imagined:

	In essence, a Harbinger chooses which spells they know at the moment they need to know them. It gives them unique free-flowing access to their magic, but stops them from outshining the utility of other classes. It also gives us design room to play around with the way they interact with this spell limit when writing subclasses & higher-level features.  
 I would recommend a Harbinger be able to discover 2 spells at once at 1st level. Increase the number by 2 each time they unlock a new level of spells. I’ve also given the Harbinger a choice between Intelligence or Wisdom for their spellcasting ability – the budget for Constitution spellcasting would’ve come out of the armor & hit dice if anything, but with a d10, medium armor, and shields, our Harbinger is plenty capable with a 12 or 14 in Constitution. Finally, I tried to think of a spellcasting focus that would fit the Harbinger, but I decided it’d be cooler if they didn’t need one.

	I have lots of ideas buzzing around in my head for this feature, but I think I’m happy with this for now. It does a couple things I think are important:

- Harbingers need to prioritize something alongside their spellcasting ability – I think martial weapons, shields, medium armor, and 1d10 hit points are enough power budget that they should be central to playing the class  
- It leaves room for later features that expand the ways this feature can be used – good subclasses should change the way you approach the game drastically, and changing the qualifiers for Fated Strike does so


  
**Harbinger’s Wrist Pocket**  
	Cool\! I’d advise moving it to 3rd level, since Harbinger doesn’t get anything at that level right now. The questions I have reading it are the action cost of putting something in **(an action probably)**, how much can fit inside at once **(I’d recommend one item for simplicity),** and what happens to it if the Harbinger dies **(similar features have the item reappear in their space).**   
	To bring the final line toward 5e design, I’d **cut it out, and specify elsewhere that the effect is magical.** Outside of *antimagic field,* this system has no rules for counteracting magic that isn’t spells, so we’re all set with this one detail.

**Harbinger’s Vitality**  
	This feature breaks one of 5e’s golden rules – healing is never free\! The system is heavily designed around attrition, so it always requires some sort of resource in addition to its action cost. You could solve this by **limiting the number of uses per day** – I’d advise against making it cost spell slots, since that brings us to the “smite-only” Paladin design space and subtracts from the rest of the class. Speaking of the Paladin design space, I’d **scale it off of a solid figure like level** instead of damage dealt. We don’t want players holding onto their uses until they score a critical hit because they feel like it’ll be a waste.  
	If you don’t want to limit the uses per day, the feature might instead **grant a lesser sum of temporary hit points.** Even if you choose this route, though, I’d **avoid scaling it off of damage.**  
	On a final note, you should **move this feature to 7th-level\!** Harbingers get a subclass feature at 6th and an Ability Score Increase at 8th, so we can use Harbinger’s Vitality to fill in that empty level.

**Spell List**  
	Harbinger’s spell list should include a healthy collection of low-level utility classics that are usually hard to access simultaneously, like *comprehend languages*, *detect magic,* and *speak with animals.* Their combat magic can include plenty of solid options without including AoE – spells like *absorb elements, shield,* and spells like *chromatic orb* or *guiding bolt* to compliment Fated Strike. Also give them a few self-buffs like *longstrider,* but don’t let them cast these spells on other characters or shine with battlefield control, ruling out options like *cure wounds*, *faerie fire,* and *hideous laughter.*
