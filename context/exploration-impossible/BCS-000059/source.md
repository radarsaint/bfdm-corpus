Prologue

Nobody told me how this war started. I was never given the time to ask either. All I know is it's a war that will forever change civilized races. A war to end all wars.

After years of fighting, this War which spans the Universe is about to be won, by one side... or by the other. As I’m sure you can tell by the tone of my voice, I have a clear preference for one outcome. As time passes, eventually the debris of this conflict will be burned to ash by the stars they eventually drift into. The countries and kingdoms of the myriad worlds which span across known space. Those worlds and empires that saw the brutal realities of this war we all face… will someday heal and hopefully... forget. That's the way of things, isn’t it? As everything and everyone else does in the end. We may be gone by then, but we will continue to live through the stories told by your generation and beyond. But enough my meanderings. You came here for a story. A proper story. A war story.

So listen, my child for the future will know that every one of us has a unique trajectory like those motes drifting through space, we all might feel disconnected. But we all had the same beginning, and we will all have the same end, an end hard-fought and long-awaited.

…

As silly as it sounds to talk about months in Space. I remember that It was June. June 16th, 3457, the year Real Maverik took the cup 3-1 in the second half of the series. The battle of the Zodiac Spiral has begun. We received word from our spy that Empress Zurial of the Axcelites, a humanoid race of Zodiac appearance and personas, was hidden in a space station named LorenZ, which was located somewhere in the Aries System of the Spiral’s borders.

You know me as Argo. I am still called Argo, but also I am the Blue Fox. It was my callsign, and this emblem of a blue wolf eating the sun that I have tattooed on the base of my neck. That is my Callsign. A sort of nickname pilots uses when they want to define a person’s character. I’m lucky to have a positive one. One of my bunkmates in the recruit racks was called Lunchbox his entire career. But here I am Meandering again. I shall endeavor from this point forward to tell the story as I remember it… a moment for a moment.

There I am, a member of the United Imperial Federation’s Shadow Division. On the day our story begins, I was thinking to myself about Shadow Div. When Test Pilots for the UIF finish with a newer, faster, more agile ship, Shadow Div is where they get sent. Our specialty is finding ways through the enemy ranks in the heat of battle to perform covert infiltrations of enemy facilities and perform either assassination, kidnapping, or espionage.

This time, our objective is to assist the UIF Assault Battalion 601 in attacking LorenZ's defenses. Once we see the enemy is engaged with our forces on SUDAR, my squad will infiltrate (using single-pilot fighter craft) the LorenZ. This is not an assassination, however. Our leaders are convinced that her death would make her a martyr, making our objective far more obtuse than anyone would like. Our mission is to capture Empress Maia and convince her to surrender.

Mission 1: Terra

HD 40307 d. A planet discovered all the way back in June of 2008. Back then, the colonization of other planets was nothing but a distant dream. It wasn’t until after the Second Cold War that humanity managed to advance their technology to simplify space travel. From that point forward, colonizing other planets only became a matter of time and effort. There were quite a few planets that could not be colonized as the environment was too extreme to even approach.

It was near the year 2757 that humanity managed to land on HD 40307 and renamed it Terra. Though Terra’s size and force of gravity was significantly larger than Earth, it looks so similar from a distance. The human ability to adapt to such environments will always prevail nonetheless.

Mission 2: Sentencing

Supreme Court of Texas December 23, 3448, 13:08

I stand before the jury today after pleading guilty on counts of grand theft of government property, use of spacecraft without a license, and illegal emigration of Earth’s atmosphere.

Mission 1: LineBreak

Mirrors are creepy objects. You put them in private places to examine yourself. But sometimes you find yourself there and just understand that this image is all that people have to go on when deciding whether or not you are trustworthy, capable, friendly, or a threat. When it comes down to it, most people would say that it's a tool for checking your appearance. They aren’t wrong. They aren’t wrong. They just don’t realize that you're seeing more than just your flight suit and buzz cut. Each of these cookie-cutter soldiers is a person just like you are underneath. They all got lives to lose and they all got people who will miss them.

What do I see when I look in the mirror? I see a man like me appraising a man like me. It can be a tricky headspace to navigate. Today, I see a twenty-two-year-old man with light brown hair that can't seem to see his own purpose. And there is a stain on the mirror. It happened when the pressure dropped and a fault in the aerosol deodorant made it pop.

I resumed my musings. “I've been fighting in this war since I was thirteen.” I thought to myself. “I have been checking the mirror for some sign that I’m a monster pretending to be a hero. But here I am. I’m just a person, really. Or is it I’m still a person? Just a person looking? And pretending and trying to imagine the interior lives of others. It’s hard enough when everyone isn’t dressed exactly the same. Or all you know of a person is the way their voice squawks over the radio.

"Argo, we are almost ready for mission launch," I hear a familiar voice announce.

I come back to my senses and remember that I am needed in the loading dock of the UIF Mother Armada. And so I close the curtain on my mirror, finish my morning routine, and make my way through the bulwarks and hallways.

Fifteen minutes later, I'm sitting down on a metal crate next to my fighter ship. It leaves my hands smelling like iron which I accidentally smell when I touch my face in thought for a moment.

“Argo, are you feeling okay?” He says, just a little too loud so that he can brush off his concern as insincere. I can hear his smile before I see it.

I look up to see a Caucasian man with black hair in the traditional military “jughead” buzz cut, wearing yet another black jumpsuit.

"Don't mind me, Rorke. Just lost in thought is all," I respond, almost under my breath. When he joins me on the crate, I wonder for a moment what we are sitting on. Probably a crate of fuel or ammo. There is always an explosive to sit on in the hanger.

"Lost? You were caught in a dang gravity well! Look, I don't know about you, but I'm thinking since this may be the last battle of the war that we are going to be flying into, our captain should give us a few words of wisdom," Rorke suggests with a smile.

"Last battle? You say that before every battle. It feels a little optimistic, but ten out of ten for trying to cheer me up." I say, trying to match bravado as a way to climb out of my funk.

"Nonsense!" Rorke climbs on top of the crate I'm sitting on and yells, "Hey, everyone! Who else agrees that our captain should give us a speech! Who's with me?"

Like a slow handclap that starts with just a few voices, but then rises to a din, it doesn't take long for the entire section of the loading dock to echo with the Rogue Division.

The chants echo "Speech! Speech! Speech!" and remembering it later in life will always cheer me up. It’s a good moment that Rorke punctuates with a flourish. Rorke gives me a grin as he jumps off the crate to join the crowd.

Rorke and I have been friends for a few years now. He joined the Federation around the time I was promoted to Lieutenant Colonel. Rorke was a big troublemaker when he first joined. The first day of his training, I wound up giving him confinement on diminished rations after getting caught stealing whiskey from the pantry. He also acted as some lone wolf hot shot that was always in a hurry to die. Always rushing to be the first to the objective. It’s not that there's anything wrong with getting stuff done. There is just a huge difference between being efficient and being reckless.

What he lacked in discipline, however, he made up for in his skills in improvisation. It’s because of this I requested him to be in the Rogue Division. His troublemaking has been put to good use against the Axcelites. His ability to improvise also works in perfect sync with my skills as a pilot. We’ve been through too much together to not be considered friends.

I climb onto the crate and take a deep breath with a smile. With what I’ve been thinking about for the past few minutes, it doesn’t take me long to figure out what I want to say. And it's not like I didn’t see Rorke’s mischief coming a mile away.

“Ladies and gentlemen, we stand here today in hopes of finally ending this war!” I began, “About nine years ago, I was forced to join the Federation after stealing a ship and wiping out a whole platoon of Axcelites on my own. Since then, I’ve been putting my life on the line for the sake of humanity just like you. Let me ask all of you, are we ready to finally go home?”

“YEAH!” the crowd screams.

“Are we going to hold back?”

“NO!”

“Then let’s send the Axcelites a message!”

The noise in the loading dock becomes deafening as the people of the Rogue Division cheers their hearts out. It fills my heart with joy to see my Division in high spirits. The joy that is tempered by the battle that is about to begin. Joy punctuated by the realities and fears of that same moment.

“While I still have your attention, allow me to give a final brief of what our mission is!” I continue, “Majors Charlotte, Rorke, Sam will squad up with me for this mission. For this mission, we are to be referred to as Shadows. The Shadows’ objective is to sneak through the enemy line and infiltrate LorenZ. Once inside, the Shadows will need to act fast. Empress Maia, who is the current objective, is on board. Empress Maia is to be captured by the Shadows before she catches wind of them and flees back to the Pisces System. The rest of the Rogue Division will be assisting the Front Striker Legion as a distraction. I know that this division was made for assassination, kidnapping, infiltration, and espionage, but the Front Striker Legion is going to need all the help they can get in this battle. Once my squad is in the LorenZ, we must find and capture Empress Maia. This will hopefully lead to the Axcelites considering raising the white flag. Empress Maia must be captured alive. Disobey this order, and you will be executed by my hand. Are there any questions?”

“Who’s buying after the battle this time?” Rorke asks while pretending not to notice where the voice came from.

“Rorke, thanks for volunteering!” I grin.

“Ah, come on!” Rorke gripes.

Laughs and cheers fill the room again.

“Let’s move out!” I order.

I turn my attention to my preparations and assume that everyone else is attending to their orders. I climb on top of my ship and double-tap the cockpit cover with my left foot. The cover slowly opens up away from me with a hiss of environmental gasses. I drop into the cockpit and go over my mental checklist.

“Utility belt, seat pack, Extreme Environment Mask,” I check aloud. The repetition acts as a mantra as I intentionally relax before the flight.

After confirming that all my equipment is all accounted for, I double tap the cover and seal myself in the cockpit. I yank the ignition cord and the ship hums to life. With my com set to the dock intercom, I began announcing the launch protocol.

“Rogue Division to the dock attendant, clear foot traffic!” I yell.

I look around to see if everyone is either on their ships or out of the docks. Probably the last thing I want to happen in my dock is to have someone hurled into space because we open the airlock negligently. Anything else like crates and tools is fine since those are strapped to the floor. The items that do get thrown into space can easily be replaced.

After confirming the dock is safe, I yell, “Rogue Division airlock, open!”

The big wall in front of me slowly rises to show the LorenZ floating far away in the void of the Aries System. There may not be any Axcelite fighters out there now, but as soon as we launch, the LorenZ will be pouring out ships out of its hull as a leaky watering can.

“Section five, launch!” I order.

Ships to my far right dart into the void.

“Section one, launch!”

Ships to my far left follow suit.

“Section four, launch!”

Ships closer to my right fly into the void.

“Section two, launch!”

It is at around this time, I start seeing ships from the Assault Division joining the fray. Not too long after, Axcelite ships start bleeding out the LorenZ.

“Section three, launch!”

I throw the engine into full throttle and my ship darts into the chaos. My squad follows suit behind me. The Axcelites seem to be taking the bait.

“Aura, switch coms to Rogue Three!” I order.

“Yes, Captain!” a soft male voice speaks through my com.

"Alright, Shadows, just like we planned. Keep them confused, stay spread out. Rorke, on me." I briefed.

"Ay, sir!" the Shadows replied.

We all break our formation. "Argo, I know we can get through the ranks pretty easily, but how are we going to get on the station?" Rorke asks.

"That's an excellent question, uh..." I think aloud.

"Sir?"

A laser hits my right-wing. In reflex, I pull the stick right to barrel roll to lessen its impact. I am lucky that the wing is still functional. My head would be on the chopping block if the mission were to be compromised before I even got close to the LorenZ.

"Perhaps we can worry about that when we get to it!" I answer as I somersault past the shooter with clever use of my inertial tether.

Rorke fires deftly, weaving between shots that were intended for me. He brings my attention to the attacker by turning them into a fireball of flaming wreckage.

“We need to get to the LorenZ before they catch wind of what we are up to,” I said.

“I know... but I believe there might be a problem,” Rorke warns.

“Problem?” I reply, not liking what I’m hearing.

It’s not too soon after I ask that I realize that the reading on my nav screen shows what I thought to be a large debris field to actually be a large amount of Axcelite fighters darting their way toward us.

“I guess I spoke too soon,” I muttered through anxious lips.

“Our spy might have been caught and was forced to talk,” Rorke suggests.

Within a matter of minutes, Rorke and I are surrounded by Axcelite Fighters.

"Argo, thinking what I'm thinking?" I feel Rorke grin.

I shut off the power to my ship and begin holding my breath.

*Way ahead of you, buddy.*

I see Rorke cut the power to his ship as he maneuvers to face me. I twist the ignition cord to cut the power to my ship. Since we didn’t do anything to slow our movement, our ships are still drifting toward LorenZ. Our ships still moving should be enough to force enemy ships to slow and avoid crashing into us. I open a glass case to the left of me. In the case, is a glowing red button. I hover my left hand over the button as I raise my other hand with three fingers out. I then count down with my hand.

*3, 2, 1.*

After slamming the button, the surrounding Axcelite Fighters begin to spark, brilliant bolts of orange and yellow light jumping from metal surface to metal surface. We wait three seconds, five seconds, ten seconds. The surrounding Axcelite Fighters are no longer functioning. The ships that weren’t really close to us still seem to be functioning, but they are still having trouble operating.

The EMPs that we just used are very effective when in a situation that involves a huge number difference. In order for it to be used effectively, however, the enemy needs to be within twenty yards of us. Though, since two of us used an EMP at the same time, the blast radius can sometimes double if we time it correctly. EMPs are still very dangerous tools to us in space, however. Which is why our ships are only given one charge per mission.

Still holding my breath, I yank the ignition cord. The ship then springs back to life. I can finally breathe again. "Rorke, do you copy?"

No response. I wait a few seconds. Rorke’s ship might just be taking longer to start up. EMPs can affect our own ship functionality for a few seconds as well. It can usually last up to a minute for the user.

"Rorke, do you copy?"

No response. It’s at this point that I start to worry. If it’s taking this long for his ship to start up, then it may be because he can’t start it up at all. There have been a few times where the use of an EMP just fry all the circuits of the ship to the point of beyond repair.

I give gentle nudges to the throttle in order to move towards Rorke's ship. As I get closer I can immediately see that the cockpit cover is fogged up from the inside by black smoke. It’s so thick that I can’t even see Rorke’s movements inside. This is the last thing any captain wants to see. The EMP didn’t just fry the circuits, it ignited them. I scramble to equip my Extreme Environment Mask. This mask allows me to breathe in the vacuum of space using plant DNA that traps air that I exhale and synthesizes it into oxygen.

*Hang in there, Rorke! I'm coming!*

I set my ship to idle mode and open the cockpit hatch. With an Eblade I grabbed from my utility belt and Liferope held in my other hand to keep me connected to the seat of the cockpit, I began to push myself out of my seat.

Before I could completely launch myself from the cockpit, I was met with a flash and knocked back into my seat as Rorke’s ship flashed and shrapnel flew everywhere in the void. Most of the scraps disintegrate as the nearby stars pull them in their solid flames. My eyes fail to see any sign of Rorke surviving the blast. Rorke was vaporized.

"RORKE IS GONE!" I scream to other shadows, or maybe to the universe. Or maybe I just opened my mouth. I realize in a burst of laser light that I can see the mirrored reflection of my face in the cockpit view screen. I see the face of a man who has just seen death. The moment passes through me. It changes something that won’t register until many years after his death. Startled by the look of anguish on my own face, my grief is interrupted by an uncaring firefight all about me.

## Mission 2: Blitzkrieg Infiltration

"They got Rorke?!" I hear Sam yell over the com.

"Sam, don't do anything rash!" I yell into the com as a sense of dread washes over me, I close my cockpit hatch and put my mask aside.

Ever since I was promoted to the oddly named rank “Captain to the Shadows", I have been making efforts to change the minds of the good old boy’s club in charge. I’ve been trying to change the branch mentality by rewriting some of our branch protocol. The UIF Counsel didn’t like my cease of control at first. It’s not that our old protocol was inefficient. What I didn’t like about the protocol, however, is how it made us sound like we were disposable.

As Shadows, like the other elite units, I believe we have earned the right to use our judgment as to whether or not it is prudent to carry forward with a mission when we lose critical personnel. With the new protocol, almost everyone in the Shadows began to spread out across the entire branch in daily espionage games to practice getting information from the other soldiers. Morale amongst our group was higher than ever. Discipline was still maintainable. Mission success rates went to near perfect.

My protocol still has its flaws though. The most notable flaw is that some soldiers may have a harder time dealing with squad casualties. Hence why Sam had his outburst.

"I’m tracking six more incomings. They have locks. What’s the call Cap? We're down one pilot and the enemy is closing," Charlotte began over the com, "If Rorke is correct about our plans being compromised, we may need to withdraw before we reach a point of no return.”

“Easier said than done, Sam.” I think to myself and then regret the thought.

I've heard about the moments in history when a blitz type of war tactic was used by armies with the thought of time being the key to the war. The problem with using this tactic is the numbers on the enemy side are too great. Plus, a blitz is more useful when you have the element of surprise, which we lost a while ago. The biggest flaw in this plan, however, is the fact we're in space. A blitz is only useful when you are fighting on land. Though, retreating would mean losing our only possible chance at winning this war without resorting to near genocide. There is no way Maia would leave herself vulnerable a second time.

"Aura, back up Inertial buffers.” The words leave my mouth on reflex, as I improvise a solution. “If we are going to do this, we need to fake a retreat. That's the only way to open a new window," I explained. "We need to radio the rest."

"I got it!" Sam echoed on behalf of the group.

"Fall back and make communication brief. We don't wanna be out of contact for too long," I warned.

At that moment, we regroup and begin making a beeline for the UIF Mother Armada. None of us are firing a single shot. Not too soon after, other UIF fighter ships begin to follow suit.

"*Incoming direct transmission from General* *Barnes!*" Aura announced.

"Open the transmission," I command.

"Damnit, Fox! You better have a good reason for retreating!" a female voice booms through the com angrily.

"General Barnes Ma'am, we need to blitz the enemy so I can sneak through the opening," I answer nervously.

General Susana Barnes is a woman whose bad side I hate to be on. Not just because she's the General, but because I've already experienced what it's like to be on both her good side and her bad side.

I had become a prodigy the moment I decided to strap myself into the cockpit of a ship and take it for a joyride. During my joyride, I was ambushed by an Axcelite platoon that had mistaken me for a member of the Federation. How I managed to come out as the winner of that fight, I am unable to recall.

It was Susana who took notice of my potential and urged the UIF Counsel to offer me a spot in the Front Striker Legion in exchange for letting me off of the serious prison time I was about to be given. I pointed out that I would be more useful in the Rogue Division based on the fact I was able to sneak past military personnel when I stole the ship.

The Counsel gave both of our claims some thought and decided to assign me to the Rogue Division as a Private. I repeatedly thanked Susana for vouching for me. Though it was at this moment I had learned that not all acts of kindness are from the pure of heart.

Susana made it clear that my potential in the war effort wasn’t the only reason she helped me. It turns out the report of my encounter with the Axcelites made her find me romantically attractive. She then threatened that she would find a way to throw me back in prison if I rejected or reported her. The following three years would end up haunting me to this day. It was after those three years that I finally managed to find the courage to speak up. I asked the Counsel to help catch Susana in the act.

The Counsel awarded my courage by promoting me to Lieutenant Colonel. As for Susana, she was put on permanent probation. She would have been discharged if I hadn’t requested that she kept her position as General. I just wanted to make her live the rest of her life knowing that I could have let the Counsel end her career.

I’m pretty sure it’s safe to say that I am permanently on her bad side.

"Wait a minute! What do you mean ‘I’? Don’t tell me that you plan on infiltrating LorenZ alone!"

*Shit! Me and my big mouth!* I scream in thought.

"Sorry ksh you're ksh!"

"Argo, don't you fucking d-"

“Aura, hang up!” I command.

Susana’s voice is cut off before she could finish cursing me out. I switched my com back to my team.

"Okay fellas, let's hit'em fast and hard. It's time to blitz these Axcelite scum!" I announced.

"Argo, did you just have another scuffle with the General?" Charlotte asks scandalously.

"No, why do you ask?" I sweat.

"You only use the word ‘fellas’ in a sentence after you know you're in trouble." Sam laughs coarsely.

"Just shut up and stick with the program!" I bark as I begin to accelerate back to the enemy lines. "Anyways, to regain our element of surprise, I'm going to maneuver through the middle of the fleet. Everyone else, find holes near the edges of the fleet and work your way inwards."

"Ay, Captain!" the two Shadows reply.

We scatter yet again. Only this time I hope we don't suffer another casualty in my squad. I'm also hoping Susana hasn't told my squad what my real plan is. Even if that is the case, I still need to accomplish this plan before she does tell anyone. If my squad finds out before I can get ahead, they will likely change their efforts into trying to slow me down. This could degrade the effort of the mission even further and jeopardize our victory window.

I decided to just go with a directly aggressive approach and make my way to LorenZ as fast as possible. Shooting at the fighters that are only in my way. To speed up my progress, I turn on my shield so I don't have to elude as much. The only major risk to having the shield on is my engine overheating due to excessive use of the shield. My plan, however, doesn't involve my ship returning home.

"Fox, don't do it!" Sam cries.

"I have to! This is our only chance to end this war without the need for genocide!" I argue.

"Going in solo is suicide!"

"Well, then I guess it's a good time to say that it's been nice fighting with you paidia!" I say as I cross myself with my left hand.

"Argo, don-"

“Aura, go silent!” I command.

The com shuts down before they can finish. I’m left alone in the cockpit with my thoughts on how to get on LorenZ.

"Now to find my point of entry," I think aloud as I reequip my mask.

With my shield still activated, I finally manage to reach LorenZ. Though it was not a moment too late that I just remembered that space stations have guns as well. To discourage any gunfire toward me, I fly as close to the wall as possible. I also decided to deactivate my shield so that I still have the juice to use it at least one more time. I'm desperate to find at least some possible hollow points in LorenZ.

I know that it would probably be simpler to enter the LorenZ through the loading dock, but that would also be a simple way to get shot. To infiltrate the LorenZ with the most success, I'm gonna have to make an entrance without causing too much damage to the entire structure of the LorenZ.

A maintenance hatch is my usual plan of action when it comes to infiltrating a space station. If we weren’t in the heat of battle, I could easily park my ship near the hatch and leave it intact for an easy getaway. My ship would be able to blend in with the wall, making it look like part of the station’s structure. Maintenance hatches can easily be hacked open from the outside and will usually lead to lightly guarded rooms or hallways. But the heat of battle makes the use of gentle tactics too risky.

The best way to make an entrance in my current circumstances is to blow a hole in a spot that can make my first few moments on board free of complications. If push comes to shove, I could always allow myself to be captured by the enemy. This can allow me to assess the strengths and weaknesses behind enemy lines and create new windows of opportunity for not only my own objective but other objectives that would be passed on to my subordinates upon the news of my capture. This is perhaps what makes the Rogue Division of the UIF the most deadly to our enemies as we are trained for these situations on a regular basis.

Not too much later, I manage to find spots on the station that I think to be escape pods.

"Could that be an escape pod bay?" I think aloud with a hopeful grin.

With time not being on my side, I am going to have to throw away plan A. If there is one unwritten rule the Rogue Division follows without any exceptions, it is to use the first opportunity given if stealth is not required.

I then pull away from the LorenZ with a spiral to avoid any gunfire. After giving myself about a hundred yards of distance from the LorenZ, I half-somersault and fly at full throttle towards the escape pod bay with my shield activated.

"KNOCK KNOCK MOTHER FU-"

> Before I can even finish my taunt, I crash into the escape pod bay of the LorenZ. I blackout from excitement.

## Mission 3: Survive & Enter

I slowly regain my senses inside the cockpit of my ship. Smoke is building up as expected. I struggle to undo my safety straps as the pain slowly creeps into my arms.

"Get your ass in gear, Argo. You've been through worse situations," I try to encourage myself.

I finally managed to undo my safety straps. I check my mask to make sure it's still functional. There seems to be a crack, but not enough for the vacuum of space to instantly kill me. This mask can still manage to turn fifteen seconds of survival time to a full minute of survival time at the least. I grab my pistol from my waist holster and use the handle to break the glass that covers the emergency ejector cord.

Before yanking the cord, I duck forward and cover my head so as to not bonk it on the ceiling of the escape pod bay. Or what's left of it at least.

Nothing happens. I yank the cord again. Nothing.

"Fuck! The door is jammed!" I growled as I began to heel kick the glass.

The ejector seat can fail for a few reasons. The first and most common reason would be that the ejector cord is stuck. I can easily declare that to not be the reason in my case since I was just able to pull it. The only problem is that I felt no click while pulling the cord. This leads to the second most common reason being that the cockpit cover is jammed.

When the cockpit cover is unable to open, the ejector seat will refuse to launch as to prevent the pilot from getting crushed between the seat and the unbreakable glass that seals the only route of escape from the cockpit. Luckily there is an easy way to fix that problem. It’s just a little time consuming, which is not on my side in the current situation.

I push my back against the bottom of my seat while I wrap my hands in the safety straps to use them as an anchor point. And to make sure I can get out of the vacuum environment quickly, I grab my grapple-gun from my seat pack. Using both of my feet simultaneously on the opposite side of the door hinges, I manage to force it open in a few kicks.

I try the ejector cord again.

My seat launches from the cockpit and smacks into the ceiling. I manage to duck before hitting the ceiling, avoiding a potential concussion. To my left, I see a door that seems to have air pressure leaking out as a mist is spewing profusely from it. I extend my left arm with my grapple-gun in hand towards the door.
